# CST Studio Suite (Microwave Studio) eigenmode figures of merit: Q0, G, R/Q, R_sh, T, Epk/Eacc, Bpk/Eacc, normalisation and TBPP

Scope note. The official CST help pages (Loss and Q, 3D Eigenmode Result templates) are not publicly reachable from this sandbox (3ds.com serves only marketing pages; no public help mirror was found). Everything below therefore comes from secondary public material: a CST-based USPAS exercise with published numerical answers, a CERN-hosted CST tutorial, JACoW/arXiv papers by CST users, 3DS community posts, and accelerator textbooks/lectures. Where a statement is only a search-engine snippet of a page I could not open, it is marked "(snippet only)". Where I ran my own analytic check, it is marked "(own calculation)". No CST version is stated by most sources; versions are given where known (CST 2011, 2012, 2014, 2018-era tutorial, 2021 community post).

## 1. Q factor: Q0 for metal walls, Rs, frequency used, external Q, PEC vs lossy walls

### Takeaway
In the CST eigenmode solver the default eigenproblem is lossless (PEC walls); Q0 is obtained afterwards in "Post Processing > Loss and Q" by first-order perturbation theory, Q0 = omega*W/P_c with P_c = (Rs/2) * integral |H_t|^2 dA and Rs from a user-given wall conductivity (copper by default). A newer "General (Lossy)" eigenmode solver (announced 2021) solves the lossy problem directly and gets total Q without the perturbation approximation. A pillbox benchmark reproduces the analytic Q0 to about 0.05 % (Q 12677 vs 12683), which fixes the Rs = sqrt(pi f mu0 / sigma) formula at the mode's own resonant frequency.

### Cited Findings
- CST computes the Q-factor of a mode "if the material conductivity is known" via Post Processing (top menu) > Loss and Q: select the mode, set the conductivity of the cavity material ("copper is by default"), click Calculate; the Q column gives the quality factor. — [Migliorati, CST tutorial, CERN indico 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/683638/contributions/2801720/attachments/1589041/2513891/Migliorati-tutorial-2018-CST2.pdf)
- Same tutorial, pillbox TM010 (r = 0.3 m, L = 0.05 m, sigma = 5.8e7 S/m, delta = 3.3791e-6 m): CST Q = 1.2677e4 vs analytic Q = L r / (delta (r+L)) = 1.2683e4. The analytic derivation uses P = (Rs/2) * integral |H|^2 dA with Rs = 1/(sigma*delta) and delta = 1/sqrt(pi mu sigma f_nml), i.e. evaluated at the mode's own frequency. — [Migliorati tutorial](https://indico.cern.ch/event/683638/contributions/2801720/attachments/1589041/2513891/Migliorati-tutorial-2018-CST2.pdf)
- CST seminar slide (CERN Accelerator School 2009 hosted copy, CST 2008-era): "Results -> Loss and Q-Calculation performs a loss calculation in the postprocessing based on perturbation theory", covering metallic losses from finite conductivity (skin effect) and dielectric losses; loss channels are output power through couplers, volume losses in lossy dielectrics, surface losses from finite conductivity; the perturbation method is on by default. (snippet only; page not opened) — [CAS Darmstadt 2009 CST seminar](https://cas.web.cern.ch/sites/default/files/lectures/darmstadt-2009/seminar-2.pdf)
- A CST user on the Imperial College side states that in CST MWS "the walls of the cavity are assumed to be PEC because its eigenmode solver does not support lossy metals; the unloaded Q can be extracted by entering the conductivity of the metal at the post-processing stage", and that CST reports only the ideal (real) resonant frequency. (snippet only; PDF returned 403) — [Imperial College paper](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/faculty-of-engineering/electrical-and-electronic-engineering/public/optical-and-semiconductor-devices/pubs/2012_09_TTST.pdf)
- GSI user page: the cavity is carved out of a PEC background and "in the post processing step, the PEC is considered as copper for loss calculations". (snippet only; the page now returns 404 so I could not read it) — [GSI web-docs, Sanjari](https://web-docs.gsi.de/~ssanjari/cavity-simulations-in-cst-studio-suiter.html)
- Jefferson Lab (Marhauser, CST 2014): the default conductivity (copper) of the conducting enclosure should be changed to PEC if only external Q is wanted. Two external-Q routes exist: (a) the "usual perturbation method" (Qext,pert), which "in cavities without dissipative material ... yields the unloaded Q0 given the surface conductivity of materials" and accounts for both surface losses (zero for PEC) and volume losses in absorbers placed in the ports; (b) Qext,lossy from the complex eigenfrequency of the lossy eigenmode, whose frequency is the loaded frequency. For Qext of order 1e7 or higher, Qext,lossy values are typically returned as zeros while Qext,pert still gives finite numbers. A CST 2014 bug mis-sorted modes in the lossy eigenmode runs; absorbers in the ports avoided it. — [Marhauser, SRF2015 THPB003](https://proceedings.jacow.org/SRF2015/papers/thpb003.pdf)
- 3DS community post (2021-03-11): CST now offers an eigenmode solver called "General (Lossy)" in which dispersive materials, open boundaries and ports inside the domain are supported; "Total Q calculation is now done directly by solver and no perturbation theory approximation is employed." — [3DS community, Eigenmode solver new features 2021](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/post/simulia-community/eigenmode-solver-new-features-2021_cksixj8uRYy8gV0Xd8vDYg)
- USPAS/LANL exercise in CST MWS, copper pillbox at 11.424 GHz (R0 = 10.044 mm, L = 8.724 mm): Q0 = 7548. Analytic Q = L r / (delta (r+L)) with sigma = 5.8e7 S/m is 7551 (own calculation), so CST's default copper is 5.8e7 S/m and the same Rs formula. — [Simakov, USPAS 2018, pillbox with answers](https://uspas.fnal.gov/materials/18ODU/PillboxCavity_WithAnswers.pdf)
- Textbook definitions used by the check: Rs = 1/(sigma delta) = sqrt(pi mu0 mur f / sigma); P_c = (Rs/2) integral |H|^2 dA; Q0 = omega W / P_c; W = (mu0/2) integral |H|^2 dV = (eps0/2) integral |E|^2 dV (peak-amplitude phasors). — [Podlech, arXiv 1303.6552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6552)
- HIE-ISOLDE QWR study (CST MWS vs HFSS vs Superfish, fields normalised to 1 J): Q0 agreed to 0.4 % (Superfish 11795, CST 11844, HFSS 11746) for a beam-port-free model; after adding beam ports CST gave about 14000 vs about 11700 from HFSS, attributed by the authors to a geometry/meshing problem around racetrack-shaped noses (changing to circular noses restored agreement). — [D'Elia and Jones, arXiv 0910.0409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.0409)
- DESY TESLA/TEMF note (not CST's solver itself): a closed-cavity (lossless-port) eigenmode analysis gives an upper bound on the final Q because radiation/port losses are excluded. (snippet only) — [TESLA 2000-08](https://flash.desy.de/sites2009/site_vuvfel/content/e403/e1644/e1446/e1448/infoboxContent1456/tesla2000-08.pdf)
- Surface-impedance wall models in the CST product line (good conductor, Ohmic sheet, lossy metal) exist for the frequency-domain/transient solvers; I found no source stating how (or whether) an Ohmic-sheet or lossy-metal boundary enters the eigenmode "Loss and Q" step. (snippet only) — [CST/3DS solver overview](https://www.3ds.com/products/simulia/cst-studio-suite/electromagnetic-simulation-solvers)

### Inferences
- For reproducing CST's printed Q0 with an NGSolve code: compute the PEC eigenmode, then P_c = (Rs/2) * surface integral of |H_tangential|^2 with Rs = sqrt(pi f mu0 / sigma), f = the PEC eigenfrequency, sigma = 5.8e7 S/m (CST default copper), Q0 = 2 pi f W / P_c. This is first-order perturbation; it ignores the small frequency shift and field change from wall loss (relative error of order 1/Q), so it should match CST's perturbative Q0 to mesh accuracy. A direct lossy-eigenmode CST run (General (Lossy)) would differ from it only at O(1/Q^2) for good conductors.
- Because the PEC is "considered as copper" by default, a CST Q0 printed without the user changing the Loss and Q conductivity corresponds to sigma = 5.8e7 S/m, not to a superconducting Rs. For SRF numbers the conductivity (or equivalent Rs) must be set by hand; G = Rs*Q0 is independent of that choice.
- When a CST model contains port or absorber material, the perturbative Q includes volume loss in that material; for a Q0 comparison use a model without ports or set the Q to the wall-only term.

### Gaps
- Official CST help text for "Loss and Q" (exact formula shown, treatment of mur, whether Rs uses the eigenfrequency or a user frequency, handling of Ohmic-sheet/surface-impedance boundaries and of anomalous skin effect) was not accessible.
- No source found on whether the perturbation Q uses the tangential H on the faceted (tetrahedral) surface versus the curved-surface (PBA/curvilinear) representation.
- The GSI page and the CAS CST seminar could not be opened in full; their statements here rest on search snippets.

## 2. Geometry factor G, R/Q, shunt impedance, accelerator vs circuit convention, voltage integration path, transit time, beta

### Takeaway
CST's "3D Eigenmode Result" shunt impedance/R-over-Q template follows the Superfish/linac convention (R = V^2/P, R/Q = V^2/(omega W), no factor 2), uses a user-defined straight integration path, and by default includes the transit-time phase factor exp(j omega z / (beta c)) with a user-set beta (some users report beta = 1 is assumed by default). This is verified indirectly: CST's printed R/Q for a copper pillbox (221 Ω) equals the analytic linac-convention value including the transit-time factor (220.3 Ω), and is twice the circuit-convention value (110.1 Ω). G is not found as a dedicated CST eigenmode output; it follows from Q0 and Rs.

### Cited Findings
- CST "follows the same definition for the shunt impedance and Rs/Q as Superfish"; the effect of the transit-time factor can be turned off (the "consider particle velocity" option) to give a "frozen" shunt impedance closer to bench measurements; Rs/Q is evaluated in the post-processor under 2D and 3D Field Results > 3D Eigenmode Result, with integration direction and length set in the dialog. (snippet only; page now 404) — [GSI web-docs, Sanjari](https://web-docs.gsi.de/~ssanjari/cavity-simulations-in-cst-studio-suiter.html); related GSI page on transit-time-factor sweeps (snippet only): [GSI web-docs, TTF](https://web-docs.gsi.de/~ssanjari/simulation-of-cavity-transit-time-factor-in-cst-studio-suiter.html)
- CERN tutorial: pillbox TM010 shunt impedance "given by (Linac convention)" R = V^2/P; computed in CST via Post Processing > Template Based Post Processing > 2D and 3D Field Results > 3D Eigenmode Result, then Evaluate, value read from the "Value" column. CST result 7.745815e5 Ω vs analytic 7.719408e5 Ω (0.34 % difference). — [Migliorati tutorial](https://indico.cern.ch/event/683638/contributions/2801720/attachments/1589041/2513891/Migliorati-tutorial-2018-CST2.pdf)
- Fermilab (G. Romanov, 2015): "CST integrates the electric field along the given voltage integration path (cavity axes typically). By definition Veff takes into account time flight factor, but currently beta = 1 is assumed"; shunt impedance Rsh = Veff^2 / P. — [Romanov, FNAL DocDB 4879](https://beamdocs.fnal.gov/AD/DocDB/0048/004879/001/01July2015_2ndHarm.pdf)
- CST seminar slide (snippet only): a Transit Time Factor can be specified which defines the speed of the particle (beta = v/c) "and guarantees a phase-correct integration of the electric field"; the same snippet gives the shunt impedance as "Rs = Vo^2/(2W)" with Vo the voltage seen by the particle. That expression has the dimensions of R/Q times frequency and is probably R/Q = Vo^2/(2 omega W) mis-transcribed; treat it as unreliable and not as evidence of a circuit convention. — [CAS Darmstadt 2009 CST seminar](https://cas.web.cern.ch/sites/default/files/lectures/darmstadt-2009/seminar-2.pdf)
- USPAS/LANL CST MWS exercise (11.424 GHz copper pillbox, L = 8.724 mm, R0 = 10.044 mm): Q0 = 7548, "shunt impedance" Rs = 1.67e6 Ω, voltage V = 3.98e6 V, Ea = 456 MV/m, so R/Q = 1.67e6/7548 = 221 Ω and Ea = V/L. — [Simakov, USPAS 2018](https://uspas.fnal.gov/materials/18ODU/PillboxCavity_WithAnswers.pdf)
- Own check of that exercise (own calculation, E_z = E0 J0(2.405 rho/R) in a closed pillbox, T = sin(x)/x, x = pi L/lambda = 1.047): T = 0.8279; linac R/Q = V^2/(omega W) = 220.3 Ω; circuit R/Q = V^2/(2 omega W) = 110.1 Ω. CST's 221 Ω therefore matches the linac convention with the transit-time factor included. At W = 1 J the analytic voltage is 3.976 MV (with T) vs 4.803 MV (without T); CST printed 3.98 MV, so the V CST prints is the transit-time-weighted voltage at W = 1 J.
- Second CST-based USPAS data set (iris cavities, pi-mode, a = 0.05 lambda: Q0 = 8550, Rs = 1.71e6 Ω, V = 3.788e6 V, L = 13.12 mm, Ea = 288 MV/m = V/L). — [Simakov, USPAS 2018, pillbox with iris](https://uspas.fnal.gov/materials/18ODU/PillboxCavityWithIris_WithAnswers.pdf)
- Convention definitions: linac: R = V0^2/Pd, R/Q = V0^2/(omega W); circuit: Rcs = V0^2/(2 Pd), "all shunt impedances are exactly half as large"; "before you discuss shunt impedances with anyone, make sure that you are using the same definition." — [Jensen, RF Basics I and II, arXiv 1302.5264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5264)
- Jensen (CAS) writes R/Q = Vacc^2/(2 omega0 W) and R = Vacc^2/(2P) as the "circuit-ohms" often used for synchrotrons, and notes the linac definition omits that 2 ("Linac-ohms"); R/Q depends on geometry only, R = (R/Q) Q. — [Jensen, Cavity basics, arXiv 1201.3202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.3202)
- Vretenar: linac definition = efficiency ratio; add a factor 2 in the denominator for the circuit/"RF" definition. — [Vretenar, Low-beta structures, arXiv 1201.2593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.2593)
- Podlech: R_a = U_a^2/P_c with U_a including the transit-time factor; R/Q = 2[integral Ez cos(omega z/(beta c)) dz]^2 / (eps0 omega integral |E|^2 dV) (linac form; the factor 2 comes from W = eps0/2 integral |E|^2 dV and is not the circuit 2); G = Rs Q0 = omega mu0 integral |H|^2 dV / integral_A |H|^2 dA. — [Podlech, arXiv 1303.6552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6552)
- Effective voltage and transit time factor (Panofsky): V0 = integral E(0,z) dz, T = integral E(0,z) cos(omega t(z)) dz / integral E(0,z) dz, effective shunt impedance R = (V0 T)^2/Pd. — [Jensen, RF Basics I and II, arXiv 1302.5264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5264)
- Users of CST for longitudinal R/Q on non-axisymmetric or on-axis problems: JACoW paper on CST VBA macros states that CST "has rich functionality" for "the longitudinal R/Q, the quality factor", while transverse R/Q requires extra operations (Panofsky-Wenzel integral over W and k_z, or direct integration of E and H). — [IPAC2016 WEPMB048](https://jacow.org/ipac2016/papers/WEPMB048.pdf)
- A paper on beam coupling impedance from eigenmode results explicitly uses the "circuit definition" R/Q = |V|^2/(2 omega U) (Eq. 4) and reports that it used HFSS or CST eigenmode solutions as input; this shows that papers using CST output may convert to the circuit definition themselves, not that CST prints it. — [arXiv 1904.04680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.04680)
- Zhang et al. (CST MWS 2011, 3.9 GHz TESLA-type cavity) quote R/Q in Ω per cavity for monopoles (accelerating mode 373.1 Ω), Ω/cm^2 for dipoles etc., "defined in [4]" (definition not reproduced in the text I read). — [Zhang, Baboi, Jones, arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)
- A January 2025 3DS community question refers to the Template Based Post-Processing step "3D Eigenmode Result: R over Q", defined along user lines (e.g. 40 off-axis lines) in the Eigenmode solver. — [3DS community, Controlling Post Processing using Python](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/question/simulia-community/cst-controlling-post-processing-using-python_cGvrhdduT82tQcdMkonZ3A)

### Inferences
- Conversion table (all with "linac" values as printed by CST, from the verified pillbox case; `Pc` = wall loss at the given Rs):
  - R_CST = |V|^2 / Pc, with V = | integral Ez(z) exp(j omega z/(beta c)) dz | along the chosen line, using peak-amplitude fields (the scale cancels in R/Q). R/Q_CST = |V|^2/(omega W), W = (eps0/2) integral |E|^2 dV = total stored energy (not per-cell/half).
  - Circuit values = CST values / 2. Per-unit-length values (Z, ZT^2) need division by the length used by the template.
  - G = Rs Q0 = omega mu0 integral |H|^2 dV / integral_A |H|^2 dA, from the same fields used for Q0; no factor of 2 ambiguity. In Ω; for copper at 5.8e7 S/m the CST Rs*Q0 comes from the sigma set in Loss and Q.
- Whether CST's V includes the sign/phase of the mode: the phase-correct integral is a complex number; a magnitude is reported. The direction and the absolute phase of the eigenvector are arbitrary, so the reported V (and R/Q) should be |V| only.
- For beta = 1 elliptical cavities the "frozen" and transit-time R/Q differ by T^2 (about 0.5 to 0.8 for typical cells); a code that uses |integral Ez dz| (no phase factor) corresponds to CST's "particle velocity off" option, and one with cos/sin phase factor at beta = 1 to the default.
- For a closed (no beam hole) pillbox, the axis integral ends on the PEC end caps, where Ez is nonzero. CST's path length is whatever the user enters; use the same z-limits as the NGSolve line integral.

### Gaps
- No public source gives the exact CST dialog options (integration start/end point inputs, "particle velocity" wording, whether the voltage is reported as peak or rms, handling of mu_r, sign conventions) or confirms the default beta; Fermilab says beta = 1 is "currently assumed" for the version they used (2015, version not stated).
- No direct CST-help statement of "linac vs circuit"; the linac convention is inferred from numerical agreement in the USPAS and CERN pillbox examples and from the GSI/Superfish snippet.
- G (geometry factor) is not documented as a CST eigenmode output; I found no source computing it with CST.

## 3. Stored-energy normalisation, field normalisation, peak surface fields (Epk, Bpk) evaluation, Eacc definition

### Takeaway
CST scales every eigenmode so the stored energy is 1 J; Q, R/Q, G are unaffected, while voltages and fields must be rescaled by sqrt(W_target/1 J). Eacc is reported as V/L with L the integration/active length (V transit-time weighted). Peak surface fields are read as the maximum of the field on the surface from the discretised solution (a maximum over mesh values), which makes them mesh-sensitive and can overshoot an analytic value near corners.

### Cited Findings
- Fermilab Main Injector cavity (CST Eigenmode): the gap voltage of 278 kV "is the voltage associated with 1 Joule stored energy within the structure, which is the standard the program follows. Output values like Q, R/Q, and tuning range are unaffected by this, but field values require a scaling factor." — [Stevenson, arXiv 2508.03312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03312)
- HIE-ISOLDE QWR: "The fields are normalized to give 1 J stored energy in the cavity (CST normalization)"; peak fields from CST (max evaluation) compared with HFSS and Superfish: peak H Superfish 16.71 kA/m vs HFSS 16.9 kA/m, CST converging with mesh refinement steps; peak E: HFSS 11.6 MV/m shown as reference line against the CST refinement curve. — [D'Elia and Jones, arXiv 0910.0409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.0409)
- USPAS/LANL CST exercise, pillbox at W = 1 J: Ea = V/L (3.98e6 V / 8.724 mm = 456 MV/m), Ep = 551 MV/m, Hp = 852 kA/m, Ep/Ea = 1.21, Z0*Hp/Ea = 0.70; with rounded corners rb = 1 mm: Hp = 850 kA/m, Ep = 551 MV/m, rb = 2 mm: Hp = 845 kA/m, Ep = 550 MV/m. — [Simakov, USPAS 2018](https://uspas.fnal.gov/materials/18ODU/PillboxCavity_WithAnswers.pdf)
- Own calculation for that sharp-cornered closed pillbox at 1 J: E0 = 550.6 MV/m (equals CST's Ep, the on-axis end-cap peak) but analytic peak surface H_phi = (E0/Z0) J1(2.405) = 758.7 kA/m, which is 11 % below the 852 kA/m CST value (CST is 12 % above analytic) in the exercise. The exercise slides do not say why; the cause (corner/edge field singularity sampled on the mesh, or a different geometry detail) is unresolved.
- Iris-loaded USPAS cavities: pi-mode a = 0.05 lambda: Ea = 288 MV/m, Ep/Ea = 1.97, Z0 Hp/Ea = 0.98; Ea = V/L with L the cell length; 2pi/3-mode values use L = 8.75 mm. — [Simakov, USPAS 2018, with iris](https://uspas.fnal.gov/materials/18ODU/PillboxCavityWithIris_WithAnswers.pdf)
- IsoDAR report (snippet only): CST eigenmode results are computed for a reference stored energy of about 1 J; energies and losses scale with the square of the voltage ratio when scaling to the target voltage. — [IsoDAR@Yemilab PDR, arXiv 2404.06281](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06281)
- Tetrahedral-mesh convergence of eigenmodes (frequency and shunt impedance): 6.2 million curvilinear tetrahedra match an 80-million-cell hexahedral PBA result for the SPL TM010 pi mode (about 704.36 MHz), while hexahedral runs needed about 670 hours. — [IPAC2012 MOPPC059](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2012/papers/moppc059.pdf)

### Inferences
- A code reproducing CST's printed absolute values (V, Ea, Ep, Bp) must scale its eigenvector to W = (eps0/2) integral |E|^2 dV = 1 J before computing them; for ratios (Epk/Eacc, Bpk/Eacc [mT/(MV/m)], R/Q, G, Q0) the normalisation drops out. Bpk = mu0 Hpk.
- Eacc = V/L: for multi-cell cavities L = N*lambda*beta/2 (active length) if the user enters it as such; CST itself only knows the path length entered in the template. With a path that includes beam tubes, Eacc will be lower than the conventional value. The conventional Eacc in the table of a cavity paper may use the transit-time-weighted V (as in the USPAS data, where V/L = 456 MV/m with T = 0.83 included).
- Peak surface fields in CST come from the discretised field (max over mesh points/surface elements, "Max Evaluation" in the HIE-ISOLDE plots) rather than from a smooth surface reconstruction; expect mesh-refinement dependence near corners and a converging-from-below or overshooting behaviour. A comparison code should evaluate max over the same sort of surface sample set (e.g. surface quadrature points) and be compared on a refined mesh; the 11 % Hp discrepancy in the pillbox data is a warning that sharp edges are dangerous.

### Gaps
- Exact way CST finds peak surface values (on boundary mesh faces vs all vertices, interpolation order, any smoothing) is not documented in the sources I could read.
- No public CST-help statement of Eacc; the V/L relation is read from the USPAS numbers.
- I found no mesh-convergence study of Epk/Bpk in CST eigenmode specifically (only the HIE-ISOLDE convergence plot over refinement steps).

## 4. Template-based post-processing (TBPP) templates relevant to cavities and their parameters

### Takeaway
Cavity quantities in CST eigenmode runs come from two places: Post Processing > Loss and Q (Q per mode) and Post Processing > Template Based Post Processing > 2D and 3D Field Results > 3D Eigenmode Result (R over Q / shunt impedance, over a user-defined line). Templates can be chained (e.g. convert to 1D, mixer for ratios); parameter sweeps inside a template re-solve the whole model unless the template is placed outside the sweep.

### Cited Findings
- Q: Post Processing (top menu) > Loss and Q; shunt impedance: Template Based Post Processing > 2D and 3D Field Results > 3D Eigenmode Result, Evaluate to read the Value column. — [Migliorati tutorial](https://indico.cern.ch/event/683638/contributions/2801720/attachments/1589041/2513891/Migliorati-tutorial-2018-CST2.pdf)
- The template is called "3D Eigenmode Result: R over Q"; the user selects mode and a line; template definitions are not part of the history list and (as of Jan 2025) could not be created from the Python interface, only their results read. — [3DS community, Controlling Post Processing using Python](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/question/simulia-community/cst-controlling-post-processing-using-python_cGvrhdduT82tQcdMkonZ3A)
- Transit-time-factor workflow (GSI, snippet only): R/Q is computed inside a parameter sweep, a normalisation R/Q with "consider particle velocity" off is computed outside the sweep, the sweep's scalar stream is converted to a 1D vector with a convert template and divided with a mixer template; a template parameter that is swept makes MWS recalculate the whole structure at every sweep point. — [GSI web-docs, TTF](https://web-docs.gsi.de/~ssanjari/simulation-of-cavity-transit-time-factor-in-cst-studio-suiter.html)
- HOM transverse shunt impedance has no built-in one-click result; JACoW authors wrote VBA macros for HOM-type identification and transverse R/Q (Panofsky-Wenzel or direct integration) and compared them with manual results (identification accuracy above 80 % even for high passbands). — [IPAC2016 WEPMB048](https://jacow.org/ipac2016/papers/WEPMB048.pdf)
- CST datasheet for accelerator components lists "shunt impedance and R/Q" among accelerator-specific quantities; the statement concerns the particle-in-cell solver's evaluation of Q, shunt impedance and R/Q (snippet only). — [3DS accelerator components datasheet](https://www.3ds.com/fileadmin/PRODUCTS/SIMULIA/PDF/datasheets/SIMULIA-EM-Accelerator_Components-Datasheet.pdf)
- Tool settings for mode counting: number of modes set in the Eigenmode solver dialog; tetrahedral-mesh default is robust and fast, AKS is fast but lossless-only on hex meshes, JDM robust but slower (CST seminar, snippet only). — [CAS Darmstadt 2009 CST seminar](https://cas.web.cern.ch/sites/default/files/lectures/darmstadt-2009/seminar-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- I did not find a published list of TBPP templates that directly output Epk, Bpk, G, Eacc or stored energy for eigenmodes; practical workflows seem to use the field-monitor/"Max" evaluation in the 3D field result plus the 1 J normalisation, and G = Rs*Q0 computed by hand.

### Gaps
- No access to the TBPP template catalogue (names, parameters such as line start/end, beta, frequency selection) from the official help; the only named cavity template found is "3D Eigenmode Result: R over Q".

## 5. Known pitfalls reported by users

### Takeaway
The most common errors are (1) factor 2 between linac and circuit conventions, (2) forgetting 1 J field scaling, (3) transit-time/beta settings (default behaviour unclear), (4) integration along a line that touches mesh edges or lies where transverse components do not vanish numerically, (5) ports/absorbers or bad geometry inflating Q, (6) mesh-dependent peak fields.

### Cited Findings
- Factor 2: the linac and circuit shunt impedance/R-over-Q differ by exactly 2 (see Q2); the literature itself uses both, so a published CST-based R/Q must be checked for which convention was applied afterwards. — [Jensen, arXiv 1302.5264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5264); [Jensen, arXiv 1201.3202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.3202)
- The same R/Q is quoted in different units: Ω (monopole), Ω/cm^2 (dipole), Ω/cm^4 (quadrupole) in CST-based work, and "LinacΩ" vs "CircuitΩ" in CLIC damped-cell benchmarks (HFSS vs CST), where the conversion Zy = Rz/Q * Q/omega * c/(2 dy^2) is used for CircuitΩ/m. — [Zhang et al., arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782); [IWG slides 2020 (author initials AG)](https://indico.cern.ch/event/915543/contributions/3849520/attachments/2032841/3402733/20200506_IWG_slides_AG.pdf)
- 1 J scaling: the voltage CST reports is the 1 J value (278 kV in the Fermilab case versus a nominal 240 kV); only field-type outputs need rescaling. — [Stevenson, arXiv 2508.03312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03312)
- Beta: "currently beta = 1 is assumed" in the voltage integration for the Fermilab-checked version, so low-beta cavities need the particle velocity set explicitly. — [Romanov, FNAL](https://beamdocs.fnal.gov/AD/DocDB/0048/004879/001/01July2015_2ndHarm.pdf)
- Transverse field noise: in an asymmetric SPL cavity with a coupler, the transverse E components on the beam axis were up to three orders of magnitude smaller than Ez but "can cause inaccurate value of shunt impedance"; the fix was a tetrahedral mesh symmetric about the beam axis so transverse components vanish. — [IPAC2012 MOPPC059](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2012/papers/moppc059.pdf)
- Q inflation: with beam ports CST gave Q0 about 14000 vs about 11700 for HFSS, resolved by changing the nose geometry (meshing/geometry sensitivity). — [arXiv 0910.0409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.0409)
- Eigenmode solvers cannot give R/Q of a waveguide-damped cell by themselves; a 2020 benchmark found R/Q depends on the damping waveguide length in both HFSS and CST eigenmode (with a port boundary), and a wakefield solver is needed; also a "factor 2 higher due to Y=0 electric BC" remark for a symmetry-plane quarter model. — [IWG slides 2020](https://indico.cern.ch/event/915543/contributions/3849520/attachments/2032841/3402733/20200506_IWG_slides_AG.pdf)
- CST version 2014 mis-sorted modes in lossy eigenmode runs (reported to CST). — [Marhauser THPB003](https://proceedings.jacow.org/SRF2015/papers/thpb003.pdf)
- Symmetry planes: when only a quarter or half of the structure is modelled, stored energy and losses are for the modelled fraction unless the symmetry factor is applied; the CLIC slide remarks "factor 2 higher due to Y=0 electric BC". (slide wording only) — [IWG slides 2020](https://indico.cern.ch/event/915543/contributions/3849520/attachments/2032841/3402733/20200506_IWG_slides_AG.pdf)

### Inferences
- Phase of the voltage: since V is complex, ensure the comparison code uses |V| (the eigenvector has an arbitrary global phase) and a mode-dependent real/imaginary split of E; for periodic or travelling mode problems use the phase advance explicitly. I found no user report of a CST phase bug; this is a generic caution.
- Mesh sensitivity of Epk/Bpk is expected from the discretised max evaluation and the pillbox data above, but no user report quantifying it for CST eigenmode was found.

### Gaps
- No forum thread (CST forum, ResearchGate, 3DS community) was retrievable that discusses R/Q factor 2 specifically for CST; the evidence for CST's convention is numerical.
- Mesh sensitivity of CST peak surface fields: no systematic public study found beyond the HIE-ISOLDE plots.
