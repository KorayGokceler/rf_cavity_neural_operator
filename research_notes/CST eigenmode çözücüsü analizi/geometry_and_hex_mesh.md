# CST Studio Suite: geometry kernel, CAD import and the hexahedral (FIT/PBA) mesh of the eigenmode solver

Scope and honesty note. The official CST online help (the only place where defaults of "lines per wavelength", "lower mesh limit", "mesh line ratio limit", AMR thresholds, healing tolerances are documented) could not be retrieved: the university/mirror hosts of the help (www.mweda.com, rose-hulman.edu, researchgate) were unreachable or blocked from this sandbox, and 3ds.com serves only marketing pages. Everything below therefore comes from vendor press/case-study material, CST-authored conference papers, accelerator-community papers/lab notes and a CERN tutorial. Items I could not source are listed under Gaps rather than guessed. Versions are given wherever a source states them (CST MWS 2002 = "Version 4", 2006B, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2014/2015, 2018, 2022-era).

Context fact that matters for the whole question: since CST MWS 2011 SP3 the Eigenmode solver exists in two flavours, a FIT solver on hexahedral meshes (the historical one) and a FEM solver on (curved) tetrahedral meshes. The CERN tutorial and the TEMF papers treat the tetrahedral one as the precise/preferred route for curved accelerator cavities. "Where CST's frequencies come from" therefore depends on which mesh type a given paper used (see Q1/Q3).

## Q1. Geometry kernel, CAD import/healing, curved-surface representation before meshing

### Takeaway
CST's modeler is built on the Spatial ACIS kernel (integrated 1994) with Spatial 3D InterOp translators for CAD import (CATIA V4/V5, IGES, STEP, VDA-FS) and automatic healing/simplification on import; SAT is the native exchange format. The geometry stays an exact B-rep (analytic/NURBS solids, Boolean-built) until the mesher samples it, so "curved surface before meshing" = exact ACIS surface, not a facetted approximation. Specific healing options, tolerance values and defaults are not public in the sources I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- CST integrated the Spatial ACIS 3D modeling engine into its software in 1994 and later added Spatial's 3D InterOp translators for "all high-end CAD translations"; CST chose ACIS over Parasolid for stability and Boolean-operation handling; before that, MAFIA had a self-built modeling kernel — [Spatial/CST case study (2016)](https://blog.spatial.com/hubfs/_CASESTUDIES/CST_2016_CS.pdf)
- Imported models go through Spatial translators (CATIA V5, CATIA V4, IGES, STEP, VDA-FS listed); "the Spatial translators perform automatic healing and simplification. The ACIS modeling kernel is used to fill gaps and smooth edges"; after import the user can edit parameters and "ACIS will rebuild the structure"; export as IGES or SAT is via Spatial translators — [Spatial/CST case study (2016)](https://blog.spatial.com/hubfs/_CASESTUDIES/CST_2016_CS.pdf)
- The meshing step takes "the model, with its assigned material properties" and fills it into the mesh; the user can cross-check with the ACIS model to see where mesh lines fall and what material fills each cell — [Spatial/CST case study (2016)](https://blog.spatial.com/hubfs/_CASESTUDIES/CST_2016_CS.pdf)
- CST MWS Version 4 (2002) added STEP import to existing IGES and STL, direct CATIA and Pro/E import, STEP and 2D DXF export; ACIS is "CST MICROWAVE STUDIO's native format" (so ACIS-based CAD is addressed directly), Parasolid-based CAD only via interchange formats; "Automatic healing with individual part reporting is set by default on import of external geometry"; healing was improved in V4 because mechanical and EM CAD have different requirements — [Microwave Journal, "A Revolution in the Time Domain" (Aug 2002)](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- Thin metallic bodies can be imported (e.g. STL car) and represented as 2D surface conditions (sheet meshing) rather than 3D cells (Version 4, 2002) — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- FEM (tetrahedral) side: curved elements "enable a conformal representation of the geometry"; CST's "True Geometry Approximation" (TGA) is said to be needed for convergence to correct results; the curved eigenmode FEM solver was introduced in MWS 2011 SP3 (June 2011) — [Microwave Journal, CST press release (Jun 2011)](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/11362-cst-announces-curved-finite-elements-now-also-for-the-eigenmode-solver)
- The curved tetrahedral eigenmode solver uses curved elements (geometry order up to 5 selectable in the 2018 solver; reference run: 200,771 curved tets of order 5, 3rd-order basis, "desired accuracy 1e-12", gave 1.27666401260 GHz for a one-cell TESLA cavity, agreeing with an independent isogeometric reference 1.276664064 GHz to 4.0e-8 relative) — [Bembel/CST comparison, arXiv 2001.09686 (CST MWS 2018)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09686)
- TEMF/Darmstadt papers describe the CST real-valued FEM eigenmode solver as "basis functions up to third order on curved tetrahedral elements" (2015) and generate the geometry/tet mesh in CST MWS before passing it to the TEMF complex-valued solver by ASCII/binary file — [Liu et al., ICAP2015](https://inspirehep.net/files/f5fa81124143df6be5126be6e243c694)
- In the hexahedral flow a cavity "was approximated by hexahedral mesh cells" with mesh lines placed so that iris radius and equator radius were exactly matched by mesh lines (user-controlled fixed lines on the ACIS geometry) — [Zhang, Baboi, Jones, arXiv 1206.2782 (CST MWS 2011)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)

### Inferences
- Because CST samples an exact ACIS B-rep and Booleans are done in ACIS, the "true" cavity for CST is the analytic CAD shape (ellipse arcs, cylinders), identical to what an NGSolve OCC/CSG model would hold. Differences in frequency between pipelines should therefore be attributed to discretization (staircase/PBA vs curved FEM), not to geometry representation — provided the same STEP/analytic definition is fed to both.
- Healing is on by default and silently fills gaps/smooths edges; for a mirrored pipeline, importing the same STEP into NGSolve/OCC should be followed by an explicit volume/area check against the analytic value because CST's healing may have altered tiny features (sources do not quantify it).
- The 2015-2018 curved-tet eigenmode solver (geometry order up to 5, basis order up to 3) is the closest CST analogue to an NGSolve curved p2/p3 HCurl pipeline; the hexahedral eigenmode solver is the "legacy FIT" route.

### Gaps
- Names, defaults and numeric tolerances of CST's import/healing options (e.g. "simplify", "heal", tolerance in mm) were not found in any public source I could open. Only the generic vendor statement of automatic healing exists.
- No public statement found on how CST internally tessellates ACIS curved surfaces for the tetrahedral mesher beyond the curved-element/TGA description above.
- ACIS version per CST release not found.

## Q2. Hexahedral mesh for the eigenmode solver: global/local properties and expert settings

### Takeaway
The hexahedral mesh is a rectilinear (non-uniform) grid whose mesh-line positions are generated by an automatic "expert system" from the structure's fixpoints and a lines-per-wavelength target at the highest frequency of interest, bounded by a lower mesh limit / max-to-min (line) ratio, optionally refined by adaptive passes. User reports for accelerator cavities use 20-70 lines per wavelength with max steps around 1 mm. Exact default values and the precise definitions of the numeric limits are not available from public sources I could read; the best available definitions come from a lab note on the transient solver, which shares the same hex mesher.

### Cited Findings
- General description: rectilinear mesh in which field values are distributed around edges and face centres; the expert mesh system "analyses a structure and its known electromagnetic properties to give a good mesh from the outset"; the mesher refines at metal edges where fields are singular (extended in Version 4 to all mesh edges, not only straight orthogonal ones) — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- The mesher picks "important points inside the structure (fixpoints)" and places mesh nodes there; the user can add fixpoints manually and control the number of mesh lines per wavelength; energy-based adaptation refines in a defined number of passes — [Vandenbosch & Vasylchenko, InTechOpen "A Practical Guide to 3D Electromagnetic Software Tools" (describes CST transient setup)](https://cdn.intechopen.com/pdfs/14726.pdf)
- CST has four hex meshing modes: Staircase, FPBA (Fast PBA), PBA, EFPBA (Enhanced-accuracy FPBA). Start with PBA, switch to EFPBA above ~100 lines/wavelength because "the mesh generator will fail when the density of mesh cells becomes too high and will suggest using EFPBA" (user lab note, transient solver, CST 2014-era with "Hexahedral Legacy Grid" vs "2014 version" option) — [Mozdzen, ASU/EDGES memo (2014)](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/loco-memos/edges_reports/tom_20141111_part2.pdf)
- Same memo, parameter menu structure: mesh is set by Solver menu, Global Mesh Properties, Boundaries and Background; multilevel sub-gridding (MSS) option, "Equilibrate mesh ratio", "max cell aspect ratio", "refine at metal edges" factor; a Fix Points tab (all boxes default-checked) and an Advanced tab with "Use TST cells" (checked by default) and "Point accuracy enhancement" (0% default, user raised to 75-95%); CST recommends sub-gridding only if it reduces cell count by factor 3; default bounding-box padding is 1/8 wavelength — [Mozdzen memo](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/loco-memos/edges_reports/tom_20141111_part2.pdf)
- Same memo, parameters appearing in its run tables: "Lines per Wavelength", "Lower Mesh Limit" (example 45), "Mesh (Line) Ratio Limit" (example 65, also 100), "Edge Enhancement"; a good-run table uses 110 lines/wavelength with "Max to min ratio 50"; it warns against setting a minimum mesh step instead of the max/min ratio ("a feature that may not be working quite right in CST ... not recommended", caused meshing problems); excessive meshing can degrade accuracy — [Mozdzen memo](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/loco-memos/edges_reports/tom_20141111_part2.pdf)
- Eigenmode, hexahedral, user settings in a CST MWS 2011 study of the TESLA-type third-harmonic cavity: solver accuracy 1e-6 (relative eigensystem residual); quarter structure with symmetry planes; max mesh step ~1.1 mm for the accelerating mode (~2.1 M cells for a quarter); per-frequency-band tables give lines per wavelength 70 (3.7-3.95 GHz) down to 20 (above ~7.5 GHz), PBA vs FPBA, 1.0-3.0 M cells, max step 0.82-1.29 mm. Rule used: FPBA only if PBA failed to produce a valid mesh. Mid-cell (periodic) model: ~130,000 cells for a quarter, max step 0.85 mm — [Zhang et al., arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)
- Eigenmode flow in CERN's CST course: choose max frequency (higher f_max → more elements), background = PEC, boundaries E_t=0; the solver dialog offers Hexahedral or Tetrahedral mesh type with an adaptive-mesh-refinement checkbox for each; the same course switches the eigenmode project to hexahedral mesh so field files can be shared with the PIC solver — [CERN Accelerator School 2023 course notes (Wendt et al.)](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1294829/contributions/5442426/attachments/2698007/4683942/CAS2023_simulations_wCover.pdf)
- Mesh refinement is by density of mesh lines at fixed positions: cell counts in the SPL-cavity studies reached 80-100 million hex cells for an elliptical 5-cell cavity — [Liu et al., IPAC2011](https://jacow.org/IPAC2011/papers/WEPC093.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Lines per wavelength" is a global density rule (cells ≈ λ_min/N) at the highest frequency of the eigenmode search window; hence for eigenmodes the frequency range setting indirectly controls mesh density (CERN note: higher f_max → more elements). For a mirror NGSolve pipeline the equivalent is h ≈ λ(f_max)/N with N = 20-70 for CST hex, versus only a few elements per wavelength for p2/p3 curved HCurl.
- "Lower mesh limit" and "mesh line ratio limit" act as bounds on the smallest local step (expressed relative to the global step / to the largest step), i.e. they prevent tiny cells (which would only matter for explicit time stepping but also hurt conditioning in eigenmode). The memo's numbers (45 vs 65 vs 50/100) are consistent with this reading, but the definition is my inference, not a quoted spec.
- Placing mesh lines exactly on iris and equator radii (fixpoints) is a user-level trick that raises PBA accuracy because it avoids partially-filled cells at the geometric extrema; relevant when comparing frequencies with a curved FEM code.

### Gaps
- Official definitions/defaults of Lines per wavelength, Lower mesh limit, Mesh line ratio limit, Smallest mesh step, fixed-point options and "expert system" criteria for the Eigenmode solver: not found (help pages inaccessible). Only transient-solver user notes were found, so eigenmode-specific differences (e.g. how f_max is derived from the eigenmode frequency window) are unverified.
- Forum threads (EDAboard) offered unvetted advice (ratio ~10, 20 lines per wavelength to start); not used as findings.
- A "curvature refinement" option for the hexahedral mesh was not found documented; curvature settings found relate to the tetrahedral curved-element mesher (angle between adjacent tets; curved surfaces by third-order polynomials per an RFQ paper below).

## Q3. PBA and TST: treatment of partially filled cells and curved PEC walls, accuracy and limits

### Takeaway
PBA (Krietenstein, Schuhmann, Thoma, Weiland, 1998) keeps the Cartesian grid but uses sub-cell geometry (the fraction of each cell/edge/face inside the PEC and dielectric) in the FIT matrices, giving second-order accuracy claims for arbitrary shaped boundaries with near-zero extra cost. TST models thin PEC sheets as 2D surface conditions or slices a cell into three regions. Practical evidence from cavities, however, shows slow, non-monotonic eigenfrequency convergence on strongly curved elliptical cavities: 80 M PBA cells were needed to reach ~704.36 MHz versus 704.398 MHz from curved FEM (a 37 kHz, about 5e-5 relative difference, but at 670 h).

### Cited Findings
- Original PBA paper: orthogonal computational grid need not conform to rounded boundaries; "sub-cellular information is taken into account, leading to an algorithm with second order accuracy for arbitrary shaped boundaries"; extra cost per iteration "slightly additional ... factor near to one"; consistent extension of FIT preserving second-order accuracy and stability of the transient solver; "in most cases even equidistant meshes produce highly accurate results"; adaptive mesh generation with user-defined accuracy requirements implemented; staircase ("conventional") filling needed >60 steps/λ to reach accuracy PBA reached with 10 steps/λ in a coax-waveguide coupler (two orders of magnitude lower cost); PBA >10x faster than MAFIA with many non-Cartesian metallic boundaries — [Krietenstein, Schuhmann, Thoma, Weiland, LINAC'98 TH4041](https://epaper.kek.jp/l98/PAPERS/TH4041.PDF)
- The same paper also describes an alternative (NO-FIT) with non-orthogonal grids, whose interpolation raises the cost by a factor 2 (2D) to 3 (3D), and states conventional Yee/Cartesian meshes suffer along curved boundaries in resonance frequencies and Q-values — [LINAC'98 TH4041](https://epaper.kek.jp/l98/PAPERS/TH4041.PDF)
- PBA lets a cell be arbitrarily sliced into a dielectric region and a metal region; Version 4 (2002) extended this to three regions (two decoupled dielectric parts plus a thin metal sheet through the cell), which avoids "critical cells" (filled with whole cells of metal) and keeps the transient time step large; "version 3 would work, but would fill up sliced cell areas with critical cells" — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- Thin Sheet Technique: metal in thin sheets represented by 2D internal surface conditions rather than 3D cells; (transient) car model with 1.6 M cells; "Use TST cells" is a default-on option in the mesh Advanced tab — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain); [Mozdzen memo](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/loco-memos/edges_reports/tom_20141111_part2.pdf)
- Fast PBA mesher was introduced as new in MWS 2006B (more robust, faster); multilevel subgridding supports PBA and TST — [Microwave Journal 2007 (2006B release)](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/12747-enhanced-3d-em-simulation-performance-with-cst-microwave-studio-2006b)
- Mode "FPBA with Enhanced PBA accuracy" exists; in eigenmode studies the authors used FPBA only when PBA failed to generate a valid mesh (typically above ~7 GHz / at coarse 20-25 lines per wavelength) — [Zhang et al., arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)
- Cavity-level accuracy evidence, five-cell 704 MHz SPL cavity, CST MWS 2010/2011 hex eigenmode: TM010 pi-mode frequency drifts from ~701 to ~704.36 MHz as mesh grows to 80 M hex cells (PBA); converged value 704.361 MHz "not very satisfying", run time ~670 h and 51 GB at 100 M cells (111 h at 61 M); curved-tet FEM gives 704.398 MHz (reported also 704.397) with far fewer cells (6 M tets) — [Liu et al., IPAC2012 MOPPC059](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2012/papers/moppc059.pdf); [Liu et al., IPAC2011 WEPC093](https://jacow.org/IPAC2011/papers/WEPC093.pdf)
- Same authors, transient solver with 49.6 M FPBA hex cells gave 703.755 MHz for the same mode vs design 704.4 MHz — [Liu et al., IPAC2012](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2012/papers/moppc059.pdf)
- Cross-code agreement: CST hex eigenmode frequencies of a 9-cell third-harmonic cavity differ from MAFIA and from HFSS by up to 10 MHz — [Zhang et al., arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)
- Boundary fields: in a Rostock talk on perturbation methods, CST's hex "dual grid with PBA (partially filled cells)" gives fields near the boundary with an "abrupt transition to zero" and defective boundary range, which matters for volume integrals (e.g. bead-pull/perturbation) — [Brackebusch/van Rienen ICAP2012 talk](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/ICAP2012/talks/moadc2_talk.pdf)
- CST's own account of why curved FEM was added: precise geometry is "required for the computation of eigenfrequencies as well as surface currents (losses)"; FEM curved elements "improve simulation accuracy" — [Microwave Journal 2011](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/11362-cst-announces-curved-finite-elements-now-also-for-the-eigenmode-solver)
- RFQ study (CST 2022-era): quality of fields near curved vanes improved when using curved tetrahedral settings (third-order polynomial surfaces) and local multi-meshing; frequency error compared to theory shown versus mesh parameters — [ESS RFQ simulations paper, IPAC2022 WEPOMS049](https://jacow.org/IPAC2022/papers/WEPOMS049.pdf)

### Inferences
- The formally second-order statement (PBA, 1998) is a transient/FIT statement for fields; for eigenfrequencies of strongly curved cavities the SPL data show drift of ~0.4% in the coarse range and an unresolved ~40 kHz residual at 80 M cells, which suggests an effective order below 2 (or a non-smooth error because partially cut cells are discarded/stabilised). Do not assume monotone second-order convergence of CST hex frequencies; extrapolate with caution.
- In FIT with PBA the cut cells keep a (possibly small) fraction of volume; stabilisation (e.g. FPBA dropping/merging critical cells) alters the effective volume, which would shift frequencies by small non-monotone amounts, consistent with the "fast PBA only if PBA fails" practice and the odd convergence curves.
- CST hex eigenvalues are not guaranteed to be upper or lower bounds of the exact eigenvalues (FIT is a finite-difference-like scheme). By contrast, a conforming FEM Ritz value is an upper bound; so differences CST-hex vs NGSolve may have either sign. Hex values at coarse meshes in the SPL data approach from below (701 -> 704.36 MHz).
- Mirroring the hex mesh in NGSolve is not meaningful; the sensible reference for a curved p2/p3 HCurl pipeline is CST's curved-tet FEM eigenmode solver (4th-5th order geometry, 3rd-order basis) or analytic cases (pillbox, spheres), with CST hex used as a cross-check at tolerance of the order of 1e-4 to 1e-3 relative on curved cavities with cell counts of a few million.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed convergence study of PBA eigenfrequency order for 3D curved cavities found; the second-order claim is the authors' own (1998 conference paper, abstract + intro level).
- TST original publication and its accuracy/limits not found (searches returned nothing); only vendor descriptions. Known TST limitations (e.g. no thickness, handling of coincident sheets) are undocumented in sources I could reach.
- Details of how PBA enters the eigenmode matrix (cell fill factors in the curl/mass matrices, the Zagorodnov/Schuhmann "conformal" time-step treatment) are in IEEE/COMPEL papers I could not open (IEEE blocks bots).

## Q4. Adaptive mesh refinement for the eigenmode solver: monitored quantity, thresholds, passes, reporting

### Takeaway
Documented: for the eigenmode solver the AMR is driven by the change in the eigenfrequencies between passes (refinement increases mesh density, e.g. in high-field areas, "until the variation in resonance frequency of the eigenmodes is less than the error value given"). Default thresholds and pass counts were not recoverable from public sources; users read convergence from a 1D result "Adaptive Meshing" folder listing mode frequencies and tetrahedron counts per pass.

### Cited Findings
- CERN CST course: with the eigenmode solver "adaptive mesh refinement will increase the mesh density in, e.g. high field areas until the variation in resonance frequency of the Eigenmodes is less than the error value given"; results appear under 1D Results > Adaptive Meshing, showing mode frequencies and number of tetrahedrons per mesh pass; for periodic cell models "Refine tetrahedral mesh" is ticked — [CAS 2023 course notes](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1294829/contributions/5442426/attachments/2698007/4683942/CAS2023_simulations_wCover.pdf)
- The same course notes list "Adaptive hexahedral mesh refinement" as a separate checkbox for hexahedral runs (switched off in their frequency-domain example) — [CAS 2023 course notes](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1294829/contributions/5442426/attachments/2698007/4683942/CAS2023_simulations_wCover.pdf)
- RFQ study: "dynamic adaptive mesh refinement ... improves mesh distribution from run to run converging to a selected frequency criterion" with curved-element settings (adjacent-tet angle, third-order curved surfaces) — [IPAC2022 WEPOMS049](https://jacow.org/IPAC2022/papers/WEPOMS049.pdf)
- Transient-solver analogue (same hex mesher family): adaptive options "energy based" (works with sub-gridding) vs "expert system based"; user sets min/max number of passes, frequency range for checks, a maximum delta between runs as stop criterion, "number of checks" = successive passes that must meet the delta; mesh growth factor; example used: 4 passes min, 7 max, delta S11 = 0.001, growth 0.7. In that study the best mesh was found early and denser meshes did not always improve results — [Mozdzen memo](https://loco.lab.asu.edu/loco-memos/edges_reports/tom_20141111_part2.pdf)
- Eigenmode solver accuracy setting used in cavity studies: relative eigensystem residual 1e-6 (hex, 2011) and "desired accuracy 1e-12" (tet, 2018) — [arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782); [arXiv 2001.09686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09686)
- Version history: Version 4 (2002) made the Eigenmode solver use the Frequency Domain solver (no extra license) to obtain a first solution, so that a handful of modes suffice — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- Adaptive generation with user-defined accuracy goals for PBA meshes was in the 1998 design — [LINAC'98 TH4041](https://epaper.kek.jp/l98/PAPERS/TH4041.PDF)

### Inferences
- For hexahedral eigenmode runs the monitored quantity is likely the same eigenfrequency change; a refinement pass places new mesh lines near high-field regions, then re-solves. The SPL data suggest that this criterion can be satisfied long before the absolute frequency is converged (drifts of 0.1-0.4% persisted), so "AMR converged" in CST hex should not be read as "frequency error below delta f".
- Users typically report convergence either as a table/plot of mode frequency versus pass or versus cell count (e.g. arXiv 1206.2782 tables lines per wavelength/cells; TEMF plots frequency vs cells). A pipeline comparing with CST should therefore store the pass-by-pass frequency history, not only the final value.

### Gaps
- Default error threshold (relative Δf), default pass limits, number of consecutive passes required, and whether refinement is error-estimator or field-energy based for the eigenmode hex solver: not documented in any reachable source.
- Whether the tetrahedral AMR uses a residual-based estimator or a frequency-difference only: not found.

## Q5. Symmetry planes, boundary conditions and the resulting mode sets

### Takeaway
CST implements symmetry planes as electric (E_t=0) or magnetic (H_t=0) boundaries on the bounding box; this both reduces cost and restricts the set of modes found to those with the corresponding symmetry, so several runs with different plane combinations are needed to get every mode. Periodic boundaries with a user-defined phase advance give the modes of infinite periodic structures; EE vs MM end-cell boundaries give different mode subsets.

### Cited Findings
- CERN course: boundaries on the simulation box set to "electric (Et = 0)"; magnetic symmetry planes in the YZ and XZ planes for the pillbox reduce the problem; "It would be best to repeat the simulation later with different symmetry planes to ensure you get all modes" (TESLA cell with periodic boundary and a phase advance per cell, e.g. 180 deg) — [CAS 2023 course notes](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1294829/contributions/5442426/attachments/2698007/4683942/CAS2023_simulations_wCover.pdf)
- For eigenmode and beam-pipe components the background should be PEC; losses are added afterwards by perturbation from the lossless solution (so the Q0 is a post-processing quantity, wall-loss perturbation) — [CAS 2023 course notes](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1294829/contributions/5442426/attachments/2698007/4683942/CAS2023_simulations_wCover.pdf)
- Third-harmonic cavity study: a quarter of the structure with symmetry planes; EE (electric) and MM (magnetic) end boundaries produce different sets of eigenmodes (monopole, dipole, quadrupole, sextupole bands), and periodic boundary conditions E(r, z+L) = E(r,z) e^{i phi} give infinite-chain modes from one cell — [arXiv 1206.2782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2782)
- RFQ: quadrupole symmetry imposed by PEC (E_t=0) on the beam axis planes and PMC (H_t=0) on transverse planes to extract the fundamental quadrupolar mode — [IPAC2022 WEPOMS049](https://jacow.org/IPAC2022/papers/WEPOMS049.pdf)
- PETRA 7-cell: PMC symmetry planes used in the real-valued FEM calculation; 620 modes between 1.3 and 19.2 GHz computed for the elliptical cell with transversal (H_tan=0) and longitudinal (E_tan=0) symmetry planes — [ICAP2015 PETRA](https://inspirehep.net/files/f5fa81124143df6be5126be6e243c694); [Brackebusch & van Rienen IPAC2013](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2013/papers/mopwo008.pdf)
- Periodic boundaries with arbitrary phase were introduced in the Eigenmode and Frequency Domain solvers in Version 4 (2002) — [Microwave Journal 2002](https://microwavejournal.com/articles/3491-a-revolution-in-the-time-domain)
- Complex-valued (lossy-port) eigenmodes are not available in CST's real-valued eigenmode solver; the TEMF code is used outside CST for ports — [ICAP2015 PETRA](https://inspirehep.net/files/f5fa81124143df6be5126be6e243c694)

### Inferences
- Symmetry planes with PEC walls on a symmetric cavity: a magnetic plane keeps modes with E_tan odd/H_tan even etc.; with several planes, each run picks a symmetry class, so a complete mode list = union over combinations (matching the course's repeat-runs advice). For the neural-operator training labels, a full-geometry NGSolve eigen-solve gives all modes including degenerate pairs, whereas a CST quarter model removes degeneracies of dipole pairs; compare mode counts accordingly.
- Hex symmetry planes align with mesh-line planes (exactly cut, no PBA effect), while symmetry planes in rotationally symmetric cavities cut curved walls identically in both codes.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of how mode numbering/ordering and multiplicity handling differ between symmetric and full models in CST; no quantification of accuracy impact of symmetry planes versus full model.
- Open (PML/waveguide port) boundaries for the eigenmode solver are not covered in sources found.
