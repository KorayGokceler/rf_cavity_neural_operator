## 1. FIT fundamentals: grids, Maxwell grid equations, material matrices, curl-curl operator, relation to Nedelec FEM, consistency order

### Takeaway
FIT (Weiland 1977) integrates Maxwell's equations in integral form over the faces/edges of a primal grid G and a dual grid G~; the topological parts (C, S, their duals) are exact incidence matrices (entries 0, +1, -1) and all approximation sits in the diagonal (or, on non-orthogonal grids, non-diagonal) material matrices. The conventional scheme is second order accurate in space; with staircase boundaries the global error on curved walls is dominated by the boundary approximation, and PBA/C-FIT restore second order for arbitrary boundaries. FIT on a Cartesian hexahedral grid is algebraically the Yee/lowest-order (N0-type) edge discretisation with a lumped (diagonal) mass matrix, not the full-mass Nedelec FEM.

### Cited Findings
- FIT originates in T. Weiland, "Eine Methode zur Loesung der Maxwellschen Gleichungen fuer sechskomponentige Felder auf diskreter Basis", AEU 31, 116-120 (1977); the later reference reviews are Weiland, Part. Accel. 15, 245-291 (1984) and Int. J. Numer. Model. 9, 295-319 (1996) ("Time domain electromagnetic field computation with finite difference methods"). — [Neugebauer & van Rienen, PAC99 (refs [1])](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p99/PAPERS/THA66.PDF); [Krietenstein et al., LINAC98 (refs [1],[2])](https://jacow.org/l98/papers/TH4041.pdf); [TU Darmstadt record of Weiland 1996](https://tubiblio.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/5125/)
- Grid structure: two staggered/dual grids. Electric grid voltages e = integral of E.ds and magnetic fluxes b = integral of B.dA live on the primal grid G; electric fluxes d and magnetic grid voltages h live on the dual grid G~. Setzer & Weiland (read in full) write the Maxwell Grid Equations as: C e = -db/dt (in the time-harmonic form C e = -i w b), C~ h = j + dd/dt, S~ d = q, S b = 0, with material relations d = D_eps e, b = D_mu h (their notation; D_eps, D_mu are diagonal material matrices incorporating cell lengths/areas/volumes, complex for lossy materials). — [Setzer & Weiland, LINAC98 (MO4041)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/l98/PAPERS/MO4041.PDF)
- Clemens' lecture slides (read in full, German) state the same: grid incidence matrices Div (S), Curl (C), Grad (G) with S C = 0 and C G = 0 (discrete div curl = 0, curl grad = 0, "exact by construction"); dual operators with S~ = -G^T, C~ = C^T for a dual-orthogonal (FIT) grid; the dual-orthogonal FIT grid pair versus a barycentric dual grid (Whitney-FEM) are named as the two ways the dual is built; on non-orthogonal grid pairs the diagonal material matrices D_eps, D_mu become non-diagonal M_eps, M_mu (angles up to 120 deg tolerated in the 2D test). — [Clemens, WIAS colloquium 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Exactness statement: "the discretised Maxwell equations remain mathematically equivalent to the continuous case. There is no discretisation error and ... exactly obey the conservation law for charge and current density"; the only approximation is in the material relations (this is the standard FIT claim as repeated in the PAC99 paper). — [Neugebauer & van Rienen, PAC99](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p99/PAPERS/THA66.PDF)
- Cartesian/tetrahedral explicit grid equations (Hebermehl et al.): with lowest-order quadrature (closed-path integral of f.ds ~ sum(+-f_i s_i), area integral ~ f * area), the frequency-domain equations on a staggered non-equidistant rectangular grid are A^T D_{s/mu~} b = i w eps0 mu0 D_{A~} e and A D_s e = -i w D_A b (their Eq. 8); A is sparse with entries 0, 1, -1 only; D_s, D_A, D_{s/mu~}, D_{A~} carry cell dimensions and material; eliminating b gives (A^T D_{s/mu~} D_A^{-1} A D_s - k0^2 D_{A~}) e = 0 with k0 = w sqrt(eps0 mu0) (Eq. 9). The same paper builds FIT on tetrahedral primal grids with Voronoi dual cells (needs circumcentres inside the tetrahedra). — [Hebermehl et al., Adv. Radio Sci. 2, 107-112 (2004)](https://ars.copernicus.org/articles/2/107/2004/ars-2-107-2004.pdf)
- Cavity eigenproblem from FIT (PAC99, MAFIA lineage): "the matrix equation { D^{-1/2} C~ D C D^{-1/2}, D^{1/2} S~^T S D^{1/2} } u = w^2 u" where D, C, S contain material and mesh information and represent the curl-curl and grad-div (= -Laplacian) operators; u is the vector of all grid voltages; for homogeneous material it is Helmholtz' equation; 13 matrix elements are non-zero per degree of freedom on the hexahedral grid; the matrix connects a cell only to neighbouring cells (next-neighbour property). (Typeset symbols in the PDF were garbled in extraction; the structure curl-curl + grad-div is clear, exact placement of the diagonal factors should be taken from the paper.) — [Neugebauer & van Rienen, PAC99](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p99/PAPERS/THA66.PDF)
- Setzer & Weiland (read in full): combining the grid equations in the frequency domain gives the standard (not generalised) eigenproblem D_eps^{-1} C~ D_mu^{-1} C e = w^2 e with complex diagonal D_eps, D_mu for lossy materials. (My reading, not stated there: in the lossless case this operator is similar to a real symmetric positive semi-definite matrix.) — [Setzer & Weiland, LINAC98](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/l98/PAPERS/MO4041.PDF)
- Clemens slide "Zeitharmonische Probleme: Resonante Felder": the algebraic eigenproblem is written [M_eps'^{-1} C M_mu^{-1} C~ (+ a grad-div / G...S term, partly garbled in extraction)] e = w^2 e, solved "e.g. with the Jacobi-Davidson method", with eps'(w) = eps + sigma/(i w). — [Clemens, WIAS 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Order of accuracy: the ACES paper "Higher Order Spatial Operators for the Finite Integration Theory" (Spachmann, Schuhmann, Weiland, ACES J. 17(1), 2002) begins: "Up to now the conventional formulation itself has had an accuracy order of two with respect to the spatial discretization", and then presents fourth and higher-order FIT variants (with a proposed stable interface to standard FIT regions). — [ACES J. 17(1) abstract](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/ACES/article/view/16893)
- Clemens' 2004 slides report a convergence test of cavity eigenvalues (R. Schuhmann, 2001, 2D FIT with "Whitney finite elements"/non-orthogonal grids): "asymptotically 2nd order", comparable to standard FIT and FEM results. — [Clemens, WIAS 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Boundary treatment: the staircase ("conventional") Yee grid makes global quantities (resonant frequencies, Q) "suffer from the modelling errors along curved boundaries" (Krietenstein et al.). Two remedies, both said to keep "second order accuracy": FIT on generalised non-orthogonal grids (needs a symmetric interpolation, cost factor 2-3 for 2D/3D) and the Perfect Boundary Approximation (PBA), where the orthogonal grid need not be conformal and sub-cell information (partially filled cells) yields second order accuracy for arbitrarily shaped boundaries "with only slightly additional numerical cost (factor near one)". Clemens' slides list the lineage: improved staircase (Weiland 1977), "Conformal FIT" (Thoma 1996, = PBA lineage), boundary-conforming grids (Schuhmann 2000). — [Krietenstein, Schuhmann, Thoma, Weiland, LINAC98 TH4041](https://jacow.org/l98/papers/TH4041.pdf); [Clemens 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Relation to FEM (published): Egger et al. (2018) show that a mass-lumped lowest-order Nedelec (N0) mixed FEM "coincides with the spatial discretization of the Yee scheme" on orthogonal structured grids; the same paper notes that unstructured FIT-like extensions (Schuhmann & Weiland 1998; Codecasa & Politi 2008) "rely on the use of two sets of grids which makes a rigorous convergence analysis difficult". Clemens' slides contrast FIT (dual-orthogonal dual grid) with Whitney-FEM (barycentric dual grid) with the same incidence-matrix topology. — [Egger et al., arXiv:1810.06243 (snippet-level; abstract/intro)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06243); [Clemens 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Measured effect of the boundary treatment in a real CST run: for the 5-cell SPL elliptical cavity (704.4 MHz design), CST MWS 2011 hexahedral eigenmode (PBA) converged to 704.361 MHz for the pi mode only at 80 million cells and ~670 h, versus 704.398 MHz from a curved-tetrahedral higher-order FEM (TEMF in-house code) at 6.2 million cells; the 4/5, 3/5, 2/5, 1/5 pi modes show the same pattern (e.g. 692.416 vs 692.446 MHz; 703.065 vs 703.101 MHz), i.e. the hex PBA values sit ~30-40 kHz (~5e-5 relative) below the FEM values at 80 M cells. A 49.6 M-cell FPBA transient run gave 703.755 MHz for the pi mode (~0.09% low). — [Liu, Mueller, Ackermann, Weiland, IPAC2012 MOPPC059 (read in full)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2012/papers/moppc059.pdf)
- Boundary-layer defect in FIT fields: for a cylindrical cavity (R = 100 mm) the CST-FIT field components near the wall are "defective" over ~70 um (dual-grid placement), versus 0.3 um for curved-tet FEM; the authors conclude that curved tetrahedral FEM "proved to reproduce the boundary much more precisely than the hexahedral dual grid of FIT" and recommend FEM for curved cavities. In that study CST FIT frequencies of TM2n2 modes were nonetheless accurate to <1.6e-4 relative up to 35.7 GHz, degrading with frequency as the cell count is memory-limited. — [Brackebusch & van Rienen, ICAP2012 MOADC2 (read in full)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/ICAP2012/papers/moadc2.pdf)
- Caveat on provenance: the 2012 TEMF/Rostock comparisons are from groups that develop competing FEM codes (TEMF CEM3D, Rostock), against CST MWS 2011 and older PBA; CST's own later material (see Q4) says hex meshing defaults moved to "EFPBA".

### Inferences
- Writing the lossless hex-FIT problem as (derived from the cited grid equations, not quoted): K_FIT e = w^2 e with K_FIT = D_eps^{-1} C~ D_mu^{-1} C; with C~ = C^T this is similar to the symmetric form M_eps^{-1/2} C^T M_nu C M_eps^{-1/2}. Compared with HCurl-N0 FEM on a brick, K_FEM c = theta M c with M a consistent (non-diagonal) mass matrix; FIT = same stiffness (C^T M_nu C with exact incidence matrices) with a lumped diagonal mass. The N0 Yee/FIT scheme with lumped mass is second order for smooth eigenfunctions on uniform grids but has no p-refinement path, whereas an HCurl p2/p3 curved scheme is O(h^p) in the field and O(h^{2p}) in eigenvalue (standard Nedelec theory, not shown in the cited sources).
- Expected systematic differences vs. conforming curved high-order FEM: (i) FIT/PBA hex frequencies converge as O(h^2) from a boundary-fitted, mostly one-sided error (the SPL table shows hex < FEM at all five modes, i.e. a systematic low bias at 80 M cells for a convex-rounded shell, but the sign for other geometries is not established by the sources); the discrete FEM eigenvalues for conforming meshes are upper bounds only under conditions (this project's invariant), FIT eigenvalues carry no such bound; (ii) lumped mass gives phase-velocity dispersion (numerical dispersion), so higher-order/high-frequency modes of a large cavity (many cells per wavelength needed) have larger relative errors, matching the MOADC2 observation that accuracy degrades with frequency at fixed memory; (iii) fields are located on the dual/primal grid and wall-adjacent field values are inaccurate within about one cell, which affects Epk, Bpk and wall-loss integrals (hence Q, G, R_sh) more than frequencies; (iv) PBA is a volume/area-fraction correction, so curvature and thin features are represented with a cell-size-dependent error in the material matrices, unlike an isoparametric curved-element geometry.

(ICS Newsletter 8(2), 13-20, July 2001; it is NOT a PIER paper as the assignment's hint suggests, per [Wuppertal publication list](https://tet.uni-wuppertal.de/en/research/publications/pub-query-result/17/) via search) were not retrievable in full text; equations above come from secondary/primary conference papers and slides that were retrievable.

The sign/size of the hex-versus-FEM bias for other cavity shapes is unquantified in the sources.
- Whether CST's current "EFPBA" (default since a ~2012-era release per CST slides) preserves strict second order is not documented in any source I could read.

## 2. AKS (Advanced Krylov Subspace) eigensolver in CST: description, null-space handling, strengths/limits

### Takeaway
Public sources only name AKS and give a few one-line characterisations (faster for well-behaved problems, lossless only, hexahedral mesh, internally computes at least 10 modes). I found NO published algorithmic description of AKS (no paper defining the Krylov method, shifting strategy, or its specific treatment of the huge curl-curl null space). Treat any detailed account of AKS internals as unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- CST product material: the Eigenmode Solver "incorporates the Advanced Krylov Subspace method (AKS) and the Jacobi-Davidson method (JDM)", for resonant structures such as high-Q accelerator cavities, filters, slow-wave structures. — [EverythingRF summary of CST release](https://www.everythingrf.com/news/details/18811-dassault-systemes-offers-cst-studio-suite-with-multiple-high-frequency-electromagnetic-simulation-solvers); [3DS CST solver page](https://www.3ds.com/it/products/simulia/cst-studio-suite/electromagnetic-simulation-solvers)
- CST seminar slides (CAS Darmstadt 2009, "seminar 2", CST presenter) — snippet-level only, the PDF itself could not be fetched here (URL returned 404 to my client, the search tool reported the text): "AKS might be faster for well behaved examples, JD is more robust and might even find good solutions for badly conditioned problems"; "The AKS method always calculates internally at least 10 modes" (so requesting 1 vs 10 modes costs about the same); JD computes modes one after another (cost roughly proportional to mode count). — [CAS Darmstadt 2009 seminar-2.pdf (search result; direct fetch failed)](https://cas.web.cern.ch/sites/default/files/lectures/darmstadt-2009/seminar-2.pdf)
- CST seminar slides Feb 2016 (DESY, "Simulations with EM Studio and Particle Studio") — snippet-level only: comparison table lists AKS as "fast, no losses, hexahedral"; JDM as "robust but slow, hexahedral"; the default as tetrahedral, "robust and fast"; one example where the hexahedral mesh did not converge while the tetrahedral converged in < 2 min. — [DESY CST seminar Feb 2016 (search result; direct fetch failed)](https://desy.de/~mpymax/Talks/Slides%20CST%20Seminar%20Feb-2016.pdf)
- FIT-side prior art for the eigenproblem on the hex grid: the MAFIA system matrix has a known sparsity pattern (13 non-zeros per DOF) so the matrix-vector product is a local operation; at the time of the 1999 PAC paper (APE-100 SIMD port) only a "simple variant of a polynomial iteration algorithm" searching the lowest eigenvalue in the subspace orthogonal to already found eigenvectors was installed (5000 fixed iterations, no convergence test), with accelerated CG (Kalkreuther & Simma, lattice QCD) and Jacobi-Davidson named as the next candidates. — [Neugebauer & van Rienen, PAC99](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p99/PAPERS/THA66.PDF)
- Krylov methods in FIT context (linear systems, not eigenproblems): Clemens' ACES 1996 paper treats BiCG, CGS, CGS2, BiCGSTAB(l), TFQMR with preconditioning for FIT time-harmonic and quasistatic systems. — [Clemens, ACES J. 11(1), 1996 abstract](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/ACES/article/view/17415)
- Null-space facts that apply to any edge-based curl-curl eigenproblem (not CST-specific): curl grad = 0 in the discrete setting (C G = 0, exact by construction in FIT), so every gradient of a nodal potential is a zero-eigenvalue "static" mode; the curl-curl matrix is positive semi-definite. — [Clemens slides: "C G = 0, S C = 0", "C M_nu C - curl-curl stiffness matrix (positive semi-definite)"](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf)
- Search-engine-reported (not read in full): for the Yee scheme the spurious null space has dimension equal to the number of cells/nodes, "one third of the total degrees of freedom" (reported from a null-space-free Jacobi-Davidson paper); Geus' ETH thesis finds Jacobi-Davidson much more effective than ARPACK implicitly-restarted Lanczos for cavities and notes that with edge elements all spurious modes have eigenvalue exactly zero. — [Geus, ETH thesis 2002 (snippet)](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/4c1f151d-4c42-48b1-a54c-ca4bdba3ae3d/content)
- PAC99 names the grad-div term (S~^T S) in the FIT eigen-operator (see Q1), which is one published way for the MAFIA-lineage operator to lift the gradient null space: it shifts the longitudinal/static part to a nonzero spectrum ("curl-curl + grad-div" form, formally -Laplace operator). — [Neugebauer & van Rienen, PAC99](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p99/PAPERS/THA66.PDF)

### Inferences
- The "no losses" note on AKS together with "at least 10 modes internally" and "may fail to converge for badly conditioned problems" is consistent with a block/restarted Krylov method (Lanczos/Arnoldi type) on a real symmetric operator, probably with shift-and-invert or polynomial filtering about a target; however this is inference, not published.
- Because a real-symmetric Krylov method on K = C^T M_nu C would converge to the huge zero-eigenvalue cluster first, any practical solver must either (a) work with the shifted operator around the target frequency ("calculate modes above f_min"), (b) add the grad-div term of PAC99 (MAFIA lineage), or (c) project with the gradient basis G. Which of these AKS uses is NOT stated in any public source I found. For a comparison with this repository's kernel projection (PV = V - G Kp^{-1} G^T M V), the FIT null space is the explicit nodal-gradient space (C G = 0 exactly) so an exact projector is available; whether CST uses it is unknown.

- No peer-reviewed or vendor description of AKS (algorithm, deflation of static modes, stopping criterion, preconditioner) found. The CST help topic "Eigenmode Solver" and "AKS Eigenmode Solver" were not retrievable. ## 3. JDM (Jacobi-Davidson) in CST: use cases, preconditioning, convergence settings

### Takeaway
Jacobi-Davidson (JDQR variant) entered the FIT lineage in MAFIA (Setzer & Weiland, 1998) specifically for highly lossy materials, where Lanczos-type subspace methods struggle; it targets eigenvalues near a user-specified complex frequency and avoids "upper limit of the spectrum" parameters. In CST, JDM is the robust-but-slow hex option (and, in the Lossy/General solver, the vehicle for complex eigenvalues); preconditioning and exact convergence settings are not documented publicly.

### Cited Findings
- Setzer & Weiland (read in full): JD "proves to be a rather suitable method for calculating a set of eigenvalues even in the case of highly absorbing materials"; unlike commonly used subspace methods it is not limited to extreme eigenvalues and finds eigenfrequencies around any user-specified frequency (target); no parameters such as an upper spectrum limit; the JDQR variant (Fokkema, Sleijpen, van der Vorst: partial Schur form with explicit deflation) is used so that subspace information from the first eigenvalue speeds up the following ones; implemented for rz-problems in the MAFIA eigenvalue module; test: HOM-damped Fermilab cavity with lossy rings (eps = 5 - 5i, mu = 100 - 3i), first 10 eigenmodes and Q computed with target set to zero (e.g. mode 1: 54.608 + 0.657i MHz, Q = 41.56; mode 2: 63.723 + 15.888i MHz, Q = 2.01). Complex diagonal D_eps/D_mu enter the standard eigenproblem. — [Setzer & Weiland, LINAC98 (MO4041)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/l98/PAPERS/MO4041.PDF)
- Jacobi-Davidson basics (as given in the TEMF comparison paper): Ritz pair from a B-orthonormal subspace nearest a target tau; residual test ||(A - theta B) u||_2 < eps; correction equation (I - B u u^T)(A - theta B)(I - u u^T B) z = -r, z orthogonal to u, solved iteratively (tfqmrs in CEM3D, bcgsl in SLEPc, qmrs in Pysparse). — [Yaman, Ackermann, Weiland, ICAP2012 FRSAC2 (read in full)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/ICAP2012/papers/frsac2.pdf)
- Vector-weighted JD for mode selection in 2D FIT waveguide models: Bandlow & Schuhmann extended JD with a weighting function so only modes with a desired field distribution are generated (ACES J. 24(6), 2009); related work (Hebermehl et al.) finds the lowest-attenuation modes of PML-terminated FIT waveguide models by a sequence of eigenproblems with modified matrices. — [ACES J. 24(6) abstract](https://journals.riverpublishers.com/index.php/ACES/article/view/16015); [Hebermehl et al. 2004](https://ars.copernicus.org/articles/2/107/2004/ars-2-107-2004.pdf)
- A TU Darmstadt/DESY talk (search snippet only) says JD control involves selecting eigenvalues inside a circular region around a user-given complex target in the complex frequency plane; I did not read the talk. — [DESY TEMF talk 2013 (search result)](https://www.desy.de/fel-beam/data/talks/files/DESY_TEMF_WA%202013-08-09.pdf)
- CST positioning (2016 slide, snippet): JDM "robust but slow" on hexahedral meshes; CAS-2009 slide (snippet): JD finds modes sequentially, so cost scales with mode count and the mode count should be kept low. — see Q2 sources.
- Independent commentary from TEMF researchers: in a CST 2012 test with the Eigenmode solver on tetrahedral meshes, CST and Matlab formed one time-consumption group (slower than the iterative SLEPc/Pysparse/CEM3D group below ~1e5-2.5e5 DOF), with the target-value ("frequency estimate") flexibility of CST and CEM3D highlighted; accuracy 1e-9 was requested for 20 eigenvalues. NOTE: in this paper the meshes were TETRAHEDRAL (CST was used to mesh), so it does not characterise the hex AKS/JDM solvers. — [Yaman et al., ICAP2012](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/ICAP2012/papers/frsac2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The mapping "CST JDM = MAFIA JDQR lineage on the FIT hex grid" is plausible (same institution, same lineage, same robustness claims) but no source states it.
- In the hex solver, a shift/target close to the desired band both selects modes and keeps the iteration away from the zero-eigenvalue null space; the grad-div trick or an explicit projection would still be required for the correction equation to be well-posed, again not documented.

- CST's JDM preconditioner, correction-equation solver, restart parameters, "accuracy" meaning (residual norm vs relative eigenvalue tolerance) and defaults per version are not in public documents I could access. The Glama MCP wrapper (a third-party tool listing) quotes an "accuracy default 1e-6" and "mode count 1-50, default 5" for the CST API, but that is a wrapper, not CST documentation. — [Glama CST orchestrator MCP tool](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/guardianer9-debug/cst-studio-orchestrator-mcp/tools/cst_configure_eigenmode_solver) (weak source)

## 4. The "default" / newer solver, release changes 2010-2025, mode count, frequency shift, accuracy, sorting

### Takeaway
CST's "default" eigenmode solver since MWS 2011 SP3 is NOT a hex-FIT Krylov-Schur variant: it is a curved-tetrahedral higher-order FEM eigensolver; hex FIT (AKS/JDM) is the alternative. I found no public statement that any CST eigensolver is "Krylov-Schur". The 2021 "General (Lossy)" eigensolver is the newest documented change. Mode sorting and loaded/unloaded frequency ambiguities are documented as bugs/pitfalls in CST 2014.

### Cited Findings
- June 2011: CST announced a finite-element Eigenmode Solver in MWS 2011 SP3, "alongside the FIT based eigenmode solver", with curved elements of arbitrary order and "True Geometry Approximation" for conformal geometry; applications include resonance frequencies, Q, and periodic structures (slow-wave structures, metamaterial unit cells with phase diagrams). — [Electronic Specifier, 7 June 2011](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=114337)
- CST talk at ICAP2012 (F. Wolfheimer, CST AG; read in full): "Curved tetrahedral mesh available (up to 3rd order)", "improved performance and convergence for many examples", tetrahedral example "< 2 min to calculate 9 modes"; and for hexahedral meshes: PBA "very accurate, but relies on valid CAD shapes", FPBA "faster ... can handle even CAD models with artifacts ... less accurate", and "EFPBA is now the default algorithm used for solvers based on hexahedral meshes; Enhanced FPBA provides both the robustness of FPBA and the accuracy of PBA". — [Wolfheimer, CST AG, ICAP2012 talk MOAAI2](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/ICAP2012/talks/moaai2_talk.pdf)
- 2016 CST slide table (snippet-level): default = tetrahedral ("robust and fast"), AKS = hexahedral/fast/no losses, JDM = hexahedral/robust/slow. — see Q2.
- March 2021 SIMULIA community post "Eigenmode solver new features 2021": the "General (Lossy)" eigenmode solver handles dispersive material, open boundaries and ports inside the computational domain, and "Total Q calculation is now done directly by solver and no perturbation theory approximation is employed." — [3DS Community, 11 Mar 2021 (fetched)](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/post/simulia-community/eigenmode-solver-new-features-2021_cksixj8uRYy8gV0Xd8vDYg)
- An older release note (search snippet) mentions "the lossy JD Eigenmode solver now offers the option to use a TD compatible material treatment" — version not identified; not verified.
- Mode sorting / frequency pitfalls: Ref. SRF2015 (read, key passages): a CST 2014 Eigenmode-solver bug produced wrong mode numbering because modes were sorted either by unloaded frequency f0 or loaded frequency fl (which depends on Qext); using dissipative material (absorbers) instead of waveguide ports cured it since the computed frequency is then fl; Qext results for high-Q couplers varied with symmetry planes and number of modes; "the Eigenmode solver might not always converge swiftly"; the authors recommend computing a small number of modes (4-10) sequentially; a full 3D LCLS-II cavity with HOM couplers up to 3.2 GHz needed "a few working days" on a 2-processor workstation (tetrahedral curved mesh). — [SRF2015 THPB003 (CERN-hosted; author list not captured in my extraction)](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/SRF2015/papers/thpb003.pdf)
- IPAC2013: "the most efficient commercially available eigenmode solvers are based on real-valued analysis", sufficient only for the lossless structure; complex-valued eigensolvers (like TEMF's FEM code, second-order curved tetrahedra) are needed for lossy/ported structures, shown on 192 TESLA modes to 3.12 GHz; the resonance is complex w = w' + j a, Q = w'/(2a). — [Liu et al., IPAC2013 MOPWO007](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2013/papers/mopwo007.pdf)
- Search-engine-only items on "accuracy"/"modes above": the CST help wording for "Accuracy" and "Calculate modes above" was not retrievable; the TEMF comparison used "accuracy 10^-9" for 20 modes in a CST tetrahedral run (see Q3), which at least shows the setting is user-controllable down to 1e-9 in 2012.

### Inferences
- "Krylov-Schur" in the user's prompt most likely refers either to generic eigensolver terminology (it appears only in a list of algorithm names in the TEMF ICAP2012 abstract) or to the FEM-side default solver; with the sources available it is not attributable to CST hex FIT. The report should state that the CST "default" is tetrahedral FEM and that the algorithmic name for it is undocumented publicly.

## 5. Losses: perturbation versus direct lossy eigenproblem; periodic boundaries with phase shift

### Takeaway
Classical CST (hex AKS and the FEM default) solves a lossless real-symmetric problem and obtains Q by first-order perturbation (power-loss integral over wall/materials); a direct complex ("lossy"/general) eigenproblem exists via JD (MAFIA lineage, 1998) and was extended in CST ("General (Lossy)", 2021) to dispersive materials, open boundaries and ports, with Q from the complex frequency. Periodic structures with Floquet phase shift are supported by the eigenmode solver (phase-advance, dispersion diagrams).

### Cited Findings
- Perturbation Q: "in cavities without dissipative material, this perturbation method yields the unloaded Q0 given the surface conductivity of materials ... accounts for both the surface losses (zero for PEC) and the volume losses in the absorber(s)"; the second method takes Qext from the complex eigenfrequency of the lossy eigenmode; if f0 ~ fl (Qext >= ~1e7) the lossy-method Qext values are "typically denoted as zeros" while the perturbation method still provides finite values; recommended to switch the cavity wall to PEC so only absorbers give Qext. — [SRF2015 THPB003](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/SRF2015/papers/thpb003.pdf)
- Direct complex eigenproblem in FIT: D_eps^{-1} C~ D_mu^{-1} C e = w^2 e with complex D_eps/D_mu (eps(w) = eps + sigma/(i w) in Clemens' slides), solved by JDQR; Q = Re(w)/(2 Im(w)) type definition (as in the MOPWO007 formula). — [Setzer & Weiland, LINAC98](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/l98/PAPERS/MO4041.PDF); [Clemens 2004](https://wias-berlin.de/events/insk/herbst04/clemens.pdf); [Liu et al. IPAC2013](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2013/papers/mopwo007.pdf)
- CST 2021: "General (Lossy)" solver gives total Q directly "without perturbation theory approximation" and allows dispersive materials, open boundaries, in-domain ports. — [3DS Community post](https://3dswym.3dexperience.3ds.com/post/simulia-community/eigenmode-solver-new-features-2021_cksixj8uRYy8gV0Xd8vDYg)
- CST 2011 note: eigenmode solver is for resonators and for periodic devices where "phase-diagrams can be derived from unit cells" — i.e., periodic boundary conditions with phase shift between master/slave faces. — [Electronic Specifier 2011](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=114337)
- Caveat about TEMF-side lossy FEM: their complex-valued FEM solver uses port boundary conditions via 2D modal expansion with iterative frequency evaluation (nonlinear in w) rather than absorbers. — [Liu et al., IPAC2013](https://accelconf.web.cern.ch/IPAC2013/papers/mopwo007.pdf)

### Inferences
- For wall-loss Q of a PEC-based comparison (this project's G, R/Q, R_sh via lossless fields) the relevant CST quantity is the perturbation Q0 from the lossless mode, so the systematic differences vs. a high-order FEM label are those of the fields near the wall (Q0 integrates |H_t|^2 over the wall): FIT's wall-adjacent magnetic field is evaluated on the dual grid (defective over ~70 um at R=100 mm per MOADC2), so Q0 errors are expected to exceed frequency errors; magnitude per se is not quantified in the sources.
- Phase-shifted periodic eigenproblems lead to complex Hermitian matrices (K(k) with e^{i k L} phase factors on the periodic edges); how AKS handles this (complex-Hermitian Krylov) is not documented; per the "no losses" note it is presumably restricted to lossless (Hermitian) problems.
