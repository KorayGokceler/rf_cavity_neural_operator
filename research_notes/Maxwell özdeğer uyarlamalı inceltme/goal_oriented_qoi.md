Scope note: notes for the report writer. Notation: K, M are the curl-curl and mass forms, a(u,v) = (curl u, curl v), m(u,v) = (u,v), λ = k² (f = c0 √λ / 2π), (u_h, λ_h) is the discrete eigenpair on the current mesh. "Inference" bullets are my own derivations or readings and are not stated as such by the sources. Sources were read in full text unless marked "(abstract/search snippet only)".

## 1. DWR for the eigenvalue itself (Heuveline–Rannacher, Becker–Rannacher, Rannacher–Westenberger–Wollner): error representation, localisation, and what it means for a frequency-driven indicator

### Takeaway
For a simple eigenvalue of a self-adjoint problem the DWR dual solution is the eigenfunction itself. The eigenvalue error is then exactly a residual of (u_h, λ_h) tested against the eigenfunction error, up to a factor (1 − σ) with σ = ½‖u − u_h‖². Localisation uses integration by parts: element residuals curl curl u_h − λ_h u_h plus tangential jumps of curl u_h, weighted by (u − Π_h u) element by element. The weight is computed from an enriched solution, e.g. order p+1. An indicator of this kind is a sharpened, sign-aware energy-norm indicator. It targets only the eigenvalue and does not ask for anything about wall or axis quantities.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic anchor: V. Heuveline, R. Rannacher, "A posteriori error control for finite element approximations of elliptic eigenvalue problems", Adv. Comput. Math. 15 (2001) 107–138, DOI 10.1023/A:1014291224961. No open-access full text was found. The content below comes from secondary sources that review it. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1014291224961); [Heidelberg library record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/144045356X)
- The general framework is R. Becker, R. Rannacher, "An optimal control approach to a posteriori error estimation in finite element methods", Acta Numerica 10 (2001) 1–102, DOI 10.1017/S0962492901000010. The Zschiedrich et al. paper below summarises its idea: write J(E) − J(E_h) as a "trivial constrained optimisation problem" with a Lagrangian whose multiplier is the dual variable E*. The dual equation contains the linearisation j′(E) of the goal. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492901000010); [Zschiedrich et al., arXiv:physics/0702128, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- For eigenproblems the normalisation is added to the variational form, a(Φ,E) − σ b(Φ,E) + χ(b(E,E) − 1) = 0 for all (Φ, χ). This makes (E, σ) the unique solution of a nonlinear variational problem to which DWR applies. "The corresponding dual problem is derived in" Heuveline–Rannacher (2001). For any chosen goal functional "it is necessary to check the unique solvability of the dual problem and its discretization". — [Zschiedrich et al., arXiv:physics/0702128, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- Rannacher's own summary of the approach: "embed the eigenvalue approximation into the general framework of Galerkin methods for nonlinear variational equations for which the DWR method is already well developed". The error representations give a posteriori bounds that cover discretisation error and also linearisation error about an only approximately known base solution, as in hydrodynamic stability. — [Rannacher, "Adaptive FE eigenvalue computation with applications to hydrodynamic stability" (abstract)](https://cemat.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/gpgaldi/abs/rannacher.pdf)
- Gedicke's thesis reviews Heuveline–Rannacher "in a direct approach rather than in the non-linear setting" for possibly non-symmetric problems with primal and dual eigenfunctions u, u*. The DWR estimators come from an asymptotically sharp estimate for simple eigenvalues, |λ − λ_ℓ| ≤ C |Res_ℓ(u* − u*_ℓ) + Res*_ℓ(u − u_ℓ)|, where C → 1/(2|b(u,u*)|) as the mesh size goes to 0. In the self-adjoint case u* = u, so the eigenfunction is its own dual. — [Gedicke, "On the numerical analysis of eigenvalue problems", HU Berlin thesis, Ch. 1 & 6](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/7235eacd-fc73-4929-a3d7-7cb5f2a57bc9/download) ([record](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/17493))
- The same thesis explains how the weights are obtained. The heuristic from Becker–Rannacher says "one does not need to approximate the weights with higher accuracy than the size of the residual terms". In practice u and u* are replaced by a higher-order solution or a higher-order interpolation. DWR estimators "avoid any additional inequality, such as approximation properties with unknown constants". Residual-type estimators, by contrast, had efficiency indices that depended strongly on the convection coefficient. — [Gedicke thesis](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/bitstreams/7235eacd-fc73-4929-a3d7-7cb5f2a57bc9/download)
- Maxwell-specific form: Harmon & Notaroš, IEEE TAP 2022, DOI 10.1109/TAP.2022.3145473, a goal-oriented hp method for the 2-D Maxwell eigenproblem in H0(curl).
  - With L2-normalised eigenfunctions, J[U] − J[U_hp] = e_λ, and "the associated dual eigenvalue problem for considering an eigenvalue QoI is identical to the forward problem, i.e., (4) is self-adjoint".
  - The simplified error identity is e_λ(1 − σ_hp) = a(u_hp, u − ψ_hp) − λ_hp m(u_hp, u − ψ_hp), with σ_hp = ½ m(u − u_hp, u − u_hp). It holds for any ψ_hp ∈ B_hp by Galerkin orthogonality.
  - They take ψ_hp = Π^curl_hp u, the curl-conforming projection-based interpolant, and report that this sharpens the local contributions compared with, e.g., ψ = 0.
  — [Harmon & Notaroš, author version, Sec. II](https://atmos.colostate.edu/~notaros/Papers/TAP_Adjoints_Benchmark.pdf)
- Localisation in Harmon & Notaroš:
  - Integration by parts per cell gives a cell residual (curl curl u_hp − λ_hp u_hp, weighted by u − ψ_hp). It also gives face terms ⟨n × curl u_hp, u − ψ_hp⟩ from both neighbours, which become a jump of the tangential curl.
  - Because u − ψ_hp is tangentially continuous, the weight is evaluated once per face.
  - The refinement indicator is η_K = |η̃_K|. The signed sum Σ η̃_K gives a correction term that improves λ_hp.
  - Using u_hp itself as the weight gives zero by Galerkin orthogonality. They therefore use u_hp+ from the enriched space with every element order raised by one.
  - They say the analysis "extend[s] … to the 3-D Maxwell eigenvalue problem directly (assuming access to the prerequisite 3-D hp-refinement infrastructure)".
  — [Harmon & Notaroš](https://atmos.colostate.edu/~notaros/Papers/TAP_Adjoints_Benchmark.pdf)
- Harmon & Notaroš, results: with this indicator plus smoothness estimation for the h/p decision, hp-refinement reaches "extremely high accuracy rapidly even for eigenvalues of singular eigenfunctions", with exponential rates. They publish new reference eigenvalues that are more accurate than earlier benchmarks. The implementation is in deal.II. — [Harmon & Notaroš, abstract & Sec. IV](https://atmos.colostate.edu/~notaros/Papers/TAP_Adjoints_Benchmark.pdf)
- Rannacher, Westenberger, Wollner, "Adaptive finite element solution of eigenvalue problems: Balancing of discretization and iteration error", J. Numer. Math. 18 (2010), DOI 10.1515/jnum.2010.015. It extends DWR to balance the discretisation error against the algebraic error of an iterative (Krylov-space) eigensolver, so the eigensolver tolerance is tied to the mesh error. (abstract/search snippet only; the full text could not be downloaded) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jnum.2010.015)
- Theory without goal orientation for Maxwell: Boffi & Gastaldi prove "optimal convergence of a standard adaptive scheme based on edge finite elements" for the 3-D Maxwell eigenproblem. The scheme uses the residual indicators of Boffi–Gastaldi–Rodríguez–Šebestová, which are reliable and efficient. The proof goes through the equivalent mixed formulation, and the numerical results show optimal rate with respect to DOFs in 3-D. — [Boffi & Gastaldi, arXiv:1804.02377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02377)
- Larson (2000) is the classical a priori/a posteriori analysis of self-adjoint elliptic eigenproblems: SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 38, 608–625, DOI 10.1137/S0036142997320164. Bibliographic check only; not read. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142997320164)

### Inferences
- For a frequency target, the DWR indicator differs from a good energy-norm indicator in three ways. (i) It is signed, so the summed estimate also corrects λ_h. (ii) Its weight u − Π_h u is a local interpolation error and not a global constant. (iii) No extra dual solve is needed, because the dual is the eigenfunction. The cost is one enriched (p+1) solve, or a cheaper local patch or higher-order reconstruction, as Gedicke's thesis discusses. A recovery (ZZ) indicator of curl E and E is the cheap stand-in. Its square estimates ‖curl e‖² − λ‖e‖² = λ_h − λ. For conforming H(curl) Ritz this is the standard identity λ_h − λ = a(e,e) − λ m(e,e) with ‖u_h‖ = ‖u‖ = 1. The present `src/data_gen/adaptive.py` volume term is of this type.
- With the project's Ritz upper-bound invariant (θ_k ≥ λ_h,k ≥ λ_k), the global signed DWR estimate of λ_h − λ should be positive. A negative global sum is a diagnostic that the enriched weight is under-resolved. The relative frequency error is half the relative λ error, so a 1e-6 frequency target corresponds to 2e-6 in λ.
- A frequency-only indicator refines where the eigenvalue error density is largest, i.e. the energy-weighted error, mostly in the cell volume and at high-curvature wall regions. Nothing in it forces resolution of the tangential H at the wall (Q0, G, Bpk) or of the longitudinal E along the axis (V, R/Q) beyond what the energy needs. This is consistent with the SLAC and Zschiedrich observations in Section 4 that energy-based refinement can leave loss quantities unconverged on the first few levels.
- For degenerate or clustered modes (dipole pairs, passbands of multi-cell cavities), the eigenvalue DWR has to be formulated for the cluster (see Section 5). A single-mode DWR on a nearly degenerate pair is unstable, because the factor C ~ 1/(2|b(u,u*)|) and the eigenfunction identity both assume a simple eigenvalue.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of Heuveline–Rannacher (2001) or Rannacher–Westenberger–Wollner (2010), so their exact formulas (the full dual system for eigenfunction functionals and the iteration-error term) are not quoted from the primary source. Here they are reconstructed from Zschiedrich et al., Gedicke and Harmon–Notaroš.
- I found no published 3-D H(curl) DWR implementation for cavity eigenvalues with reported efficiency indices on accelerator geometries. Harmon–Notaroš is 2-D, and the SLAC work uses energy-gradient or ZZ indicators.

## 2. Goal-oriented adaptivity for functionals of the eigenfunction (wall loss → Q0, G; axis voltage → R/Q, T): which dual problem, and is it a cheap shifted linear solve?

### Takeaway
For a goal J(u) of the eigenfunction, the DWR dual problem is the adjoint of the linearised eigenproblem including the normalisation constraint. It is a single linear solve with the shifted operator (K − λ_h M), restricted to the M-orthogonal complement of u_h, so it is a singular but consistent system. Its right-hand side is the derivative of the goal. Two complications are specific to cavities. First, wall-loss and axis-voltage functionals are not bounded on H(curl), because they need tangential traces of curl E on the wall or a line trace of E_z. They have to be regularised, for example extended into a thin volume layer, as Zschiedrich et al. did for a Maxwell loss functional. Second, the shifted operator is indefinite for any mode above the first. The one published Maxwell example shows that loss-targeted refinement beats energy-based refinement mainly in the pre-asymptotic range.

### Cited Findings
- In the Lagrangian/optimal-control view, the dual equation is a(Φ, E*) = j′(E; Φ) for all Φ, and its discrete version is a(E*_h, Φ_h) = j′(E_h; Φ_h). The primal and dual residuals ρ, ρ* then give bounds of the form |j(E − E_h)| ≤ C Σ_T ‖ρ‖_T ‖ρ*‖_T. For an eigenproblem with the normalisation term, the dual comes from Heuveline–Rannacher, and its solvability must be checked for each goal. — [Zschiedrich, Burger, Pomplun, Schmidt (ZIB/JCMwave), Proc. SPIE 6475 (2007), arXiv:physics/0702128](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- In the same paper the loss functional involves derivatives of E, of the form ℑ∫_Γ Φ × μ⁻¹ ∇×Φ · n, and "is not defined on the whole space H(curl). A regularization of the functional is required", done "by an appropriate extension of the boundary integral expression into a volume integral around the boundary". In the implementation ∇×Φ is replaced by ∇×E_h. "The so modified functional is unchanged when applied to the solution field E." — [Zschiedrich et al., Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- In the same paper, the imaginary part of the eigenvalue is, up to normalisation, proportional to the losses, so "construction of a goal orientated error estimator for the computation of the quality factor of a cavity should start from" that loss functional. — [Zschiedrich et al., Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- Numerical result: a leaky-waveguide eigenmode with 3rd-order elements, comparing uniform, energy-norm adaptive and loss-functional adaptive refinement. "As expected with the loss based mesh adaption … the imaginary part … converges faster than with the energy based mesh adaption, especially for lower accuracy demands". With the energy estimator "the imaginary part is not improved in the first three refinement steps". "For high accuracy demands they asymptotically show nearby the same behavior". The loss-based run reaches 4 digits in the imaginary part in under 10 s on a PC. The reference is n_eff = 2.4123720 + 2.91348e-8 i. — [Zschiedrich et al., Sec. 6, Tables 1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0702128)
- Goal-oriented estimation of point values and other local functionals with a regularised (mollified) dual problem is a classical technique: Prudhomme & Oden, "On goal-oriented error estimation for elliptic problems: application to the control of pointwise errors", CMAME 176 (1999) 313–331, DOI 10.1016/S0045-7825(98)00343-0. Bibliographic check only. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0045-7825(98)00343-0)
- The goal error is bounded by the product of the primal and dual errors. "This product allows, roughly speaking, doubled convergence rates compared to the best possible approximation of the PDE solution itself". The first optimal-rate result for goal-oriented AFEM is Mommer–Stevenson for Poisson. Giles & Süli, Acta Numerica 11 (2002) 145–236, DOI 10.1017/S096249290200003X, is the standard reference on the duality argument. — [Becker, Brunner, Hilbert, Innerberger, Praetorius, arXiv:2601.01965 (Jan 2026), Sec. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01965); [Giles & Süli DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096249290200003X)
- Industrial precedent: SLAC/RPI added AMR to Omega3P and S3P "to improve convergence on wall loss calculations". The local indicator was "the local integral of the gradient of the stored energy" (energy density U = ε|E|²/2 + μ|H|²/2), and later "the ZZ error metric on both E and H fields alternatively". This is a heuristic loss-oriented indicator, not a DWR dual. — [Ge, Lee, Li, Ng, Rahman, Sun, Ko (SLAC), Luo, Shephard (RPI), "Accurate wall loss determination in accelerator design", SLAC-PUB-10449 / PAC2003](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-6/6f1ab226c1f92f358546c3b24013a673)

### Inferences
- **Dual problem for a functional of a simple eigenpair.** This is my derivation from the Lagrangian with normalisation m(u,u) = 1, consistent with Zschiedrich's form and with Heuveline–Rannacher as cited there.
  - Find (z, ζ) such that a(φ, z) − λ m(φ, z) + 2ζ m(u, φ) = J′(u)φ for all φ, together with m(u, z) = 0. Testing with φ = u gives ζ = J′(u)u / 2.
  - So z solves (K − λM) z = g − (g·u) M u with z ⊥_M u, where g = J′(u). This is one linear solve with the shifted operator on the complement of u. The system is singular at λ, but the projected right-hand side satisfies the compatibility condition (Fredholm alternative).
  - On the discrete level, λ_h is an exact eigenvalue of the discrete pencil, so the discrete system is exactly singular. It needs deflation or projection against u_h: for example MINRES or GMRES with the projector I − u_h u_hᵀM, or a bordered (saddle-point) system [K − λ_h M, M u_h; u_hᵀM, 0].
  - The system is indefinite for every mode above the lowest, so CG does not apply directly.
  - In H(curl) the gradient kernel is harmless here: on gradients, K − λM = −λM is nonsingular. Even so, the dual inherits spurious gradient components unless g is divergence-compatible. Projecting out gradients, as the model already does with Kp, keeps the dual in the physical subspace.
- **Scale-invariant figures of merit make the dual simpler.** Q0 = ωU/P, G = R_s Q0, R/Q = |V|²/(ωU), T and Epk/Eacc, Bpk/Eacc are all homogeneous of degree 0 in the eigenvector, so J′(u)u = 0. Then ζ = 0 and the right-hand side g = J′(u) is already compatible. The dual is just (K − λM) z = J′(u), z ⊥_M u.
  - For a ratio J = N/D, J′φ = (N′φ − J D′φ)/D. Example: Q0 ∝ λ^{1/2} m(u,u) / ∫_wall |n × curl u|², so its derivative couples a volume mass term and a wall-trace term.
  - The eigenvalue dependence (ω in Q0 and R/Q) enters through the λ-row of the Lagrangian. It adds the term ∂J/∂λ · (λ − λ_h), which is a known, second-order-small quantity from Section 1.
- **Wall-loss functional (Q0, G).** P_wall = (R_s/2)∫_wall |H_t|² with H = curl E/(iωμ0). For E ∈ H(curl), curl E is only in L2 (in H(div) when curl curl E ∈ L2), so its tangential wall trace is not bounded. A goal-oriented method needs either a Zschiedrich-style volume-layer regularisation or an equivalent volume form, i.e. an extraction-type identity with a cut-off function. The resulting dual right-hand side is concentrated in a boundary layer. The dual z is therefore large near the high-H_t parts of the wall (equator for TM010), and DWR weights concentrate refinement there, together with the primal residual. This matches the SLAC practice of refining where wall currents concentrate.
- **Axis voltage (R/Q, T).** V = ∫_axis E_z(z) e^{i k z} dz is a line functional, which is unbounded on L2 in 3-D. Its regularisation (averaging over a small tube, or using the fact that E is smooth in the interior) gives a dual source along the axis, roughly a line current with a phase factor. The dual solution then behaves like the field of a line antenna and is singular near the axis. DWR therefore refines near the axis, but also wherever the primal residual pollutes the axis field, mainly the iris and nose regions in multi-cell or re-entrant cavities. R/Q = |V|²/(ωU) also needs the volume energy, which is a well-behaved (L2) term.
- **Cost estimate.** Per goal and per mode, one indefinite shifted solve of the same size as the eigenproblem. The eigensolver already factorises or preconditions (K − σM), so this is cheaper than the eigen-solve. Each mode has a different shift, though. With K modes and 4–6 figures of merit, this argues for combining the goals before solving (Section 5).

### Gaps
- I found no paper that formulates and tests DWR for wall-loss (Q0/G) or axis-voltage (R/Q, T) functionals of closed-cavity eigenmodes in 3-D H(curl). Zschiedrich et al. (leaky waveguide, loss functional) is the closest published Maxwell analogue.
- I found no source quantifying the efficiency index of a regularised wall-trace functional estimate for p ≥ 2 curved H(curl) elements.
- I did not find the exact Heuveline–Rannacher dual system in a readable primary source, so the derivation above is my own and should be checked against the paper.

## 3. Peak surface fields (Epk, Bpk): what the literature and accelerator practice do

### Takeaway
I found no goal-oriented a posteriori theory for maximum-norm boundary quantities of Maxwell eigenfunctions. The only rigorous max-norm (pointwise) a posteriori work for eigenproblems is a 2025 preprint for scalar elliptic problems: reliable up to a log factor, based on regularised derivative Green's functions. Accelerator practice is empirical:
- local mesh control in the peak region (iris for Epk, equator or coupler edges for Bpk);
- higher-order and curved surface elements (Omega3P quadratic surfaces, p up to 6; SLANS biquadratic elements);
- a separate convergence check on the peak fields, because they do not converge monotonically and can get worse with naive uniform densification.

Reported cross-code agreement is about 1 % on Bpk and 1–8 % on Epk.

### Cited Findings
- Pointwise (L∞) a posteriori theory for eigenproblems: Li, Liang, Xu propose a residual-type estimator for simple eigenvalues of second-order scalar elliptic eigenproblems and prove "reliability and efficiency … in the sense of L∞-norm, up to a logarithmic factor of the mesh size". The key ingredient is "new estimates for regularized derivative Green's functions". It is extended to nonconforming FE. It is not H(curl) and not a boundary maximum. — [Li, Liang, Xu, arXiv:2511.06815 (Nov 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.06815)
- Source-problem background: Nochetto (1995) derived pointwise a posteriori estimators for 2-D polygonal domains, based on a priori estimates for regularised Green's functions; later work extended this to 3-D. Hartmann & Grätsch (2006) split the pointwise-goal Green's function into a known fundamental solution and a regular FE-approximated part. (search snippets/abstracts only) — [Nochetto abstract](https://math.umd.edu/~rhn/papers/abstracts/linf.html); [Hartmann & Grätsch, Comput. Mech. 2006](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00466-005-0711-4)
- Early TESLA practice (URMEL era): "Epk/Eacc is strongly a function of NPMAX as well as mesh"; "The true Epk/Eacc must be calculated with 25000 Mesh points"; it was important "to keep the resonant frequency nearly equal to 1.3GHz in order to get the true Epk/Eacc". — [Zu & Chen, "Study on TESLA cavity shape", PAC1993](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p93/PDF/PAC1993_1095.PDF)
- Superfish practice: "considerably finer meshes are required near the nose of a drift tube to ensure accurate calculation of the resonant frequency and related secondary quantities". Merson & Boicourt evaluated frequency, shunt impedance Z, T, stored energy, Q and "the maximum electric field on the boundary" over mesh spacings 0.014–0.30 cm (410 to 124,576 points). The reference is the value the finest-mesh runs appear to converge to, because no closed form exists. — [Merson & Boicourt, "SUPERFISH accuracy dependence on mesh size", PAC1989 pp. 186–189](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p89/PDF/PAC1989_0186.PDF) (results figures partly unreadable in the scanned text)
- SLANS: "This code has better accuracy in comparison with earlier URMEL code used for calculation of TESLA cavities. With SLANS we can expect accuracy better than 0.1 %." This is a general statement, not tied to a stated quantity or mesh study. — [Shemelin et al., "An optimized shape cavity for TESLA: concept and fabrication", PAC2003 TPAB051](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p03/papers/TPAB051.pdf)
- 3-D FE (ANSYS) practice for SRF cavities, from Zaplatin:
  - "An acceptable mesh creation is an iterative process. All simulation results … are highly dependent on the mesh density". The usual procedure is "to create a fine mesh in critical areas on the surfaces", using sub-volumes with local sizes.
  - "the most important feature of the mesh adjustment is to control Epk/Eacc and Bpk/Eacc", and "it is not obvious that the more tense mesh corresponds to the better results"; a regular mesh "might be disadvantageous".

- Code comparison with convergence over refinement steps (HIE-ISOLDE QWR, beam ports removed):
  - CST with mesh refinement up to about 1e6 cells, HFSS with 2 µm surface refinement and about 110k tets, and Superfish at 2.5 mm, where "finer meshing does not change the results", taken as the reference.
  - The CST max E and max H converge over 9 refinement steps toward Superfish's H = 16.71 kA/m; HFSS gives 16.9 kA/m, about 1.1 % higher (my calculation).
  - With beam ports, Epk and Hpk stay consistent between HFSS and CST, but the CST Q0 "is overestimated, about 14000".

In their words: "manual refinement is inefficient and convergence is less than optimal, in particular for high wall loss in a narrow surface area". This was the motivation for AMR. — [Ge et al., SLAC-PUB-10449](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-6/6f1ab226c1f92f358546c3b24013a673)

By default HFSS refines about 30 % of the "bad" tets per pass. — [HFSS tutorial, 2018 Axion Cavity Workshop (FNAL)](https://indico.fnal.gov/event/17488/contributions/43435/attachments/26886/33319/MJones_-_HFSS_Tutorial_for_2018_Axion_Cavity_Workshop.pdf); [Ansys forum](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/forum/?p=32124); [Ansys HFSS course deck](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/courses/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/HFSS_GS_2020R2_EN_LE3_FEM_MeshSoln.pdf)

### Inferences
- Epk and Bpk are sup-norm functionals of traces: E_n for Epk, and H_t ∝ n × curl E for Bpk. They are not differentiable where the maximiser jumps between surface points, and the maximiser location moves with the mesh. These two facts explain the erratic, non-monotone convergence reported by Zaplatin and by Zu & Chen. A DWR treatment needs either:
  - (a) a smooth surrogate, e.g. the L^p-norm of |H_t| on the wall with large p, or a log-sum-exp, which is differentiable with a dual right-hand side concentrated near the maximiser; or
  - (b) a two-stage approach: find the maximiser x* on the current mesh, then use a regularised point functional at x* (Prudhomme–Oden style) as the goal, and re-detect x* each cycle.
  Neither is published for cavities (see Gaps).
- Smooth fillet geometries such as TESLA or elliptical cells have smooth fields, and the peaks sit on smooth parts of the wall. Peak fields then converge, but at most at the trace rate of the field, about h^p for H_t with order-p H(curl) elements and an exact or high-order geometry. This is slower than f (h^{2p}). Geometry error at the fillet (curvature representation) enters first order. Curved (isoparametric) elements of at least the field order are therefore required. This is consistent with SLAC's emphasis on quadratic or curved surfaces.
- Geometries with sharp re-entrant edges (unrounded coupler ports, slot edges) have field singularities: by the standard edge behaviour, |H| ~ r^{−1/3} at a 270° edge. The "peak field" there is not mesh-convergent at all and grows as h → 0. Practice rounds such edges or reports the peak on the rounded design. A sampler for peak values in a dataset should exclude or flag sharp-edge vertices. This is standard theory, not from a fetched source.
- Practical recipe consistent with the sources:
  - refine the peak regions explicitly: iris and nose for Epk, equator for Bpk in TM010, coupler and HOM-hook edges for Bpk;
  - raise the order and geometric order on the wall;
  - evaluate the maximum on a fixed dense surface sample (quadrature points of the wall facets), not on vertices;
  - declare convergence only when Epk and Bpk change by less than their own tolerance between two cycles, in addition to ΔF.
  The project's adaptive.py already uses ΔQoI-based stopping with "max peaks".

### Gaps
- I found no goal-oriented (DWR) or a posteriori max-norm method for boundary peaks of 3-D Maxwell eigenfunctions, nor any paper that tests an L^p-surrogate goal for Epk or Bpk.
- I found no publicly documented CST rule for checking surface-field convergence in the eigenmode solver. The CST studies found only show peak-field vs refinement-step plots (HIE-ISOLDE).
- A widely quoted "1 % on Bpk" accuracy is supported only indirectly: HFSS vs Superfish 1.1 % (QWR) and 1.3 % (ILC cell). Epk disagreements are larger (8.2 % in the ILC-cell comparison). I found no systematic study.

## 4. Convergence of frequency, Q0 (G), R/Q and peak fields with mesh refinement: published studies (TESLA, pillbox; SLANS, Superfish, ACE3P/Omega3P, high-order FEM)

### Takeaway
Frequency converges fastest. It is second order in the field error, and high-order curved FEM reaches 1e-5 relative or better at modest DOF counts: 20 kHz out of 1.3 GHz with 67k quadratic tets in Omega3P, and 0.01 % frequency requirements met for the ILC and NLC structures. Q0 (wall loss) converges about an order of magnitude more slowly in the same runs, which is why SLAC introduced AMR for wall loss. In SLAC's DLWG example the Q error axis spans about 10× the frequency error axis. Peak fields converge more slowly still and not monotonically. I found no published, systematic error-vs-DOF study that covers f, R/Q, G, Epk/Eacc and Bpk/Eacc together for a TESLA cell with high-order FEM. Available numbers are scattered across code-specific reports.

### Cited Findings
- Omega3P on the ILC low-loss end group: the frequency error (%) vs memory for linear, quadratic and cubic elements shows "required memory is significantly less for higher order elements". "For an error of 20 kHz error out of 1.3 GHz, 67,000 quadratic elements were used … less than 1 minute to run on 16 CPUs requiring 6 GB memory" (about 1.5e-5 relative). The NLC DDS required "accuracy of 0.01% in the accelerating frequency", which was met with Omega3P. — [Ng et al., "State of the art in EM field computation", EPAC2006 THXFI01](https://proceedings.jacow.org/e06/PAPERS/THXFI01.PDF); [Kabel et al., ICAP2006 talk](https://epaper.kek.jp/icap06/TALKS/WEA4IS01_TALK.PDF)
- ILC cavity requirement: modelled "to 0.01 % accuracy in frequency so that tuning in the fabrication process can be avoided". Microwave QC verified "cavity frequency accuracy to 0.01% relative error (1 MHz out of 11 GHz)". The same paper shows a figure of frequency (top) and Q (bottom) convergence over AMR levels, numbers not extractable. — [Ko et al., LINAC2010 FR101](https://jacow.org/LINAC2010/papers/FR101.pdf)
- Wall-loss convergence, SLAC/RPI:
  - Trispal 4-petal cavity, manual refinement with Omega3P quadratic elements, 0–4 M DOF. From the axis ticks, frequency spans about 1.06598–1.06610 GHz (about 1e-4 relative) while Q spans about 12100–12300 (about 1.6 %).
  - DLWG with ZZ-driven AMR: dF/F within 0 to −0.35 % but dQ/Q within 0 to −3.5 % over about 14k elements (axis ranges read from Fig. 10, approximate).
  - Q vs experiment (Trispal π/0 modes): MAFIA −22.3 %/−0.2 %, MWS −6.8 %/11.0 %, Omega3P −19.6 %/3.4 %. The table lists Omega3P π-mode Q as "1211", apparently a typo for 12111.
  - "AMR uses much less number of DOFs to achieve convergence" than manual refinement.
  — [Ge et al., SLAC-PUB-10449, PAC2003](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-6/6f1ab226c1f92f358546c3b24013a673)
- SLAC design-note summary (RIA RFQ): adaptive refinement improved frequency accuracy by about 10× and wall-loss (Q) accuracy by about 2× at a fraction of the CPU time of the non-adaptive run. (search snippet only; not verified in a primary paper) — [SLAC Confluence note](https://confluence.slac.stanford.edu/x/MIKuD)
- Analytic cavities with 2nd-order curved FEM (COMSOL RF example), f and Q vs h_max:
  - TM010 cylinder, analytic 9.412 GHz / Q 8065: h_max gives 9.458 / 7891; h/2 gives 9.419 / 8004; h/4 gives 9.411 / 8056; h/8 gives 9.411 / 8065.
  - Spherical TM011, analytic 9.698 GHz / Q 14594: 9.752 / 14121 → 9.697 / 14641, which overshoots Q.
  - Convergence is "fastest for the rectangular cavity and slowest for the spherical cavity", i.e. the geometry approximation matters. As recorded in the sibling CST note.
  — [COMSOL, "Computing Q-factors and resonant frequencies of cavity resonators"](https://doc.comsol.com/6.0/doc/com.comsol.help.models.rf.cavity_resonators/models.rf.cavity_resonators.pdf)
- Cross-solver eigenmode convergence (superconducting-circuit 3-D cavity; Palace AMR O2, Palace manual O4, HFSS AMR O2, COMSOL O2):
  - Palace AMR uses "an error indicator that estimates the local deviation between the numerically computed electric field within an element and a smoothed field value", i.e. recovery/ZZ.
  - Final frequencies differ by 0.020 % (COMSOL) and 0.110 % (HFSS) from Palace AMR. Convergence is plotted vs DoF^{-1/3}.
  — [Sommers et al., arXiv:2511.01220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01220)
- TESLA-specific, high-order: IGA (exact NURBS geometry) is used for TESLA cavities because it gives "an exact handling of the domains and their deformations" with "accurate and highly regular solutions". The reports found give frequencies and UQ, not R/Q, G or peak-field convergence. — [Corno, de Falco, De Gersem, Schöps, arXiv:1711.01828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.01828)
- SLANS: an accuracy "better than 0.1 %" is claimed for TESLA shape optimisation (unspecified quantity). — [Shemelin et al., PAC2003](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p03/papers/TPAB051.pdf)
- Superfish: mesh-size rules were derived empirically for frequency, Z, T, U, Q and boundary Emax. The nose and gap regions need much finer meshes. — [Merson & Boicourt, PAC1989](https://proceedings.jacow.org/p89/PDF/PAC1989_0186.PDF)
- Wall-loss accuracy has practical consequences: "the wall loss leads to shunt impedance degradation and … excessive wall loss results in undesirable RF pulse heating", and "tighter frequency and Q accuracy would mean fewer tuners and better cooling design" (the latter from the design-note summary). — [Ge et al., SLAC-PUB-10449](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-6/6f1ab226c1f92f358546c3b24013a673)

### Inferences
- **Expected asymptotic rates** for order-p H(curl) elements (NGSolve order p, full-degree curl), smooth eigenfunctions, and geometry of order at least p. This is standard a priori theory combined with the "product of errors" statement in Becker et al. (2026).
  - λ and f: O(h^{2p}).
  - Volume energy U: O(h^{2p}).
  - Wall loss P and Q0 = ωU/P computed directly from the trace of curl E_h: the trace error is O(h^p) in L2(∂Ω). The squared-norm functional ∫|H_t|² has error ≈ 2 Re∫ H_t·δH_t + O(δ²). The first-order term does not cancel because there is no Galerkin orthogonality for this functional, so expect O(h^p), possibly up to O(h^{p+½}) from boundary-layer effects.
  - The axis voltage V is an interior line functional where E is smooth. Expect between O(h^{p+1}) (L2-type) and O(h^{2p}) (adjoint-consistent), depending on pollution.
  - Peak fields: O(h^p) at best, with extra log factors and non-monotone behaviour.
  - With a DWR-corrected value J(u_h) + η, both Q0 and V can in principle reach O(h^{2p}), which is the doubling result.
- **Example targets.** A 1e-6 frequency target needs a coarser mesh than a 1e-3 Q0 target when Q0 converges at O(h^p) and f at O(h^{2p}). In DOF terms, with N ~ h^{-3}: f error ~ N^{-2p/3} and Q0 error ~ N^{-p/3}. For p = 3, f ~ N^{-2} and Q0 ~ N^{-1}. Hence the project's 1e-3/1e-6 target pair (`adaptive.py`) is consistent, and both errors are near 1e-6 in squared or relative form, as the module docstring argues.
- The observed SLAC ratio (dQ/Q about 10× dF/F at equal elements) and the COMSOL table (f converged to 4 digits at h/4 while Q still moved 0.1 % from h/4 to h/8) agree with this qualitative ordering, f ≪ Q0 < peaks.

### Gaps
- No published systematic error-vs-DOF study was found for a TESLA cell (or pillbox) covering f, R/Q, G, Epk/Eacc and Bpk/Eacc together with SLANS, Superfish, ACE3P/Omega3P or high-order FEM. Superfish (Merson–Boicourt 1989) is the closest, for DTL, SCL and RFQ geometries, and its scanned figures are not readable.
- Measured convergence orders (slopes) for Q0 or peak fields vs DOF with p ≥ 2 curved H(curl) elements were not found in the literature. The project's own pillbox measurements (docs/30) may be the best available data.
- SLAC-PUB-12984 (Omega3P description) could not be fetched (TLS chain issue on slac.stanford.edu), so its convergence data, if any, is not included.

## 5. Multi-objective adaptivity: several modes and several outputs at once (union or weighted sum, per-mode normalisation)

### Takeaway
The numerical-analysis standard is a single combined goal: J_c = Σ_i w_i sign(J_i(ũ) − J_i(u_h)) J_i, with w_i = 1/|J_i(ũ)| so every goal is controlled in relative error. The signs avoid error cancellation and are estimated from an enriched solution ũ. Only one combined dual solve is needed per level. A 2026 analysis gives optimal rates without sign knowledge by cycling through the duals in the marking step. For eigenmodes, clusters and degenerate pairs must be treated together, because indicators built from a subset of the cluster's discrete modes can stall convergence. Commercial and industrial practice is a weighted sum of per-mode indicators (COMSOL: user weights, default equal), plus stopping on several quantities (HFSS ΔF plus expression cache).

### Cited Findings
- Combined goal in Endtmayer's checkerboard paper, following Endtmayer–Langer–Wick: J_c(v) = Σ_{i=1}^{N} sign(J_i(ũ) − J_i(u_h)) / |J_i(ũ)| · J_i(v). Here ũ is an enriched or higher-order approximation. "This choice should avoid error cancellation." Weighting by 1/|J_i| leads to a similar relative error in all functionals. — [Endtmayer, arXiv:2405.18567](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18567)
- State of the art (2026):
  - Earlier multigoal works (Hartmann–Houston 2003, Hartmann 2008, Endtmayer–Langer–Wick 2019/2020) need "only two problem solves per mesh level … the primal problem and one suitably combined multigoal dual problem".
  - The combination "requires knowledge of the sign of each goal-error difference … which is not available in practice". ELW19 recover the signs from an enriched solution under a strong saturation assumption.
  - The new NGO-AFEM "cycl[es] through all dual problems with an appropriately modified MARK module, while still solving only two problems" per step. It proves linear convergence and optimal rates s + t, where t = min_j t_j is the worst dual rate. Scope: symmetric linear elliptic PDE with linear goals.
  — [Becker, Brunner, Hilbert, Innerberger, Praetorius, arXiv:2601.01965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01965)
- References for the multi-target line:
  - Hartmann & Houston, "Goal-oriented a posteriori error estimation for multiple target functionals" (2003), DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-55711-8_54.
  - Hartmann, "Multitarget error estimation and adaptivity in aerodynamic flow simulations", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 31 (2008) 708–731, DOI 10.1137/070710962.
  - Endtmayer, Langer, Wick, "Multigoal-oriented error estimates for non-linear problems", J. Numer. Math. 27 (2019) 215–236, DOI 10.1515/jnma-2018-0038.
  — [Hartmann–Houston DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-55711-8_54); [Hartmann DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/070710962); [ELW DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jnma-2018-0038)
- Eigenvalue clusters: "degeneracy of the convergence may be observed when the error indicator is computed by taking into account only a subset of the discrete eigenmodes approximating the eigensolutions we are interested in (multiple or belonging to a cluster)". The cluster theory is in Boffi–Gallistl–Gardini–Gastaldi, "Optimal convergence of adaptive FEM for eigenvalue clusters in mixed form", Math. Comp. 86 (2017) 2213–2237, DOI 10.1090/mcom/3212. — [Boffi & Gastaldi, arXiv:1804.02377](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02377); [BGGG DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3212)
- COMSOL eigenvalue adaptation: with Solution selection "All" (default for eigenvalue studies) or "Manual", the "Weights" field takes positive relative weights per eigenmode. The error estimate is a weighted sum over the selected eigenmodes (default weight 1 each; omitted weights count as zero). Element selection is "Rough global minimum" (element-count growth factor 1.7), "Fraction of worst error" (0.5) or "Fraction of elements" (0.5). The default refinement is "Longest edge refinement". The page does not state the estimator's norm or formula. — [COMSOL 6.3 Reference Manual, Adaptive Mesh Refinement (Stationary and Eigenvalue Adaptation)](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_solver.36.157.html)
- SLAC alternated the ZZ indicator between E and H fields ("using the ZZ error metric on both E and H fields alternatively"), a simple union of two field indicators aimed at both frequency and wall loss. — [Ge et al., SLAC-PUB-10449](https://s3.cern.ch/inspire-prod-files-6/6f1ab226c1f92f358546c3b24013a673)
- HFSS eigenmode adaptivity converges on frequency change per pass (multiple modes). Additional output-variable criteria can be imposed through the Expression Cache; the evidence is a forum post about the driven solver. — [HFSS tutorial (FNAL 2018)](https://indico.fnal.gov/event/17488/contributions/43435/attachments/26886/33319/MJones_-_HFSS_Tutorial_for_2018_Axion_Cavity_Workshop.pdf); [Ansys forum](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/forum/?p=32124)

### Inferences
- **Normalising per mode for eigenvalues.** The eigenvalue DWR of mode k estimates λ_h,k − λ_k. The relative frequency error is (λ_h,k − λ_k)/(2λ_k). Dividing each mode's indicator by λ_k makes the modes comparable in relative terms. This is the weight 1/|J_i| rule with J_i = λ_i, and it is what the project's η_vol does, with unit-L2 modes and division by λ_k. Without it, higher modes dominate, since their absolute error is larger.
- **Weights are signed per goal.** Ritz gives λ_h ≥ λ, so for eigenvalue goals the sign is known: all positive. No cancellation occurs between eigenvalue goals in a combined J_c. Q0, R/Q and the peaks have unknown signs, so Endtmayer–Langer–Wick sign recovery (enriched solution) or Becker et al.'s cycling marking is needed when combining them.
- **Union vs weighted sum vs max.** Any of three combinations is defensible at low cost:
  - (a) a weighted sum of squared relative indicators, η_T² = Σ_k Σ_i w_{k,i} η_{T,k,i}², which is the current project choice;
  - (b) "max-marking", i.e. Dörfler-mark the union of the element sets each goal would mark;
  - (c) Becker et al.'s scheme: each cycle, mark for the goal with the currently largest estimated error.
  Option (c) has the only optimality proof (for linear elliptic problems). Option (a) can starve a goal whose indicator is small in absolute terms but whose target tolerance is tight. Scaling each indicator by 1/tol_i (relative error / tolerance) fixes this. That is a weight w_i = 1/(|J_i| tol_i), a direct generalisation of the 1/|J_i| rule.
- **Clusters.** For degenerate dipole pairs and passband modes, sum the indicator over the whole cluster with cluster-invariant goals (mean f, Σ R/Q, max peaks, as adaptive.py does). This follows the Boffi–Gastaldi warning about subsets of a cluster.
- **Dual cost with K modes and G goals.** Combining the goals per mode (J_c,k) needs K dual solves, one per mode, because each mode has its own shift λ_k. Combining across modes in a single dual is impossible, since the shifted operators differ. A reasonable compromise is goal-oriented duals for the accelerating mode only (Q0, R/Q, peaks matter there) plus energy or eigenvalue indicators for the other modes.

### Gaps
- I found no multi-goal (several figures of merit × several modes) adaptive study for cavity eigenproblems. All multigoal theory found is for source problems: linear and nonlinear elliptic, or flow.
- The COMSOL page gives no formula for its per-mode error estimate (norm, eigenvalue scaling). CST's eigenmode adaptive-refinement criteria were not found in public documentation (see the sibling CST notes).
- I found no evidence on whether the Endtmayer–Langer–Wick sign recovery remains reliable for the indefinite shifted dual problems of higher eigenmodes.
