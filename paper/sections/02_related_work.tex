\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Neural operators.}
Neural operators learn maps between function spaces: DeepONet splits the approximation into a branch and a trunk network~\citep{lu2021learning}, the Fourier neural operator (FNO) parametrises an integral kernel in Fourier space on regular grids~\citep{li2021fourier}, and \citet{kovachki2023neural} give a general framework.
Complex domains are handled by a learned deformation onto a uniform grid (Geo-FNO)~\citep{li2023fourier}, by graph--Fourier coupling through a signed-distance representation (GINO)~\citep{li2023geometry}, or by message passing on the mesh~\citep{pfaff2021learning}.
Transformer operators use a linear-complexity attention~\citep{katharopoulos2020transformers}: the Galerkin transformer reads it as a Petrov--Galerkin projection~\citep{cao2021choose}, GNOT adds heterogeneous normalised attention and geometric gating for irregular meshes~\citep{hao2023gnot}, and Transolver attends over learned slices of mesh points~\citep{wu2024transolver}.
Our trunk belongs to this last family, with quadrature weights playing the role of the mass.
Evaluating a trained operator on another discretisation is a stated feature of FNO-type models~\citep{li2021fourier}, and aliasing between discretisations has been analysed~\citep{bartolucci2023representation}; here the discretisation is a conforming finite-element space, and the Ritz structure is kept across spaces (\cref{sec:method}).
These models regress the solution field, or a scalar functional of it, at the mesh points.
Ours emits a \emph{basis} of $m$ vector fields; fields and frequencies are produced by a finite-element Rayleigh--Ritz step, so the output inherits the structure of the discrete problem.

\paragraph{Learning eigenvalue problems and subspaces.}
Neural eigensolvers parametrise eigenfunctions and minimise a variational or fixed-point objective for \emph{one} operator: spectral inference networks~\citep{pfau2019spectral}, NeuralEF for kernels~\citep{deng2022neuralef}, the Deep Ritz method~\citep{e2018deep}, a semigroup fixed-point loss in high dimension~\citep{han2020solving}, and unsupervised learning of the lowest eigenpairs of self-adjoint operators with an error bound~\citep{benshaul2023deep}.
Closer to our setting, Deep Eigenspace Network learns the eigen\emph{subspace} of a parametric non-self-adjoint problem from geometry-adaptive POD bases and a Fourier neural operator~\citep{li2026deep}, and FEENet predicts coefficients in a finite-element eigenfunction basis computed per geometry~\citep{li2026finite}.
The classical analogue of a Ritz step in a parameter-adapted subspace is the reduced-basis method: Rayleigh--Ritz in the span of snapshot eigenvectors, with output bounds for symmetric eigenvalue problems~\citep{machiels2000output}.
For Maxwell cavities on parameter-dependent domains the reduced space must be cleansed of spurious gradient modes, which has been done with tree--cotree gauges~\citep{kappesser2023reduced,ziegler2024mixed}.
We keep the Ritz step but replace snapshots by a basis predicted from the geometry, and the gauge by the kernel projection.
A regressed parametric eigenspace can also hot-start LOBPCG~\citep{fanaskov2025deep,knyazev2001toward}, a use of our model we leave to future work.
For electromagnetic resonators, supervised networks map the parameters of dielectric-loaded cylindrical cavities to the resonant frequency and mode coefficients~\citep{lewis2022machine}, and deep networks regress photonic-crystal nanocavity quality factors from hole patterns~\citep{asano2019iterative}; both are parametric regressors for a fixed template.
A physics-informed network targets the lowest mode of a single 3D RF cavity with a soft divergence-free loss~\citep{shali2024dfsnet}.
We differ in three respects: the operator is amortised over a family of 3D geometries; the learned object is a basis that is projected onto the discretely divergence-free subspace and fed to Rayleigh--Ritz, so spurious zero modes are excluded by construction rather than penalised; and the Ritz values are upper bounds of the discrete eigenvalues (\cref{prop:upper-bound}).

\paragraph{Physics-informed, constrained and finite-element-coupled learning.}
Physics-informed networks add the PDE residual to the loss~\citep{raissi2019physics}, as does the physics-informed neural operator~\citep{li2024physics}; such constraints need not hold at inference.
Hard constraints can instead be built into the architecture: divergence-free networks via differential forms~\citep{richterpowell2022neural} and a differentiable PDE-constrained layer that selects an optimal combination of learned functions~\citep{negiar2023learning}.
Our projection layer is of this kind, specialised to the discrete curl--curl structure.
Other works couple learning to a mesh: hybrid FEM--NN models differentiate through a finite-element solve~\citep{mitusch2021hybrid}; FEONet predicts finite-element coefficients and trains on the weak-form residual~\citep{lee2025finite}; a data-driven exterior calculus keeps discrete conservation exact while learning the metric~\citep{trask2022enforcing}.
In electromagnetics, FNO surrogates have been trained for scattering and inverse design~\citep{augenstein2023neural}.
As far as we found, none of these learns the Maxwell eigenproblem on conforming high-order $H(\mathrm{curl})$ spaces on curved meshes, where the constraint is the kernel of the curl.

\paragraph{Maxwell eigenproblems and Nédélec elements.}
Nodal discretisations of the cavity problem produce spurious modes~\citep{bossavit1990solving}; curl-conforming elements~\citep{nedelec1980mixed} avoid them, and their analysis is treated in~\citep{boffi2000fortin,caorsi2001spurious}, in the monograph of~\citet{monk2003finite} and in the surveys of~\citet{hiptmair2002finite} and~\citet{boffi2010finite}.
We use hierarchical high-order bases with the local complete sequence property~\citep{schoberl2005high} and curved elements through NGSolve and Netgen~\citep{schoberl2014cpp11,schoberl1997netgen}.
The upper-bound property rests only on the min--max principle on the discrete space~\citep{babuska1991eigenvalue}: it orders predictions against the discrete eigenvalues of that space and says nothing about the exact ones.

\paragraph{RF cavity simulation and design.}
SUPERFISH solves axisymmetric cavities in 2D~\citep{halbach1976superfish}; CST Studio Suite is a widely used commercial 3D code; Omega3P of the ACE3P suite is a parallel higher-order Nédélec eigensolver~\citep{lee2009omega3p,ko2010advances}; Palace is an open-source finite-element code with eigenmode analysis~\citep{awslabs2023palace}.
Shape optimisation of superconducting cavities calls such solvers in a loop and reduces their cost by sensitivity analysis~\citep{kranjcevic2019constrained} or adjoint shape derivatives of Maxwell eigenvalues~\citep{ziegler2024gradient,herter2024numerical}; each design or gradient still costs a solve, which a trained model amortises over a family of shapes.
Machine learning in accelerator science has mostly produced surrogates of beam-dynamics simulations~\citep{edelen2020machine}; for cavities, a recent inverse-design network maps target observables to spline-parametrised multicell and cylindrical geometries~\citep{yaker2026neural}.
Our model instead predicts the modes themselves, with fields and derived figures of merit, for a geometry given by a mesh.

\paragraph{Multi-fidelity learning.}
Multi-fidelity methods combine few expensive and many cheap evaluations, from autoregressive Gaussian processes~\citep{kennedy2000predicting} (surveyed by \citet{peherstorfer2018survey}) to composite networks~\citep{meng2020composite} and multi-fidelity DeepONets, which learn a low-fidelity operator and a correction~\citep{lu2022multifidelity,howard2023multifidelity}.
Mixing many coarse-mesh with few fine-mesh samples has been studied for flow surrogates~\citep{song2021transfer}; neural operators are also trained on coarse data and applied at higher resolution~\citep{li2021fourier,li2024physics}.
In these works the low-fidelity data are an auxiliary signal that a second network corrects.
Here the low-fidelity label is the discrete eigenpair of the model's own space, which is what the Ritz layer approximates, so low-fidelity labels can serve as the main training target (\cref{sec:multifidelity}).
