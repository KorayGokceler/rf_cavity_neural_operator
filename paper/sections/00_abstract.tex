Predicting the resonant modes of three-dimensional radio-frequency (RF) cavities---their frequencies, electric
fields and figures of merit such as $\Qz$, $\RoQ$ and the peak surface fields---normally requires one
finite-element eigensolve per geometry. We present a neural operator that does not regress these quantities
directly. It maps the geometry, represented by the quadrature points of a curved tetrahedral mesh, to a learned
basis of vector fields; a fixed linear map turns the basis into degrees of freedom of a second-order N\'ed\'elec
space, a kernel projection removes the discrete gradients, and a Rayleigh--Ritz step returns the modes. In exact
arithmetic the predicted eigenvalues are upper bounds of the discrete eigenvalues of whichever conforming space
the model runs in, and no spurious zero modes arise. A single geometry can be fitted to a relative eigenvalue
error of $10^{-6}$--$9\times10^{-6}$. % src: docs/28 §3
We build an adaptive third-order label generator on curved meshes, validated against analytic pillbox modes
(relative frequency error $6\times10^{-7}$ at 157k degrees of freedom), % src: docs/31 §1
and show that low-fidelity labels---one second-order solve on the mesh the model uses---cost $2.8$--$4.3\times$
less than third-order labels on that mesh and differ from them by at most $0.047\,\%$ in frequency and
$0.32\,\%$ in field. % src: docs/34 §8.1
In a small single-run study on one cavity family, models trained on low-fidelity labels were comparable in
accuracy on held-out geometries to models trained on third-order labels of similar cost, and their errors
against the two label types differ by $0.02$ percentage points. Because the label difference is far smaller
than the present model error, the study does not yet probe the regime in which the label type matters. % src: docs/34 §8.2
