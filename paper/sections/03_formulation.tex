\section{Problem formulation and discretisation}\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{The cavity eigenproblem}\label{sec:03-eigenproblem}
% src: docs/20 §8, docs/19 §6.1, AGENTS.md
Let $\Omega\subset\R^3$ be a connected, bounded cavity with a perfectly conducting (PEC) wall $\bdry$ and vacuum
($\varepsilon_0,\mu_0$) inside. The resonances of the lossless cavity are the pairs $(\E,k)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-strong}
  \curl\curl\,\E = k^2\,\E \ \text{ in } \Omega,\qquad \mathbf n\times\E=\mathbf 0 \ \text{ on } \bdry,
  \qquad k=\omega/c_0 ,
\end{equation}
with $c_0=\SI{299792458}{m/s}$ % src: src/qoi/figures.py C0
and resonant frequency $f=\omega/2\pi$. In weak form, find $\E\in\Hcurl\setminus\{0\}$ and $\lambda=k^2$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-weak}
  (\curl\E,\curl\mathbf v)_\Omega=\lambda\,(\E,\mathbf v)_\Omega\qquad\forall\,\mathbf v\in\Hcurl ,
\end{equation}
the wall condition being essential. The curl--curl form is only semi-definite: its kernel in $\Hcurl$ is
$\nabla H^1_0(\Omega)\oplus\mathcal H$, with $\mathcal H$ the harmonic Dirichlet fields, of dimension $b_2$ = number of wall components beyond the first (one per floating conductor; the
gradient of a potential constant on that component). % src: docs/20 §8, docs/19 §6.1
Handles of $\Omega$ add nothing to this kernel in the $\E$ formulation, which is why we use $\E$ and not $\Hf$ (for $\Hf$ the harmonic fields
of handles are not gradients and cannot be projected out). % src: AGENTS.md invariants, docs/19 §6.1
The physical resonances are the positive eigenvalues $0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\dots$ with divergence-free eigenfields. For learning, the infinite kernel matters: any learned field contains
gradient components, which have zero energy but non-zero mass and give Rayleigh quotients near~$0$ (spurious modes) unless removed explicitly.

\subsection{Conforming discretisation on curved meshes}\label{sec:03-discrete}
% src: docs/28 §2, docs/29 §1, src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators / solve_modes
Let $\mathcal T_h$ be a tetrahedral mesh of a CAD model of the cavity \citep{schoberl1997netgen} whose elements are images
$F_T(\hat T)$ of the reference tetrahedron under polynomial maps of degree $q$ (the curve order; $q=3$ throughout, % src: docs/29 §1, field_labels.DEFAULTS curve=3
so the geometry is iso- or super-parametric for $p\le 3$). $\Vh$ is the conforming Nédélec space of the first kind \citep{nedelec1980mixed} on $\mathcal T_h$, mapped with the covariant Piola transform, with zero
tangential trace on $\bdry$ and the hierarchical basis of \citet{schoberl2005high}, as implemented in NGSolve \citep{schoberl2014cpp11}.
\emph{Order convention.} Throughout, $p$ is the implementation's order: $\Vh$ contains the complete vector polynomials of degree $p$, $p=0$ is the lowest-order (Whitney) element,
and the gradients of the $H^1$ space of order $p+1$ lie in $\Vh$. Thus ``$p=2$'' is not the element N2 of Nédélec's indexing; there are about $10.7$ ($p=2$) and $26.9$ ($p=3$) DOFs per tetrahedron (112k and 282k DOFs on 10.5k tets). % src: highorder.field_operators / solve_modes (fes.CreateGradient); checked: HCurl(order=p) gives an H1 space of order p+1 for p=1,2,3 on a curved mesh; DOFs: docs/28 §4 table
We use $p=2$ for the model space and $p=3$ for the reference labels. % src: docs/29 §1
Let $\Sh$ be the $H^1_0$-conforming space of order $p+1$ on the same mesh, extended by one function per additional wall component
(equal to $1$ there and $0$ on the other components), so that $\nabla\Sh\subset\Vh$ and $\curl\nabla\Sh=0$.
With $n$ free (non-wall) DOFs and basis $\{\mathbf w_i\}$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-matrices}
  \Kmat_{ij}=(\curl\mathbf w_j,\curl\mathbf w_i),\quad
  \Mmat_{ij}=(\mathbf w_j,\mathbf w_i),\quad
  \Kp=\Gmat^{\top}\Mmat\Gmat ,
\end{equation}
where $\Gmat$ is the matrix of $\nabla:\Sh\to\Vh$. $\Kmat$ is symmetric positive semi-definite, $\Mmat$ and $\Kp$ are symmetric positive definite, and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$, which holds pointwise at every quadrature point because
$\curl\nabla\varphi=0$. This gives $\operatorname{range}\Gmat\subset\ker\Kmat$; the rest of the paper uses the equality as an explicit assumption:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-A}
  \textbf{(A)}\qquad \ker\Kmat=\operatorname{range}\Gmat\quad\text{on }\Vh ,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\dim\ker\Kmat=\dim\Sh$ (counting the extra wall-component functions). (A) is the rank form of exactness of the discrete de~Rham sequence at $\Vh$. With the covariant Piola map it holds when $\Sh$ is mapped by the plain pull-back and the basis of $\Vh$ contains
the gradients of $\Sh$ exactly, as the complete-sequence basis does \citep{schoberl2005high,monk2003finite,arnold2006finite}; we do not prove it for the quadrature-defined forms on the curved elements used here.
We verified (A) numerically on 15 small curved-mesh cases (five meshes, $p=1,2,3$, among them a spherical shell with two wall components and a solid torus): in all of them $\dim\ker\Kmat=\operatorname{rank}\Gmat$ equals the number of interior $\Sh$ DOFs plus the extra wall potentials (\cref{tab:A04-rank}). % src: docs/35 §1
The Galerkin discretisation of \eqref{eq:03-weak} is the generalised eigenproblem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-gev}
  \Kmat\,\mathbf x=\lambda\,\Mmat\,\mathbf x ,
\end{equation}
whose null space is $\operatorname{range}\Gmat$. The physical part of the spectrum lives on the
\emph{discretely divergence-free} subspace
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-proj}
  Z_h=\{\mathbf v\in\Vh:\ (\mathbf v,\nabla\varphi)=0\ \ \forall\varphi\in\Sh\}
  =\operatorname{range}\Pmat,\qquad
  \Pmat=\mathsf I-\Gmat\,\Kp^{-1}\Gmat^{\top}\Mmat ,
\end{equation}
the $\Mmat$-orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{range}\Gmat$ ($\Pmat$ is the $\Mmat$-orthogonal projector onto it).
The \emph{discrete eigenvalues} $\lambda_{h,1}\le\lambda_{h,2}\le\dots$ are the positive eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:03-gev}, i.e.\ the
eigenvalues of $\Kmat$ restricted to $Z_h$. Besides $(\mathcal T_h,q,p)$ they depend on the explicit quadrature rule (degree $2p+4$, see below), since $\Kmat$ and $\Mmat$ are the quadrature-defined forms; (A) and $\Mmat\succ0$ are all that the bounds of \cref{sec:method} use. Because $Z_h$ is not contained in
the divergence-free fields, the $\lambda_{h,k}$ are not ordered with respect to the exact $\lambda_k$. They converge to $\lambda_k$ under refinement by discrete compactness \citep{monk2003finite,boffi2010finite}; bounds in this paper are relative to $\lambda_{h,k}$ of the space in which a computation runs, not to $\lambda_k$.

\paragraph{Normalisation and frequencies.}
% src: src/data/mesh_features.py normalise(); src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators; src/data/field_dataset.py check_labels
Operators are assembled on the normalised geometry $\boldsymbol\xi=(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)/s$, with $\mathbf c$ the volume centroid and $s$ the largest
absolute coordinate deviation of a vertex from $\mathbf c$. The eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:03-gev} are then $\lambda=s^2k^2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-freq}
  f_k=\frac{c_0\sqrt{\lambda_k}}{2\pi\,s}.
\end{equation}
The model, labels and losses of \cref{sec:method} all work in these normalised units (a stored label eigenvalue is $s^2k^2$). Eigenvectors are $\Mmat$-normalised, determined up to sign and, in a multiple eigenspace, up to an orthogonal transformation.

\paragraph{One integration rule for all forms.}
% src: docs/28 §2 numerical note; src/data_gen/highorder.py volume_measure, solve_modes
All forms ($\Kmat$, $\Mmat$, the stiffness of $\Sh$ that equals $\Kp$, shifted operators) are evaluated with one explicit tetrahedral rule of degree $2p+4$, % src: highorder.volume_measure(order, bonus=4)
because on curved elements NGSolve otherwise picks each form's rule from its own integrand and the discrete operators become mutually inconsistent (details in \cref{sec:A03-qoi}). With one rule the eigenvalues agree
with a dense solve of \eqref{eq:03-gev} to $10^{-13}$ % src: docs/28 §2
and $\Pmat$ is an $\Mmat$-orthogonal projector to rounding.

\subsection{Figures of merit}\label{sec:03-qoi}
% src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.1–§1.5; src/qoi/figures.py figures_of_merit, surface_resistance; src/data_gen/highorder.py mode_qoi
We use peak (not RMS) phasors $e^{j\omega t}$, so $\Hf=(j/\omega\mu_0)\curl\E$, and the lossless field with a small surface resistance $R_s$ for the
wall loss. Every quantity is reported for the mode scaled to stored energy $U=\SI{1}{J}$ (as CST does), so amplitude and sign of $\E$ drop out:
\begin{align}
  U&=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\!\int_\Omega|\E|^2\,dV=\tfrac12\mu_0\!\int_\Omega|\Hf|^2\,dV, &
  P_c&=\tfrac12R_s\!\oint_{\bdry}|\mathbf n\times\Hf|^2\,dS, \notag\\
  \Qz&=\frac{\omega U}{P_c}, &
  G&=\Qz R_s=\frac{\omega\mu_0\int|\Hf|^2dV}{\oint|\mathbf n\times\Hf|^2dS}, \label{eq:03-q}\\
  V_{\mathrm{acc}}&=\Bigl|\int_\ell \E\!\cdot\!\mathbf d\;e^{\,j\omega t/(\beta c_0)}\,dt\Bigr|, &
  T_{\mathrm{tr}}&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}}{\int_\ell|\E\!\cdot\!\mathbf d|\,dt}, \notag\\
  \RoQ&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}{\omega U}, &
  R_{\mathrm{sh}}&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}{P_c}=\RoQ\,\Qz, \label{eq:03-rq}\\
  \Epk&=\max_{\bdry}|\E|, &
  \Bpk&=\mu_0\max_{\bdry}|\Hf|=\max_{\bdry}\frac{|\curl\E|}{\omega}. \notag
\end{align}
Here $R_s=\sqrt{\pi f\mu_0/\sigma}$ is the skin-effect resistance at the mode's own frequency ($\sigma=\SI{5.8e7}{S/m}$, copper); $\Qz\propto1/R_s$, while $G$, $\RoQ$, $T_{\mathrm{tr}}$
and the peak ratios do not depend on the wall material. The beam axis $\ell$ has unit tangent $\mathbf d$ and arc length $t$, $\beta=1$, and $\Eacc=V_{\mathrm{acc}}/\Lacc$. % src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.1
We use the \emph{linac} convention for $\RoQ$ and $R_{\mathrm{sh}}$ \citep{wangler2008rf,padamsee2008rf}; the circuit convention halves both. % src: docs/24 §1.4
The beam axis is the line $x=y=0$ along $z$ (exceptions per family) and $\Lacc$ the inner cavity length along it, beam pipes excluded; for a predicted field, $U$ is taken from $\E$ and the predicted frequency enters $\Hf$ (\cref{sec:A03-qoi}). % src: docs/24 §1.2

\subsection{What a model must produce}\label{sec:03-targets}
A model maps a geometry with its curved mesh to the $\nmodes$ lowest frequencies $f_k$ and fields $\mathbf u_k\in Z_h\subset\R^{n}$ with $\mathbf u_j^\top\Mmat\mathbf u_k=\delta_{jk}$ and
Rayleigh quotients consistent with $f_k$ through \eqref{eq:03-freq}. The figures of merit \eqref{eq:03-q}--\eqref{eq:03-rq} are fixed functionals of $(f_k,\mathbf u_k)$ and the geometry, not separate outputs.
Since $\mathbf u_k$ is determined only up to sign and rotation within an eigenspace, accuracy is measured by invariant quantities (frequency errors; $\Mmat$-norm subspace distances).
\Cref{sec:method} constructs outputs that satisfy these constraints by design.
