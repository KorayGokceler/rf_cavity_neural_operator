\section{Problem formulation and discretisation}\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{The cavity eigenproblem}\label{sec:03-eigenproblem}
% src: docs/20 §8, docs/19 §6.1, AGENTS.md
Let $\Omega\subset\R^3$ be a connected, bounded cavity with a perfectly conducting (PEC) wall $\bdry$ and vacuum
($\varepsilon_0,\mu_0$) inside. The resonances of the lossless cavity are the pairs $(\E,k)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-strong}
  \curl\curl\,\E = k^2\,\E \ \text{ in } \Omega,\qquad \mathbf n\times\E=\mathbf 0 \ \text{ on } \bdry,
  \qquad k=\omega/c_0 ,
\end{equation}
with $c_0=\SI{299792458}{m/s}$ % src: src/qoi/operators.py C0
and resonant frequency $f=\omega/2\pi$. In weak form, find $\E\in\Hcurl\setminus\{0\}$ and $\lambda=k^2$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-weak}
  (\curl\E,\curl\mathbf v)_\Omega=\lambda\,(\E,\mathbf v)_\Omega\qquad\forall\,\mathbf v\in\Hcurl ,
\end{equation}
the wall condition being essential. The curl--curl form is only semi-definite: its kernel in $\Hcurl$ is
$\nabla H^1_0(\Omega)\oplus\mathcal H$, the gradients of $H^1_0$ functions plus a finite-dimensional space $\mathcal H$ of
harmonic Dirichlet fields whose dimension $b_2$ is the number of wall components beyond the first (one per floating conductor; each field is the
gradient of a potential that is constant on its component). % src: docs/20 §8, docs/19 §6.1
Handles of $\Omega$ add nothing to this kernel in the $\E$ formulation, which is why we use $\E$ and not $\Hf$ (for $\Hf$ the harmonic fields
created by handles are not gradients and cannot be removed by a gradient projection). % src: AGENTS.md invariants, docs/19 §6.1
Eigenvalue $0$ thus has infinite multiplicity, and the physical resonances are the positive eigenvalues
$0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\dots$, whose eigenfields are divergence free. This matters for learning: any learned
approximation of the fields contains gradient components, which have zero energy $(\curl\cdot,\curl\cdot)$ but non-zero mass and produce
Rayleigh quotients near~$0$ (spurious modes) unless they are removed explicitly.

\subsection{Conforming discretisation on curved meshes}\label{sec:03-discrete}
% src: docs/28 §2, docs/29 §1, src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators / solve_modes
Let $\mathcal T_h$ be a tetrahedral mesh of a CAD description of the cavity \citep{schoberl1997netgen} whose elements are images
$T=F_T(\hat T)$ of the reference tetrahedron under polynomial maps of degree $q$ (the curve order; $q=3$ throughout, % src: docs/29 §1, field_labels.DEFAULTS curve=3
so the geometry is iso- or super-parametric for $p\le 3$). $\Vh$ is the conforming Nédélec space of the first kind \citep{nedelec1980mixed} on
$\mathcal T_h$, mapped with the covariant Piola transform and with zero tangential trace on $\bdry$. Its order $p$ is indexed as in NGSolve
\citep{schoberl2014ngsolve}: $p=0$ is the lowest-order (Whitney) element, and $\Vh$ contains the gradients of the Lagrange space of
degree $p+1$; the basis is the hierarchical one with the local complete-sequence property \citep{schoberl2005high}. We use $p=2$ for the space of the model and
$p=3$ for the reference labels. % src: docs/29 §1
Let $\Sh$ be the $H^1_0$-conforming space of order $p+1$ on the same curved mesh, extended by one function per additional wall component
(equal to $1$ there and $0$ on the other components). Then $\nabla\Sh\subset\Vh$, and the discrete de~Rham sequence is exact
\citep{monk2003finite,arnold2006finite}, so $\nabla\Sh$ is exactly the kernel of the curl in $\Vh$. With $n$ the number of free (non-wall) DOFs and
$\{\mathbf w_i\}$ the basis, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-matrices}
  \Kmat_{ij}=(\curl\mathbf w_j,\curl\mathbf w_i),\quad
  \Mmat_{ij}=(\mathbf w_j,\mathbf w_i),\quad
  \Gmat\ \text{the matrix of }\nabla:\Sh\to\Vh,\quad
  \Kp=\Gmat^{\top}\Mmat\Gmat .
\end{equation}
$\Kmat$ is symmetric positive semi-definite, $\Mmat$ and $\Kp$ are symmetric positive definite, and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$. The Galerkin
discretisation of \eqref{eq:03-weak} is the generalised eigenproblem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-gev}
  \Kmat\,\mathbf x=\lambda\,\Mmat\,\mathbf x ,
\end{equation}
whose null space is $\operatorname{range}\Gmat$. The physical part of the spectrum lives on the
\emph{discretely divergence-free} subspace
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-proj}
  W_h=\{\mathbf v\in\Vh:\ (\mathbf v,\nabla\varphi)=0\ \ \forall\varphi\in\Sh\}
  =\operatorname{range}\Pmat,\qquad
  \Pmat=\mathsf I-\Gmat\,\Kp^{-1}\Gmat^{\top}\Mmat ,
\end{equation}
the $\Mmat$-orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{range}\Gmat$ ($\Pmat$ is the $\Mmat$-orthogonal projector onto it).
The \emph{discrete eigenvalues} $\lambda_{h,1}\le\lambda_{h,2}\le\dots$ are the positive eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:03-gev}, i.e.\ the
eigenvalues of $\Kmat$ restricted to $W_h$. They depend only on $(\mathcal T_h,q,p)$. Because $W_h$ is not contained in
the divergence-free fields, the $\lambda_{h,k}$ are not ordered with respect to the exact $\lambda_k$ (for lowest-order elements on a pillbox the
TM$_{010}$ frequency approaches the exact one from below, with deviations $-0.75\%$ to $-0.05\%$ as the mesh-size parameter of that study falls from $0.20$ to $0.05$). % src: docs/19 §6.3 table (TM010, E)
They converge to $\lambda_k$ under refinement by discrete compactness \citep{monk2003finite,boffi2010finite}. Bounds in this paper are therefore
relative to $\lambda_{h,k}$ of the space in which a computation runs, not to $\lambda_k$.

\paragraph{Normalisation and frequencies.}
% src: src/data/dataset_converter_3d.py normalise(); src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators; src/data/field_dataset.py check_labels
Operators are assembled on the normalised geometry $\boldsymbol\xi=(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)/s$, with $\mathbf c$ the volume centroid and $s$ the largest
absolute coordinate deviation of a vertex from $\mathbf c$. The eigenvalues of \eqref{eq:03-gev} are then $\lambda=s^2k^2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:03-freq}
  f_k=\frac{c_0\sqrt{\lambda_k}}{2\pi\,s}.
\end{equation}
Eigenvectors are $\Mmat$-normalised and determined up to sign and, in a multiple eigenspace (frequent in symmetric cavities), up to an orthogonal transformation.

\paragraph{One integration rule for all forms.}
% src: docs/28 §2 numerical note; src/data_gen/highorder.py volume_measure, solve_modes
On curved elements the integrands are rational, and NGSolve chooses the quadrature rule of each form from its own integrand. The form
$\curl\mathbf u\cdot\curl\mathbf v-\sigma\,\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v$ assembled at once then differs from $\Kmat-\sigma\Mmat$ by $3\times10^{-4}$ on a pillbox
at $p=2$, which biased shift-and-invert eigenvalues by $4\times10^{-5}$. We therefore evaluate every form (stiffness, mass, the stiffness of $\Sh$ that equals
$\Kp$, shifted operators) with one explicit tetrahedral rule of degree $2p+4$. % src: highorder.volume_measure(order, bonus=4)
The eigenvalues then agree with a dense solve of \eqref{eq:03-gev} to $10^{-13}$, % src: docs/28 §2
and $\Kp$ assembled as the stiffness of $\Sh$ equals $\Gmat^\top\Mmat\Gmat$ to $10^{-15}$, % src: highorder.solve_modes comment
so $\Pmat$ is an $\Mmat$-orthogonal projector to rounding.

\subsection{Figures of merit}\label{sec:03-qoi}
% src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.1–§1.5; src/qoi/operators.py figures_of_merit, surface_resistance; src/data_gen/highorder.py mode_qoi
We use peak (not RMS) phasors $e^{j\omega t}$, so $\Hf=(j/\omega\mu_0)\curl\E$, and the lossless field with a small surface resistance $R_s$ for the
wall loss. Every quantity is reported for the mode scaled to stored energy $U=\SI{1}{J}$ (as CST does), so amplitude and sign of $\E$ drop out:
\begin{align}
  U&=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\!\int_\Omega|\E|^2\,dV=\tfrac12\mu_0\!\int_\Omega|\Hf|^2\,dV, &
  P_c&=\tfrac12R_s\!\oint_{\bdry}|\mathbf n\times\Hf|^2\,dS, \notag\\
  \Qz&=\frac{\omega U}{P_c}, &
  G&=\Qz R_s=\frac{\omega\mu_0\int|\Hf|^2dV}{\oint|\mathbf n\times\Hf|^2dS}, \label{eq:03-q}\\
  V_{\mathrm{acc}}&=\Bigl|\int_\ell \E\!\cdot\!\mathbf d\;e^{\,j\omega t/(\beta c_0)}\,dt\Bigr|, &
  T&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}}{\int_\ell|\E\!\cdot\!\mathbf d|\,dt}, \notag\\
  \Eacc&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}}{\Lacc}, &
  \RoQ&=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}{\omega U},\quad R_{\mathrm{sh}}=\frac{V_{\mathrm{acc}}^2}{P_c}=\RoQ\,\Qz, \label{eq:03-rq}\\
  \Epk&=\max_{\bdry}|\E|, &
  \Bpk&=\mu_0\max_{\bdry}|\Hf|=\max_{\bdry}\frac{|\curl\E|}{\omega}. \notag
\end{align}
Here $R_s=\sqrt{\pi f\mu_0/\sigma}$ is the skin-effect resistance at the mode's own frequency, with $\sigma=\SI{5.8e7}{S/m}$ (copper) by default;
$\Qz\propto 1/R_s$, while $G$, $\RoQ$, $T$ and the peak ratios do not depend on the wall material. The beam axis $\ell$ has unit tangent $\mathbf d$ and arc
length $t$, and the particle velocity is $\beta c_0$ with $\beta=1$. % src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.1
The modulus makes $V_{\mathrm{acc}}$ independent of the time and axial origins, and $T\in[0,1]$ because the denominator is $\int|E_\parallel|$ and not
$|\int E_\parallel|$. $\Epk/\Eacc$ (dimensionless) and $\Bpk/\Eacc$ (in $\si{\milli\tesla\per(\mega\volt\per\metre)}$) are the peak-field ratios. % src: docs/24 §1.3, §1.5
We use the \emph{linac} convention for $\RoQ$ and $R_{\mathrm{sh}}$ \citep{wangler2008rf,padamsee2008rf}; the circuit convention (parallel RLC, $P=V^2/2R$) halves
both and leaves $\Qz$, $G$, $T$ and the peak ratios unchanged. % src: docs/24 §1.4

\paragraph{Beam axis and accelerating length.}
% src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.3; src/qoi/operators.py beam_axis, acceleration_length
All geometry families are built with the beam along $z$, and the default axis is the line $x=y=0$; exceptions are fixed per family (e.g.\ the half-wave
coaxial resonator is crossed along $x$ at mid-length). $\Lacc$ is the inner length of the cavity along the axis, beam pipes excluded: the sum of the cell lengths for
elliptical cavities, the cell length for pillbox-type cells, the tank length for spoke and drift-tube structures. For families without a beam it
is the chord of the axis inside $\Omega$, and $\RoQ$ is then a defined functional and not an accelerator figure. Axis and $\Lacc$ follow the nominal geometry
parameters, so on smoothly deformed samples they are those of the undeformed shape. % src: docs/24 §1.3 (limitation)

\paragraph{Eigenpair consistency.}
For a discrete eigenpair, $\tfrac12\mu_0\!\int|\Hf|^2$ computed from $\E$ and the eigenfrequency equals $\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\!\int|\E|^2$ because
$\omega^2=c_0^2\lambda/s^2$ with $\lambda$ the Rayleigh quotient; for a predicted field and frequency that are not an eigenpair the two differ by the
Rayleigh-quotient mismatch. We therefore take $U$ from $\E$ and use the frequency explicitly in $\Hf$, hence in $P_c$, $\Bpk$ and $\Qz$. % src: docs/24 §1.2
On high-order fields the functionals are evaluated directly, with wall values taken from the adjacent volume element (for $\E\in\Hcurl$ a plain boundary
evaluation gives only the tangential trace, which vanishes on a PEC wall). % src: highorder.mode_qoi docstring
Wall quantities ($\Qz$, $G$, $\Epk$, $\Bpk$) are far more sensitive to element quality than frequencies, which is why mesh quality is controlled in the data pipeline (\cref{sec:data}). % src: docs/29 §2.2

\subsection{What a model must produce}\label{sec:03-targets}
For a geometry with its curved mesh, a model maps the geometry to $\bigl(\{f_k\}_{k=1}^{\nmodes},\{\mathbf u_k\}_{k=1}^{\nmodes}\bigr)$: the $\nmodes$ lowest
frequencies and the $\nmodes$ fields as coefficient vectors $\mathbf u_k\in\R^{n}$ in $\Vh$ with $\mathbf u_j^\top\Mmat\mathbf u_k=\delta_{jk}$, $\mathbf u_k\in W_h$ and
Rayleigh quotient consistent with $f_k$ through \eqref{eq:03-freq}. The figures of merit \eqref{eq:03-q}--\eqref{eq:03-rq} are not separate outputs: they are fixed
functionals of $(f_k,\mathbf u_k)$ and the geometry (axis, $\Lacc$, wall). Because $\mathbf u_k$ is determined only up to sign and rotation within an eigenspace, accuracy is
measured by invariant quantities (frequency errors, and subspace distances in the $\Mmat$-norm). \Cref{sec:method} constructs outputs that satisfy these constraints by design.
