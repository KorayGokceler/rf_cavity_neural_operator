\subsection{Elliptical half-cells}\label{sec:A05-halfcell}

A half-cell joins an iris ellipse $(a,b)$ and an equator ellipse $(A,B)$ by their common tangent line, found by a least-squares solve with five starting points and tolerance $10^{-15}$.
A wall is accepted if the radius does not decrease along it and, for a re-entrant wall, its angle to the beam axis is at most $105^\circ$ and it stays at least $0.05\,R_{eq}$ from the iris plane.
End half-cells differ from the mid-cell with probability $0.6$ (ratios to the mid-cell: $L\times[0.9,1.1]$, $A\times[0.9,1.2]$, $B\times[0.85,1.4]$, $a,b\times[0.5,1.05]$, iris radius $\times[1,1.35]$).
Published designs enter as sampling centres only (perturbed by $\pm10\,\%$ per parameter and rescaled); labels always come from the finite-element solve.
The ranges were widened until all $14$ published reference half-cells that we compiled were accepted: the TESLA mid-cell and both end cups~\cite{aune2000superconducting}, the low-loss and ICHIRO cells~\cite{juntong2009highgradient}, NLSF and NLSF-RE, the Cornell ERL mid-cell and both ends, FCC-ee C3794, C3795 (mid and end) and FCCUROS5; four of them were rejected by the earlier solver tolerance and overlap rule.
Several of these dimensions could not be checked against full texts, so we treat them as plausible reference shapes, not as authoritative design data.

\subsection{Element counts}\label{sec:A05-counts}

For pillbox cells with pipes ($R=\SI{57}{mm}$, $r_p=\SI{17}{mm}$) fillet floors of $0.02$, $0.04$, $0.06$, $0.08$ (iris radius \SI{1.2}{mm}--\SI{4.5}{mm}) give $45$k, $30$k, $25$k and $18$k tets.
Four draws of the ridged box, which has fillets along every edge, gave $38$k--$89$k tets, four draws of the spoke family $5.2$k--$7.8$k (curvature safety 2, before adaptive refinement).

\subsection{Meshing and refinement details}\label{sec:A05-mesh}

\paragraph{Mesh repair.}
Meshes are accepted at a minimum Jacobian ratio of at least $0.05$; elements below $0.1$ are bisected and re-curved (about $+5\,\%$ tets), and only if that fails is the mesh regenerated with doubled curvature safety (about $3\times$ the tets).
Ridged boxes and drift-tube tanks are healed in OpenCascade first (sewing and solid-making are disabled because they delete revolved solids).
% src: docs/29 §2.2, §2.3
\paragraph{Size field.}
The size field of \cref{sec:05-adaptive} uses $\rho=0.25$ and $h_{\mathrm{new}}/h\in[0.3,1]$; the restrictions accumulate over iterations because rebuilding from the latest field alone oscillates, and the predicted element count (accurate to $15\,\%$) is scaled down uniformly if it exceeds the DOF budget.
Curved elements with a Jacobian ratio below $0.05$ get half the local size.
The start mesh uses curvature safety $1.5$ (falling back to $2$ if elements fold); an example of the bisection closure is that marking 200 of 8109 tets of a pillbox cell with pipes produced $29$k tets ($105$ new tets per marked tet).
% src: docs/30 §4; adaptive.py mesh_remesher, size_field
\paragraph{Stopping rule.}
Between two consecutive levels (at least two solves) the relative change of the cluster-mean frequency must be below $10^{-6}$, and the relative changes of cluster-mean $\Qz$ and $G$, summed $\RoQ$ (clusters with $\RoQ$ above $1\,\%$ of the largest) and maximum $\Epk$, $\Bpk$ below $10^{-3}$; modes with relative spacing below $10^{-3}$ form a cluster, so the test is invariant to rotations inside a degenerate pair.
Peaks are left out of the test for samples with sharp edges.
% src: adaptive.py DEFAULTS, invariant_qoi, adaptive_modes; docs/30 §6

\subsection{Reload consistency}\label{sec:A05-reload}

A mesh repaired by bisection keeps a refinement hierarchy in NGSolve, whose topology still numbers the bisected parents' edges and faces.
On one pillbox cell with pipes the repaired mesh gave $116\,322$ order-two DOFs and the same mesh after saving and loading $115\,704$, so stored fields did not fit the rebuilt space.
Meshes are therefore rebuilt through a file round trip before storage (eigenvalues on reloaded meshes agree with the originals to $10^{-14}$).
The accelerating length $\Lacc$ is the sum of the cell lengths for elliptical cavities, the cell for pillbox and re-entrant cells, the tank for spoke and drift-tube linac cavities, the outer diameter for the coaxial resonator and the box length for the ridged box (otherwise the axis chord); the beam axis is the $x$-axis through mid-length for the coaxial resonator and through the ridge gap for the ridged box.
On deformed samples the axis and $\Lacc$ come from the nominal geometry.

\subsection{Generator speed-ups}\label{sec:A05-speedup}

\Cref{tab:A05-speedup} lists the measured speed-ups of the label generator.
The largest was the transfer to the model mesh, which re-searched every model integration point in the label mesh for every form and mode; points are now located once.
The gradient stiffness $\Kp$ is assembled as a gradient--gradient form on the potential space instead of a sparse triple product (same matrix to a relative $9\cdot10^{-16}$), and oversized draws are rejected right after mesh generation.
The labels are unchanged: at 150k DOFs frequencies differ by $1.6\cdot10^{-15}$, $\Qz$ and $G$ by $7\cdot10^{-14}$, $\RoQ$ and peaks by at most $7\cdot10^{-10}$; at 450k DOFs the field subspace differs by $8\cdot10^{-6}$, limited by the Arnoldi tolerance, whereas the transfer alone differs by $3.5\cdot10^{-14}$.
