\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

The network predicts neither a field nor an eigenvalue.
It predicts $m$ vector fields $\bm\psi_1,\dots,\bm\psi_m$ on the geometry; a fixed, mesh-dependent linear-algebra layer maps them into the space $\Vh$ of \cref{sec:formulation}, removes their gradient part, and a Rayleigh--Ritz step selects the $K$ best combinations (\cref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{Tokens and trunk}\label{sec:04-trunk}

Let $\mathcal T_h$ be the curved mesh of $\Omega$ with $N_t$ elements and $\Vh$ the Nédélec space of order $p$ on it.
The tokens are the $N=N_t\,n_q$ quadrature points $\bm x_q$ of a reference rule of degree $2p$ on every element, $n_q=14$ at $p=2$ and $n_q=24$ at $p=3$.
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py _quadrature (IntegrationRule(TET, 2*order)); n_q = 14, 24 checked with NGSolve 6.2; docs/34 §4.2
Vertices are avoided because coarse curved meshes can have no interior vertex (torsion function $0/0$).
% src: docs/28 §2, §3
Each token carries nine features on the curved geometry (normalised coordinates): position, wall distance, unit vector to the nearest wall point, point volume (quadrature weight over its maximum) and the torsion function $\chi$ ($-\Delta\chi=1$, $\chi=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, solved in the order-$(p+1)$ $H^1_0$ space on the same mesh, normalised by its maximum).
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py point_features, wall_features; docs/28 §2

Let $\gamma(\bm x)=\sqrt{2/d_{\rm f}}\,[\cos(\bm\Xi\bm x),\sin(\bm\Xi\bm x)]$ be a random Fourier feature map with a frozen Gaussian matrix~\cite{rahimi2007random,tancik2020fourier}.
The embedding is $\bm h^0_q=\mathrm{MLP}([\gamma(\bm x_q)\,\Vert\,\bm y_q])$ with the feature vector $\bm y_q\in\R^9$, followed by $L$ pre-norm residual blocks $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{Attn}_w(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$, $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$ with, per head,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-attn}
  [\mathrm{Attn}_w(\bm h)]_i=\frac{\sum_{q} w_q\,\mathcal S_{iq}\;\Theta_v\bm h_q}{\sum_{q} w_q\,\mathcal S_{iq}+\varepsilon_{\rm att}},\qquad
  \mathcal S_{iq}=\phi(\Theta_q\bm h_i)^{\!\top}\phi(\Theta_k\bm h_q),\quad \phi=\mathrm{elu}+1,
\end{equation}
the linear attention of Katharopoulos et al.~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers} with a weight on every key.
The weights $w_q\propto|\det J|\,\hat w_q$ are the quadrature weights of the mesh, normalised to $\sum_qw_q=1$, and $\varepsilon_{\rm att}=10^{-6}$.
% src: src/models/layers.py LinearAttention (eps=1e-6), mass_weights; src/data/field_dataset.py (Area = quadrature weights)
Both sums are quadratures of $\int\mathcal S\,\Theta_v\bm h\,\mathrm d\bm y$ and $\int\mathcal S\,\mathrm d\bm y$ for the separable kernel $\mathcal S$: a global integral operator evaluated with the mesh's own quadrature (cf.\ the Galerkin-type attention of Cao~\cite{cao2021choose}); forming $\sum_q w_q\phi(\Theta_k\bm h_q)(\Theta_v\bm h_q)^{\!\top}$ once makes a block cost $O(Nd^2)$ for embedding dimension $d$.
The head is a two-layer MLP, $\bm\psi_{1..m}(\bm x_q)=\mathrm{head}([\bm h^L_q\,\Vert\,\gamma(\bm x_q)])\in(\R^3)^m$; it does not depend on the numbering of mesh entities, so the $\bm\psi_j$ are geometric fields.
Width, depth, heads, $m$, $K$ and the Fourier dimension are hyperparameters; the configuration-file defaults (\cref{sec:A04-cost}) are larger than the sizes used for all reported results (\cref{sec:experiments}).

\subsection{From point values to degrees of freedom}\label{sec:04-map}

The point values become DOFs of $\Vh$ through a fixed linear map in two stages.
\emph{(i) Element fit.}
Let $\Phi_t\in\R^{n_q\times n_\ell}$ be a basis of the polynomials of degree $\le p$ ($n_\ell=\binom{p+3}{3}$: $10$ at $p=2$, $20$ at $p=3$) at the quadrature points of element $t$, and $\bm\Lambda_t=\mathrm{diag}(|\det J_t(\hat{\bm x}_q)|\,\hat w_q)$.
Each Cartesian component of $\bm\psi_j$ is fitted by weighted least squares, $\bm a_{t,c,j}=\mathsf F_t\bm\psi_{t,c,j}$ with $\mathsf F_t=(\Phi_t^{\!\top}\bm\Lambda_t\Phi_t)^{-1}\Phi_t^{\!\top}\bm\Lambda_t$, which reproduces polynomials of degree $\le p$.
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators (P = solve(A, Phi^T W), W = detJ*wq); n_l = dim P_p = 10, 20
\emph{(ii) Interpolation into $\Vh$.}
A fixed sparse operator $\Cmat$ maps the element-wise polynomial field to $\Vh$.
In the implementation it is the NGSolve conversion operator~\cite{schoberl2014cpp11}, which applies the dual basis of $\Vh$ element by element and averages shared DOFs.
% src: ngsolve ConvertOperator docstring ("interpolation operator defined by element-wise application of dual shapes (and averaging between elements)"); docs/28 §2 calls it projection-based interpolation
It plays the role of a projection-based interpolation operator~\cite{demkowicz2005projection}; we use only its linearity and locality.
With $\mathsf I_{\rm int}$ the $0/1$ diagonal matrix of non-wall DOFs and $\mathsf F=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\mathsf F_t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-V}
  \Vmat_{:,j}=\mathsf I_{\rm int}\,\Cmat\,\mathsf F\,\bm\psi_j,\qquad \Vmat\in\R^{n\times m},
\end{equation}
so $\bm n\times\E=0$ holds structurally, and gradients flow through two sparse products.
All operators use one explicit integration rule on the curved elements, as the label solver does.
% src: AGENTS.md invariants (highorder.volume_measure); docs/28 §2 numerical note

\subsection{Kernel projection}\label{sec:04-proj}

The gradients $\nabla\Sh$ (plus one potential per extra connected component of the wall) span the kernel of $\Kmat$ on $\Vh$.
A generic $\Vmat$ has a large gradient part (about $60\,\%$ of the mass of the raw basis in a smoke test of the lowest-order variant), which would appear as spurious modes with $\theta\approx0$.
% src: docs/20 §6 (grad_frac 0.59, 30 geometries, N0 model)
With $\mathsf B=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat$ and $\mathsf Z=\Kp^{-1}\mathsf B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-proj}
  \Pmat\Vmat=\Vmat-\Gmat\mathsf Z,\quad
  \Av:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat,\quad
  \Mdiv:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat-\mathsf B^{\!\top}\mathsf Z,
\end{equation}
by $\Kmat\Gmat=0$ and $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ (a Schur complement).
$\Pmat\Vmat$ is the $\Mmat$-orthogonal projection of $\Vmat$ onto the discretely divergence-free subspace $Z_h=\{\bm v\in\Vh:\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat=0\}$; neither Gram matrix requires it to be formed.
$\Kp=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat$ is symmetric positive definite.

$\mathsf Z$ is computed by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients, batched over samples and right-hand sides, until $\|\mathsf r_j\|_2\le\tau\|\mathsf B_{:,j}\|_2$ for every column, $\tau=10^{-8}$ (at most $3000$ iterations, with a warning if reached).
% src: src/models/hcurl.py _pcg (check_every=10, thr = tol*||rhs||); configs/field_3d.yaml (kp_tol 1e-8, kp_maxiter 3000)
The solve runs in float64; in float32 the CG iteration did not converge within its iteration cap and $\Mdiv$ was off by about $79\,\%$ in a test.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py project_basis comment ("float32 CG does not converge (M_div off by ~79%, docs/21 B3)"); AGENTS.md invariants; referee M5 (CG hit the 2000-iteration cap)
A cheap diagonal penalty on the gradient content instead of the projection was measured and rejected (\cref{sec:07-penalty}).
% src: docs/31 §3.5 (penalty vs projection ablation, owned by 07)
$\mathsf B\mapsto\mathsf Z$ is the symmetric operator $\Kp^{-1}$, so its backward pass is one more solve, $\bar{\mathsf B}=\Kp^{-1}\bar{\mathsf Z}$, implemented as a custom PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} autograd function.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py KpSolve
The trunk costs $O(Nd^2L)$ per geometry; the float64 CG solves dominate (about $22$\,s per training step of the default-size model, not the one of the experiments, on a $13$k-tetrahedron mesh with $183$k tokens at order two, four CPU threads; \cref{sec:A04-cost}).
% src: docs/28 §4 "Measured per step" (13k tets, 183k tokens, CPU, 4 threads: step ~22 s); docs/28 §4 ("p3 is impractical for training")

\subsection{Rank-revealing Rayleigh--Ritz layer}\label{sec:04-ritz}

The Ritz problem $\Av c=\theta\,\Mdiv c$ on $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ is degenerate exactly when a combination $\Vmat c$ is a pure gradient: then $\Pmat\Vmat c=0$ and both $\Mdiv c$ and $\Av c$ vanish.
A ridge would give them an arbitrary $\theta$ inside the spectrum, so we reveal the rank.
Let $\mathsf D=\mathrm{diag}(\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat)^{-1/2}$ and $\mathsf D\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat\mathsf D+\varepsilon_{\rm M}\mathsf I=\mathsf L\mathsf L^{\!\top}$, $\varepsilon_{\rm M}=10^{-8}$.
The eigenvalues $\mu_i\in[0,1]$ of $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}=\mathsf O\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\mathsf O^{\!\top}$ are the divergence-free mass fractions of the whitened directions.
Directions with $\mu_i\le\tau_{\rm drop}=10^{-6}$ are discarded; with the $r'$ remaining ones, $\mathsf W=\mathsf L^{-\top}\mathsf O_{\rm kept}\mu_{\rm kept}^{-1/2}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-ritz}
  \mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\,\Av\,\mathsf D\mathsf W\,\mathsf y=\theta\,\mathsf y,\qquad c=\mathsf D\mathsf W\mathsf y.
\end{equation}
% src: src/models/hcurl.py projected_eigh (mass_ridge 1e-8, drop_tol 1e-6); docs/20 §2
The discarded directions receive $\theta=+\infty$ (a large finite number) and sort last.
The $K$ smallest values give $f_k=c_0\sqrt{\theta_k}/(2\pi s)$, with $s$ the length scale of the normalised coordinates (eigenvalues scale as $s^{-2}$; stored labels and losses use the same normalised units), and the $\Mmat$-orthonormal fields $\E_k=\Pmat\Vmat c_k$.
In the backward pass of both eigendecompositions the factor $1/(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ of the standard eigenvector gradient~\cite{ionescu2015matrix,seeger2017autodiff} is replaced by $\Delta/(\Delta^2+\varepsilon_{\rm b})$, $\varepsilon_{\rm b}=10^{-4}$, since cavities have (near-)degenerate mode pairs.
% src: src/models/layers.py BroadenedEigh; configs/field_3d.yaml (eig_broadening 1e-4)

\subsection{Upper-bound guarantee}\label{sec:04-bound}

Let $\lamh_1\le\lamh_2\le\dots$ be the positive eigenvalues of $\Kmat x=\lambda\Mmat x$ on $\Vh$, for the forms of \cref{sec:formulation} assembled with one explicit integration rule (so $\lamh_k$ depends on this rule, and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$ holds to rounding for the same rule); $f=c_0\sqrt\lambda/(2\pi s)$ is monotone in $\lambda$, so all statements hold for both.
$Z_h$ is the discretely divergence-free subspace of \cref{sec:formulation}.
We use assumption (A) of \cref{eq:03-A}, $\ker\Kmat=\mathrm{range}\,\Gmat$ on $\Vh$ (the rank form of discrete exactness; $\Kmat\Gmat=0$ gives only ``$\supset$''), and that $\Mmat$ is positive definite; it is not proved here for the curved meshes used, but we verified it numerically on $15$ small cases (five curved meshes, $p=1,2,3$): $\dim\ker\Kmat=\operatorname{rank}\Gmat$ equals the number of columns of $\Gmat$ in all of them (\cref{tab:A04-rank}).
% src: docs/35 §1 (15 cases, dense eigensolves, meshes of at most 6312 free DOFs)
Under (A), $\Vh=\ker\Kmat\oplus Z_h$ is an $\Mmat$-orthogonal splitting, $\Kmat$ is positive definite on $Z_h$, and the positive eigenvalues of the full problem are those of the pencil restricted to $Z_h$ (an eigenvector with $\lambda>0$ is $\Mmat$-orthogonal to $\ker\Kmat$ because $\bm g^{\!\top}\Mmat x=\lambda^{-1}\bm g^{\!\top}\Kmat x=0$).
Courant--Fischer~\cite{parlett1998symmetric} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-minmax}
  \lamh_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset Z_h\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Ritz values are upper bounds]\label{prop:upper-bound}
Assume (A) and that $\mathsf Z$ solves $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ exactly.
Let $\Vmat\in\R^{n\times m}$ be arbitrary and let $r'$ be the number of directions kept by the Ritz layer.
Then there is a subspace $\mathcal U\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)\subset Z_h$ of dimension $r'$ whose Rayleigh--Ritz values for $(\Kmat,\Mmat)$ are the finite outputs $\theta_1\le\dots\le\theta_{r'}$ of \eqref{eq:04-ritz}, and
\[
  \theta_k\ \ge\ \lamh_k\ >\ 0\qquad(k=1,\dots,r'),
\]
while $\theta_k=+\infty$ for $k>r'$.
Hence no spurious zero mode occurs and $f_k\ge f_{h,k}$ for every network weight and input geometry, before and after training.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1.} $\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Pmat\Vmat=\mathsf B-\Kp\mathsf Z=0$, so by (A) every column of $\Pmat\Vmat$ is $\Mmat$-orthogonal to $\ker\Kmat$, i.e.\ lies in $Z_h$.

\emph{Step 2.} Since $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}=\mathsf O\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,\mathsf O^{\!\top}$, the matrix $\mathsf W$ satisfies $\mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf W=\mathsf I_{r'}$ (the ridge only enters $\mathsf L$).
With $\mathsf Y=\mathsf D\mathsf W$ and \eqref{eq:04-proj}, $(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)=\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Mdiv\mathsf Y=\mathsf I_{r'}$: the columns of $\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y$ are $\Mmat$-orthonormal, hence independent, and span $\mathcal U\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ with $\dim \mathcal U=r'$.
The matrix in \eqref{eq:04-ritz} is $\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Av\mathsf Y=(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)$, the Gram matrix of $\Kmat$ in an $\Mmat$-orthonormal basis of $\mathcal U$, so its eigenvalues are the Rayleigh--Ritz values on $\mathcal U$~\cite{parlett1998symmetric} and
\[
  \theta_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset \mathcal U\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\]

\emph{Step 3.} $\mathcal U\subset Z_h$, so this minimum runs over a subfamily of the subspaces in \eqref{eq:04-minmax}; hence $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$, and $\lamh_1>0$ by (A).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the proposition does not give.}
(i) The bound is index-wise and gives no mode identity: if the span misses a physical mode, a later eigenvalue fills its index and still satisfies it; there is no accuracy bound either (accuracy is measured, \cref{sec:experiments}).
(ii) Every Rayleigh--Ritz method in $Z_h$ has this property; what is specific here is that it holds for arbitrary network output, so training cannot violate it.
(iii) It is relative to the \emph{discrete} eigenvalue of the space the model runs in: since $\lamh_k\to\lambda_k$ as the space is enriched~\cite{boffi2010finite} but $\lamh_k-\lambda_k$ has no guaranteed sign, we make no claim about the exact eigenvalue.
The Ritz error $\theta_k-\lamh_k\ge0$ is controlled by the energy-norm best-approximation error of the eigenvectors~\cite{boffi2010finite}.

\begin{remark}[Inexact projection]\label{rem:04-inexact}
\Cref{prop:upper-bound} is exact for an exact $\mathsf Z$; the CG solve stops at a relative residual $\tau=10^{-8}$.
The numerator of the Rayleigh quotient is unaffected ($\Av=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat$ is evaluated directly and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$); only $\Mdiv$ is perturbed, by a relative amount $\bar\eta$ that can be computed a posteriori, and then $\tilde\theta_k\ge\lamh_k/(1+\bar\eta)$ if $\bar\eta<1$ (\cref{sec:A04-inexact}).
So for the implemented projection the bound holds up to a relative error $\bar\eta$; in a stress test with synthetic bases on two meshes $\bar\eta\approx(1\text{--}3)\,\tau$ ($\approx10^{-8}$ at the shipped $\tau$), and the a~posteriori guarantee held in every run with $\bar\eta<1$.
The plain bound $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$ was violated only for bases with a gradient admixture, by up to $1.5\times10^{-8}$ (relative; gradient mass fraction $0.99$) and $2\times10^{-11}$--$1.4\times10^{-10}$ (fraction $0.5$), far below the model error.
The rule discarding directions of tiny divergence-free fraction protects only for $\tau\lesssim10^{-6}$: at $\tau=10^{-4}$ pure-gradient columns survive it ($\bar\eta=1$, $\theta\approx0$).
In the equal-cost study the smallest margin $f_{\rm model}/f_{\rm FE}-1$ over arms, geometries and modes was $+0.47\,\%$; the stress test is in \cref{sec:A04-stress}.
% src: docs/35 §2 (eta-bar approx 1-3 tau; violations up to -1.5e-8 at gamma 0.99, -2e-11..-1.4e-10 at gamma 0.5, shipped tau; drop rule fails at tau=1e-4); docs/34 §8.2 (at least +0.47 %); docs/34 §4.1-§4.2 quote no number
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Space independence.}\label{rem:04-space}
The network sees only points, features and weights, and the Ritz layer needs only $\Kmat,\Mmat,\Gmat,\Kp,\Cmat,\mathsf F$ of the space it runs in, so the same weights can be run on another conforming space (order three, or a refined mesh) by rebuilding these operators, and the Ritz values then bound the discrete eigenvalues of \emph{that} space (in the transfer study all predicted eigenvalues lay above the finite-element eigenvalue of the evaluated space, \cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: docs/34 §4.2 (no number quoted: units slip in the doc)

\subsection{Losses}\label{sec:04-loss}

Let $\mathsf U_\star=[\bm u_1,\dots,\bm u_{n_{\rm tgt}}]$ be the stored target fields on $\Vh$ (label eigenvectors projected onto the model space, \cref{sec:data}) and, for $\mathsf N\in\{\Mmat,\Kmat\}$, let $\mathsf G_{\mathsf N}$ be the $\mathsf N$-Gram matrix of $[\Pmat\Vmat\,|\,\mathsf U_\star]$.
It is assembled without forming $\Pmat\Vmat$ and without a second CG solve: the $\Pmat\Vmat$ block is $\Mdiv$ or $\Av$, and the cross blocks are $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf U_\star-\mathsf Z^{\!\top}\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf U_\star$ and $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\mathsf U_\star$.

\paragraph{Span loss.}
With $\Pi_{\mathsf N}$ the $\mathsf N$-orthogonal projector onto $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ (a Cholesky solve of the Jacobi-scaled Gram matrix with relative ridge $10^{-9}$), the uncaptured fraction of target $\bm u_k$ is $r_k^{\mathsf N}=1-\|\Pi_{\mathsf N}\bm u_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2/\|\bm u_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2\in[0,1]$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-span}
  \mathcal L_{\rm span}=\frac1{n_{\rm tgt}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm tgt}}\sqrt{\tfrac12\big(r_k^{\Mmat}+r_k^{\Kmat}\big)+10^{-8}}.
\end{equation}
% src: src/training/lightning_module.py span_residual, _span_terms (span_norm both, span_root true, span_ridge 1e-9, eps 1e-8)
$\sqrt{r_k^{\Mmat}}$, $\sqrt{r_k^{\Kmat}}$ are the relative best-approximation errors in the mass and energy norms (the root keeps the gradient from vanishing); $\mathcal L_{\rm span}$ is invariant under recombination of the columns of $\Vmat$ and under rotations in degenerate target clusters, so it needs no eigenvalue matching.

\paragraph{Other terms.}
The self-supervised term $\mathcal L_{\rm ss}=-\log\sum_{i\le r'}\theta_i^{-1}$ over the kept Ritz values needs no labels (the code evaluates $\mathrm{tr}(\Av^{-1}\Mdiv)$ by a ridge-regularised Cholesky solve, equal to this when no direction is dropped) and, since $\theta_i\ge\lamh_i$, is bounded below by $-\log\sum_{i\le r'}\lamh_i^{-1}$, with equality iff the span is that of the $r'$ lowest eigenvectors (unique only if $\lamh_{r'}<\lamh_{r'+1}$); the projected mass keeps gradient content from lowering it (without projection the compliance exploded by more than $10^3$).
% src: docs/20 §3 (selfsup: "Neredeyse-gradyan bir kolonda projeksiyonsuz compliance >10^3x patlar (test edildi)")
$\mathcal L_{\rm ortho}$, the mean squared off-diagonal correlation of $\Mdiv$, penalises collinear fields, and $\mathcal L_{\rm freq}=\frac1K\sum_k(z_k-z_k^\star)^2$ acts on frequencies standardised with the training-label mean and standard deviation.
The total is $\sum_{\rm t}\alpha_{\rm t}\mathcal L_{\rm t}$ with weights $(\alpha_{\rm span},\alpha_{\rm ss},\alpha_{\rm ortho},\alpha_{\rm freq})=(1,0.01,0.01,0.1)$.
% src: configs/field_3d.yaml (span_weight 1.0, selfsup_weight 0.01, ortho_weight 0.01, freq_weight 0.1)
A loss on the figures of merit exists in the code but is disabled for this model.

Each sample's span term is multiplied by $\omega_{\mathrm{fid}}$ and its frequency term by $\omega_{\mathrm{fid}}\beta_{\mathrm{fid}}$, with $\mathrm{fid}\in\{\LF,\HF\}$ the fidelity of its label (\cref{sec:multifidelity}); the defaults are $\omega=\beta=(1,1)$.
% src: docs/34 §2, configs/field_3d.yaml (fidelity_weights, fidelity_freq_scale), src/training/lightning_module.py _compute_loss_3d, _span_terms
