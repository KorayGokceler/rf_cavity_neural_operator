\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

The network predicts neither a field nor an eigenvalue.
It predicts $m$ vector fields $\bm\psi_1,\dots,\bm\psi_m$ on the geometry; a fixed, mesh-dependent linear-algebra layer maps them into the space $\Vh$ of \cref{sec:formulation}, removes their gradient part, and a Rayleigh--Ritz step selects the $K$ best combinations (\cref{fig:pipeline}).

\subsection{Tokens and trunk}\label{sec:04-trunk}

Let $\mathcal T_h$ be the curved mesh of $\Omega$ with $N_t$ elements and $\Vh$ the Nédélec space of order $p$ on it.
The tokens are the $N=N_t\,n_q$ quadrature points $\bm x_q$ of a reference rule of degree $2p$ on every element, $n_q=14$ at $p=2$ and $n_q=24$ at $p=3$.
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py _quadrature (IntegrationRule(TET, 2*order)); n_q = 14, 24 checked with NGSolve 6.2; docs/34 §4.2
Vertices are not used because coarse curved meshes can have no interior vertex; in single-sample overfitting, vertex tokens with barycentric interpolation stopped at a relative eigenvalue error of $0.8$--$1.1\times10^{-4}$ ($p=2$) against $10^{-6}$--$9\times10^{-6}$ for quadrature-point tokens (\cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: docs/28 §3 (0.8-1.1e-4 after 2000 steps vs 1e-6 - 9e-6 after 1250 steps, p = 2)
Each token carries nine features computed on the curved geometry in normalised coordinates: position, wall distance, unit vector to the nearest wall point, point volume (quadrature weight over its maximum) and the torsion function $\chi$ ($-\Delta\chi=1$ in $\Omega$, $\chi=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, solved in the order-$(p+1)$ $H^1_0$ space on the same mesh, normalised by its maximum).
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py point_features, wall_features; docs/28 §2

Let $\gamma(\bm x)=\sqrt{2/d_{\rm f}}\,[\cos(\mathsf B_{\rm F}\bm x),\sin(\mathsf B_{\rm F}\bm x)]$ be a random Fourier feature map with a frozen Gaussian matrix~\cite{rahimi2007random,tancik2020fourier}.
The embedding is $\bm h^0_q=\mathrm{MLP}([\gamma(\bm x_q)\,\Vert\,\bm y_q])$ with the feature vector $\bm y_q\in\R^9$, followed by $L$ pre-norm residual blocks $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{Attn}_w(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$, $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$ with, per head,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-attn}
  [\mathrm{Attn}_w(\bm h)]_i=\frac{\sum_{q} w_q\,\phi(\mathsf A_q\bm h_i)^{\!\top}\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)\;\mathsf A_v\bm h_q}{\sum_{q} w_q\,\phi(\mathsf A_q\bm h_i)^{\!\top}\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)+\varepsilon_{\rm att}},\qquad \phi=\mathrm{elu}+1,
\end{equation}
the linear attention of Katharopoulos et al.~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers} with a weight on every key.
The weights $w_q\propto|\det J|\,\hat w_q$ are the quadrature weights of the mesh, normalised to $\sum_qw_q=1$, and $\varepsilon_{\rm att}=10^{-6}$.
% src: src/models/layers.py LinearAttention (eps=1e-6), mass_weights; src/data/field_dataset.py (Area = quadrature weights)
Both sums are quadratures of $\int\kappa\,\mathsf A_v\bm h\,\mathrm d\bm y$ and $\int\kappa\,\mathrm d\bm y$ for a separable kernel $\kappa$: a global integral operator evaluated with the mesh's own quadrature (an interpretation shared with the Galerkin-type attention of Cao~\cite{cao2021choose}), whose value changes under refinement by quadrature error only.
Forming $\sum_q w_q\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)(\mathsf A_v\bm h_q)^{\!\top}$ once makes a block cost $O(Nd^2)$ for embedding dimension $d$.
The head is a two-layer MLP, $\bm\psi_{1..m}(\bm x_q)=\mathrm{head}([\bm h^L_q\,\Vert\,\gamma(\bm x_q)])\in(\R^3)^m$; it does not depend on the numbering of mesh entities, so the $\bm\psi_j$ are geometric fields.
Defaults are $d=128$, $L=4$, $4$ heads, $m=24$, $K=6$ and a $64$-dimensional Fourier map of length scale $0.1$; the experiments use a smaller configuration (\cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: configs/field_3d.yaml (embed_dim 128, n_layers 4, n_heads 4, n_basis 24, num_field_modes 6, rff_dim 64, rff_length_scale 0.1)

\subsection{From point values to degrees of freedom}\label{sec:04-map}

The point values become DOFs of $\Vh$ through a fixed linear map in two stages.
\emph{(i) Element fit.}
Let $\mathbb P_p$ be the polynomials of degree $\le p$ in the reference coordinates ($n_\ell=\binom{p+3}{3}$), $\Phi_t\in\R^{n_q\times n_\ell}$ a basis evaluated at the quadrature points of element $t$ and $\mathsf W_t=\mathrm{diag}(|\det J_t(\hat{\bm x}_q)|\,\hat w_q)$.
Each Cartesian component of $\bm\psi_j$ is fitted by weighted least squares, $\bm a_{t,c,j}=\mathsf F_t\bm\psi_{t,c,j}$ with $\mathsf F_t=(\Phi_t^{\!\top}\mathsf W_t\Phi_t)^{-1}\Phi_t^{\!\top}\mathsf W_t$, which reproduces every element of $\mathbb P_p$ (the normal matrix is invertible; $n_q\ge n_\ell$: $14\ge10$ at $p=2$, $24\ge20$ at $p=3$).
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators (P = solve(A, Phi^T W), W = detJ*wq); n_l = dim P_p = 10, 20
\emph{(ii) Interpolation into $\Vh$.}
A fixed sparse operator $\Cmat$ maps the element-wise polynomial field to $\Vh$.
In the implementation it is the NGSolve conversion operator~\cite{schoeberl2014cpp11}, which applies the dual basis of $\Vh$ element by element and averages shared DOFs.
% src: ngsolve ConvertOperator docstring ("interpolation operator defined by element-wise application of dual shapes (and averaging between elements)"); docs/28 §2 calls it projection-based interpolation
It plays the role of a projection-based interpolation operator~\cite{demkowicz2005projection}; we use only its linearity and locality.
With $\mathsf I_{\rm int}$ the $0/1$ diagonal matrix of non-wall DOFs and $\mathsf F=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\mathsf F_t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-V}
  \Vmat_{:,j}=\mathsf I_{\rm int}\,\Cmat\,\mathsf F\,\bm\psi_j,\qquad \Vmat\in\R^{n\times m},
\end{equation}
so $\bm n\times\E=0$ holds structurally, and gradients flow through two sparse products.
All operators are assembled once per geometry with one explicit integration rule on the curved elements, so that the forms of the model and of the label solver coincide.
% src: AGENTS.md invariants (highorder.volume_measure); docs/28 §2 numerical note

\subsection{Kernel projection}\label{sec:04-proj}

The gradients $\nabla\Sh$ (plus one potential per extra connected component of the wall) span the kernel of $\Kmat$ on $\Vh$.
A generic $\Vmat$ has a large gradient part (about $60\,\%$ of the mass of the raw basis in a smoke test of the lowest-order variant), which would appear as spurious modes with $\theta\approx0$.
% src: docs/20 §6 (grad_frac 0.59, 30 geometries, N0 model)
With $\mathsf B=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat$ and $\mathsf Z=\Kp^{-1}\mathsf B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-proj}
  \Pmat\Vmat=\Vmat-\Gmat\mathsf Z,\quad
  \Av:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat,\quad
  \Mdiv:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat-\mathsf B^{\!\top}\mathsf Z,
\end{equation}
by $\Kmat\Gmat=0$ and $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ (a Schur complement).
$\Pmat\Vmat$ is the $\Mmat$-orthogonal projection of $\Vmat$ onto the discretely divergence-free subspace $W_h=\{\bm v\in\Vh:\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat=0\}$; neither Gram matrix requires it to be formed.
$\Kp=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat$ is symmetric positive definite ($\Gmat$ has full column rank), so this E formulation needs no zero-mean handling.

$\mathsf Z$ is computed by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients, batched over samples and over the $m$ right-hand sides, each (sample, column) pair with its own step sizes, until $\|\mathsf r_j\|_2\le\tau\|\mathsf B_{:,j}\|_2$ for every column, $\tau=10^{-8}$ (checked every ten iterations; at most $3000$ iterations, with a warning if reached).
% src: src/models/hcurl.py _pcg (check_every=10, thr = tol*||rhs||); configs/field_3d.yaml (kp_tol 1e-8, kp_maxiter 3000)
The solve runs in float64; in float32 the iteration did not converge and $\Mdiv$ was off by about $79\,\%$ in a test (we attribute this to cancellation in the Schur complement).
% src: src/models/hcurl.py project_basis comment ("float32 CG does not converge (M_div off by ~79%, docs/21 B3)"); AGENTS.md invariants
Since $\mathsf B\mapsto\mathsf Z$ is the symmetric operator $\Kp^{-1}$, its backward pass is one more solve with the same matrix, $\bar{\mathsf B}=\Kp^{-1}\bar{\mathsf Z}$, implemented as a custom PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} autograd function instead of differentiating through the iterations.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py KpSolve

\subsection{Rank-revealing Rayleigh--Ritz layer}\label{sec:04-ritz}

The Ritz problem $\Av c=\theta\,\Mdiv c$ on $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ is degenerate exactly when a combination $\Vmat c$ is a pure gradient: then $\Pmat\Vmat c=0$ and both $\Mdiv c$ and $\Av c$ vanish.
A ridge would give such directions an arbitrary $\theta$ inside the spectrum, and the losses of \cref{sec:04-loss} do not see gradient content, so we reveal the rank instead.
Let $\mathsf D=\mathrm{diag}(\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat)^{-1/2}$ and $\mathsf D\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat\mathsf D+\varepsilon_{\rm M}\mathsf I=\mathsf L\mathsf L^{\!\top}$, $\varepsilon_{\rm M}=10^{-8}$.
The eigenvalues $\mu_i\in[0,1]$ of $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}=\mathsf Q\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\mathsf Q^{\!\top}$ are the divergence-free mass fractions of the whitened directions.
Directions with $\mu_i\le\tau_{\rm drop}=10^{-6}$ are discarded; with the $r'$ remaining ones, $\mathsf W=\mathsf L^{-\top}\mathsf Q_{\rm kept}\mu_{\rm kept}^{-1/2}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-ritz}
  \mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\,\Av\,\mathsf D\mathsf W\,\mathsf y=\theta\,\mathsf y,\qquad c=\mathsf D\mathsf W\mathsf y.
\end{equation}
% src: src/models/hcurl.py projected_eigh (mass_ridge 1e-8, drop_tol 1e-6); docs/20 §2
The discarded directions receive $\theta=+\infty$ (a large finite number) and sort last.
The $K$ smallest values give $f_k=c_0\sqrt{\theta_k}/(2\pi s)$, with $s$ the length scale of the normalised coordinates (eigenvalues scale as $s^{-2}$), and the $\Mmat$-orthonormal fields $\E_k=\Pmat\Vmat c_k$.
Both symmetric eigendecompositions use a backward pass in which the factor $1/(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ of the standard eigenvector gradient~\cite{ionescu2015matrix,seeger2017autodiff} is replaced by the Lorentzian-broadened $\Delta/(\Delta^2+\varepsilon_{\rm b})$, $\varepsilon_{\rm b}=10^{-4}$, because cavities have (near-)degenerate dipole and quadrupole pairs.
% src: src/models/layers.py BroadenedEigh; configs/field_3d.yaml (eig_broadening 1e-4)

\subsection{Upper-bound guarantee}\label{sec:04-bound}

Let $\lamh_1\le\lamh_2\le\dots$ be the positive eigenvalues of $\Kmat x=\lambda\Mmat x$ on $\Vh$ (wall DOFs removed); $f=c_0\sqrt\lambda/(2\pi s)$ is monotone in $\lambda$, so all statements hold for both.
We assume:
\begin{description}
\item[(A)] the columns of $\Gmat$ span $\ker\Kmat$ on $\Vh$, and $\Mmat$ is positive definite.
\end{description}
(A) is the exactness of the discrete sequence $\Sh\xrightarrow{\ \nabla\ }\Vh\xrightarrow{\ \curl\ }$ of the conforming spaces used here, with one extra potential per additional wall component~\cite{hiptmair2002finite,arnold2006finite}.
Under (A), $\Vh=\ker\Kmat\oplus W_h$ is an $\Mmat$-orthogonal splitting, $\Kmat$ is positive definite on $W_h$, and the positive eigenvalues of the full problem are those of the pencil restricted to $W_h$ (if $\Kmat x=\lambda\Mmat x$, $\lambda>0$, then $\bm g^{\!\top}\Mmat x=\lambda^{-1}\bm g^{\!\top}\Kmat x=0$ for all $\bm g\in\ker\Kmat$, so $x\in W_h$; dimensions match).
Courant--Fischer~\cite{parlett1998symmetric} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-minmax}
  \lamh_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset W_h\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Ritz values are upper bounds]\label{prop:upper-bound}
Assume (A) and that $\mathsf Z$ solves $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ exactly.
Let $\Vmat\in\R^{n\times m}$ be arbitrary with zero wall rows and let $r'$ be the number of directions kept by the Ritz layer.
Then there is a subspace $U'\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)\subset W_h$ of dimension $r'$ whose Rayleigh--Ritz values for $(\Kmat,\Mmat)$ are the finite outputs $\theta_1\le\dots\le\theta_{r'}$ of \eqref{eq:04-ritz}, and
\[
  \theta_k\ \ge\ \lamh_k\ >\ 0\qquad(k=1,\dots,r'),
\]
while $\theta_k=+\infty$ for $k>r'$.
Hence no spurious zero mode occurs and $f_k\ge f_{h,k}$ for every network weight and input geometry, before and after training.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1.} $\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Pmat\Vmat=\mathsf B-\Kp\mathsf Z=0$, so by (A) every column of $\Pmat\Vmat$ is $\Mmat$-orthogonal to $\ker\Kmat$, i.e.\ lies in $W_h$.

\emph{Step 2.} Since $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}=\mathsf Q\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,\mathsf Q^{\!\top}$, the matrix $\mathsf W$ satisfies $\mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf W=\mathsf I_{r'}$ (the ridge only enters $\mathsf L$).
With $\mathsf Y=\mathsf D\mathsf W$ and \eqref{eq:04-proj}, $(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)=\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Mdiv\mathsf Y=\mathsf I_{r'}$: the columns of $\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y$ are $\Mmat$-orthonormal, hence independent, and span $U'\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ with $\dim U'=r'$.
The matrix in \eqref{eq:04-ritz} is $\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Av\mathsf Y=(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)$, the Gram matrix of $\Kmat$ in an $\Mmat$-orthonormal basis of $U'$.
Its eigenvalues are therefore the Rayleigh--Ritz values on $U'$~\cite{parlett1998symmetric}, and by Courant--Fischer on $U'$
\[
  \theta_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset U'\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\]

\emph{Step 3.} $U'\subset W_h$, so this minimum runs over a subfamily of the subspaces in \eqref{eq:04-minmax}; hence $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$, and $\lamh_1>0$ by (A).
\end{proof}

The proposition concerns the \emph{discrete} eigenvalues of the space the model runs in.
Since $\lamh_k\to\lambda_k$ as the space is enriched~\cite{boffi2010finite}, but $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$ does not imply $\theta_k\ge\lambda_k$, we make no claim about the exact eigenvalue (in the label comparisons of \cref{sec:multifidelity} the order-two eigenvalues exceeded the order-three ones by $0.004$--$0.047\,\%$ in frequency).
% src: docs/34 §8.1 (frequency LF/HF - 1 = +0.004 ... +0.047 %)
Two consequences feed the losses: if $\Pmat\Vmat$ has full column rank, $\sum_{i\le m}\theta_i^{-1}\le\sum_{i\le m}\lamh_i^{-1}$ with equality iff $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ is spanned by the $m$ lowest eigenvectors (the left side is $\mathrm{tr}(\Av^{-1}\Mdiv)$); and the Ritz error $\theta_k-\lamh_k\ge0$ is controlled by the energy-norm best-approximation error of the eigenvectors in $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$~\cite{boffi2010finite}.

\begin{remark}[Inexact projection]\label{rem:04-inexact}
The proposition is exact only for an exact $\mathsf Z$.
CG returns $\tilde{\mathsf Z}=\mathsf Z+\delta$ with $\rho=\Kp\delta$, $\|\rho_{:,j}\|\le\tau\|\mathsf B_{:,j}\|$.
\emph{The numerator is unaffected:} $\Av=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat$ is evaluated directly and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$, so the residual gradient component $-\Gmat\delta$ of the computed field $\tilde{\Pmat}\Vmat$ carries no curl energy.
\emph{The denominator is perturbed:} the layer uses $\tilde{\mathsf M}_{\rm div}=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat-\mathsf B^{\!\top}\tilde{\mathsf Z}=\Mdiv+\mathsf E$, $\mathsf E=-\mathrm{sym}(\mathsf Z^{\!\top}\rho)$.
For a coefficient vector $c$, $c^{\!\top}\mathsf Ec=-\langle\Gmat\mathsf Zc,\Gmat\delta c\rangle_{\Mmat}$, and $\Gmat\mathsf Zc$ is the $\Mmat$-projection of $\Vmat c$ onto $\mathrm{range}\,\Gmat$, hence
\[
  \eta(c):=\frac{|c^{\!\top}\mathsf Ec|}{c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c}\le\frac{\|\rho c\|_{\Kp^{-1}}}{\mu(c)\,\|\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}},\qquad
  \mu(c)=\frac{\|\Pmat\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}^2}{\|\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}^2}.
\]
If $\bar\eta=\sup\eta(c)<1$ over the coefficient subspace kept by the Ritz layer, then $c^{\!\top}\tilde{\mathsf M}_{\rm div}c\le(1+\bar\eta)\,c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c$, every computed Rayleigh quotient is at least $(1+\bar\eta)^{-1}$ times that of the exact field $\Pmat\Vmat c\in W_h$, and Step~3 gives $\tilde\theta_k\ge\lamh_k/(1+\bar\eta)$.
An inexact solve can thus lower the Ritz values only by the relative amount $\bar\eta$, proportional to the CG residual and inversely proportional to the divergence-free fraction $\mu$, which is why directions with tiny $\mu$ are discarded rather than regularised.
(The returned field $\tilde{\Pmat}\Vmat c$ has the same numerator and the larger mass $c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c+\|\rho c\|_{\Kp^{-1}}^2$, so its own quotient is lowered by a second-order amount.)
We do not bound $\bar\eta$ a priori: $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$ is rigorous in exact arithmetic and observed for the implemented solver.
In every evaluation of \cref{sec:experiments} the predicted eigenvalues exceeded the discrete eigenvalue of the evaluated space, with smallest margins of $+0.006\,\%$ (native space), $+0.009\,\%$ (order three, refined mesh) and $+0.47\,\%$ (equal-cost study).
% src: docs/34 §4.1 (smallest difference +0.006 %), §4.2 (min. +0.009 %), §8.2 (at least +0.47 %)
Floating-point rounding is not covered by the argument.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Space independence]\label{rem:04-space}
The network sees only points, features and quadrature weights, and the Ritz layer needs only $\Kmat,\Mmat,\Gmat,\Kp$ and the maps $\Cmat,\mathsf F$ of the space in which it runs; \cref{prop:upper-bound} does not depend on how $\Vmat$ was produced.
The same weights can therefore be evaluated on another conforming space (order three on the same mesh, or a uniformly refined mesh) by rebuilding these operators, and the Ritz values are upper bounds of the discrete eigenvalues of \emph{that} space; measured margin $\ge+0.009\,\%$ in every case (\cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: docs/34 §4.2
Accuracy does not improve in the larger space, since the learned fields are the same smooth fields and the token density ($24$ instead of $14$ points per element at order three) differs from training.
% src: docs/34 §4.2 ("Doğruluk iyileşmiyor")
\end{remark}

\subsection{Losses}\label{sec:04-loss}

Let $\mathsf T=[t_1,\dots,t_{n_{\rm tgt}}]$ be the stored target fields on $\Vh$ (label eigenvectors projected onto the model space, \cref{sec:data}) and, for $\mathsf N\in\{\Mmat,\Kmat\}$, let $\mathsf G_{\mathsf N}$ be the $\mathsf N$-Gram matrix of $[\Pmat\Vmat\,|\,\mathsf T]$.
It is assembled without forming $\Pmat\Vmat$ and without a second CG solve: the $\Pmat\Vmat$ block is $\Mdiv$ or $\Av$, the cross blocks are $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf T-\mathsf Z^{\!\top}\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf T$ and $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\mathsf T$, valid for any $\mathsf T$.

\paragraph{Span loss.}
With $\Pi_{\mathsf N}$ the $\mathsf N$-orthogonal projector onto $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ (a Cholesky solve of the Jacobi-scaled Gram matrix with relative ridge $10^{-9}$), the uncaptured fraction of target $t_k$ is $r_k^{\mathsf N}=1-\|\Pi_{\mathsf N}t_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2/\|t_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2\in[0,1]$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-span}
  \mathcal L_{\rm span}=\frac1{n_{\rm tgt}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm tgt}}\sqrt{\tfrac12\big(r_k^{\Mmat}+r_k^{\Kmat}\big)+10^{-8}}.
\end{equation}
% src: src/training/lightning_module.py span_residual, _span_terms (span_norm both, span_root true, span_ridge 1e-9, eps 1e-8)
$\sqrt{r_k^{\Mmat}}$ and $\sqrt{r_k^{\Kmat}}$ are the relative best-approximation errors in the mass and energy norms; the square root keeps the gradient from vanishing as the span becomes accurate.
$\mathcal L_{\rm span}$ is invariant under invertible recombination of the columns of $\Vmat$ and under sign changes and rotations in degenerate target clusters, so it needs no eigenvalue matching or cluster detection.

\paragraph{Other terms.}
The self-supervised term $\mathcal L_{\rm ss}=-\log\mathrm{tr}(\Av^{-1}\Mdiv)=-\log\sum_i\theta_i^{-1}$ needs no labels; by the first consequence of \cref{prop:upper-bound} it is bounded below by $-\log\sum_{i\le m}\lamh_i^{-1}$, with equality exactly for the lowest-$m$ eigenspace.
Being built from the projected mass, it cannot be lowered by a nearly-gradient column; the unprojected compliance was measured to blow up by more than $10^3$ in such a case.
% src: docs/20 §3 (selfsup: "Neredeyse-gradyan bir kolonda projeksiyonsuz compliance >10^3x patlar (test edildi)")
The conditioning term $\mathcal L_{\rm ortho}=\frac1{m(m-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}\Mdiv_{ij}^2/(\Mdiv_{ii}\Mdiv_{jj})$ penalises collinear learned fields.
The frequency term $\mathcal L_{\rm freq}=\frac1K\sum_{k}(z_k-z_k^\star)^2$ uses $z=(f-\bar f)/\sigma_f$, the frequency in GHz standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the training labels; its gradient reaches the network through both eigendecompositions.
The total is $\mathcal L=\alpha_{\rm span}\mathcal L_{\rm span}+\alpha_{\rm ss}\mathcal L_{\rm ss}+\alpha_{\rm ortho}\mathcal L_{\rm ortho}+\alpha_{\rm freq}\mathcal L_{\rm freq}$ with $(\alpha_{\rm span},\alpha_{\rm ss},\alpha_{\rm ortho},\alpha_{\rm freq})=(1,0.01,0.01,0.1)$.
% src: configs/field_3d.yaml (span_weight 1.0, selfsup_weight 0.01, ortho_weight 0.01, freq_weight 0.1)
A loss on the figures of merit exists in the code but is disabled for this model.

\paragraph{Fidelity weights.}
Every sample carries the fidelity of its label, $\mathrm{fid}\in\{\LF,\HF\}$ (\cref{sec:multifidelity}).
With per-sample values $\ell_b$ of a term, a batch of size $B$ uses $\frac1B\sum_b\omega_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\ell_b$ for the span term and $\frac1B\sum_b\omega_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\beta_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\ell_b$ for the frequency term: $\omega=(\omega_\LF,\omega_\HF)$ weights both and $\beta$ acts on the frequency term only, because $\HF$ frequencies can lie below the lowest value the order-two Ritz layer can reach whereas $\LF$ frequencies are its own discrete eigenvalues.
The defaults are $\omega=\beta=(1,1)$.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:04-cost}

The trunk costs $O(Nd^2L)$ time and $O(Nd)$ memory, linear in the number of tokens; with gradient checkpointing (recomputing the encoder and each attention and feed-forward half in the backward pass) the peak process memory of training fell from $9.9$ to $3.8$\,GB with bit-identical gradients.
% src: docs/28 §4 (training memory: 9.9 GB -> 3.8 GB, gradients bit-identical)
The mesh-dependent part dominates: a CG iteration costs $O(\mathrm{nnz}(\Kp)\,m)$ in float64, the iteration count grows roughly like $h^{-1}$ (in the lowest-order variant from $60$ to $170$ iterations while the edge count grew from $2\,053$ to $47\,919$), and the backward pass adds one more solve; the $m\times m$ Ritz layer is negligible.
% src: docs/20 §5 (PCG iterations 60 ... 170 for N_e 2053 ... 47919)
On a curved pillbox-with-pipes mesh of $10.5$k tetrahedra, order two has $112$k DOFs, $41$k potentials and $148$k tokens, and order three $282$k DOFs, $100$k potentials and $253$k tokens; assembling the operators takes $7$--$10$\,s and $37$\,s on one thread.
% src: docs/28 §4 table (netgen curvaturesafety 2: 10.5k tets, p2 112k DOF / 41k potentials / 148k tokens / 7-10 s; p3 282k / 100k / 253k / 37 s)
For a mesh of $13$k tetrahedra ($183$k tokens, order two) on four CPU threads we measured $4.2$\,s for operator assembly, $0.34$\,s for batch collation and about $22$\,s per training step.
% src: docs/28 §4 "Measured per step" (13k tets, 183k tokens, CPU, 4 threads)
Operators are rebuilt per sample rather than stored, since they would take about $0.4$\,GB per sample at order two against $1.4$\,MB for the curved mesh, and order three is impractical for training on a CPU at these sizes.
