\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

The model never predicts a field or an eigenvalue directly.
It predicts $m$ vector fields $\bm\psi_1,\dots,\bm\psi_m$ on the geometry, maps them by a fixed linear operator into the discrete space $\Vh$ of \cref{sec:formulation}, removes their gradient part, and lets a Rayleigh--Ritz step select the $K$ best combinations.
Everything between the network and the output is a fixed, mesh-dependent linear-algebra layer; the only learned object is the field-valued network.
\Cref{fig:pipeline} gives the overview.

\subsection{Tokens and trunk}\label{sec:04-trunk}

\paragraph{Tokens.}
Let $\mathcal T_h$ be the curved mesh of $\Omega$ with $N_t$ elements and let $\Vh$ be the Nédélec space of order $p$ on it (\cref{sec:formulation}).
On every element we use the same reference quadrature rule of degree $2p$.
The tokens of the trunk are the $N=N_t\,n_q$ quadrature points $\bm x_q$ of all elements, with $n_q=14$ at $p=2$ and $n_q=24$ at $p=3$.
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py _quadrature (IntegrationRule(TET, 2*order)); n_q = 14, 24 checked with NGSolve 6.2; docs/34 §4.2
Quadrature-point tokens were chosen because coarse curved meshes can have no interior vertex at all.
An earlier variant with vertex tokens and barycentric interpolation reached a relative eigenvalue error of $0.8$--$1.1\times10^{-4}$ ($p=2$) after 2000 steps of single-sample overfitting, whereas quadrature-point tokens reached $10^{-6}$--$9\times10^{-6}$ after 1250 steps (\cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: docs/28 §3 (0.8-1.1e-4 / 2000 steps vs 1e-6 - 9e-6 / 1250 steps, p = 2)
Each token carries nine features computed on the curved geometry in normalised coordinates: the position $\bm x_q$, the distance to the wall, the unit vector to the nearest wall point, the point volume (quadrature weight divided by its maximum) and the torsion function $\chi$ ($-\Delta\chi=1$ in $\Omega$, $\chi=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, solved in the $H^1_0$ space of order $p+1$ on the same curved mesh and divided by its maximum).
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py point_features, wall_features (x,y,z, dist, dir x3, volume, torsion); docs/28 §2

\paragraph{Mass-aware linear attention.}
Let $\gamma(\bm\xi)=\sqrt{2/d_{\rm f}}\,[\cos(\mathsf B_{\rm F}\bm\xi),\sin(\mathsf B_{\rm F}\bm\xi)]$ be a random Fourier feature map with a frozen Gaussian matrix $\mathsf B_{\rm F}$~\cite{rahimi2007random,tancik2020fourier}, applied to the normalised coordinates.
The embedding is $\bm h^0_q=\mathrm{MLP}([\gamma(\bm x_q)\,\Vert\,\bm y_q])$ with the feature vector $\bm y_q\in\R^9$.
Each of the $L$ trunk blocks is a pre-norm residual block, $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{Attn}_w(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$ followed by $\bm h\leftarrow\bm h+\mathrm{FFN}(\mathrm{LN}\,\bm h)$, where, per head,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-attn}
  [\mathrm{Attn}_w(\bm h)]_i=\frac{\sum_{q} w_q\,\phi(\mathsf A_q\bm h_i)^{\!\top}\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)\;\mathsf A_v\bm h_q}{\sum_{q} w_q\,\phi(\mathsf A_q\bm h_i)^{\!\top}\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)+\varepsilon_{\rm att}},\qquad \phi=\mathrm{elu}+1,
\end{equation}
the linear attention of Katharopoulos et al.~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers} with a weight $w_q$ on every key.
The weights are the quadrature weights ($|\det J|$ times the reference weight $\hat w_q$) normalised to $\sum_q w_q=1$, and $\varepsilon_{\rm att}=10^{-6}$.
% src: src/models/layers.py LinearAttention (eps=1e-6), mass_weights; src/data/field_dataset.py (Area = quadrature weights)
Both sums in \eqref{eq:04-attn} are element quadratures of $\int\kappa(\bm h_i,\bm h(\bm y))\,\mathsf A_v\bm h(\bm y)\,\mathrm d\bm y$ and $\int\kappa(\bm h_i,\bm h(\bm y))\,\mathrm d\bm y$ with a separable kernel $\kappa$: a global integral operator evaluated with the quadrature of the mesh itself, an interpretation shared with the Galerkin-type attention of Cao~\cite{cao2021choose}.
Refining the mesh changes the sums by quadrature error only.
The cost is $O(Nd^2)$ per block instead of $O(N^2d)$, because $\sum_q w_q\phi(\mathsf A_k\bm h_q)(\mathsf A_v\bm h_q)^{\!\top}$ is formed once.
(The normalisation $\sum_q w_q=1$ cancels in the ratio except through $\varepsilon_{\rm att}$.)
The head is a two-layer MLP, $\bm\psi_{1..m}(\bm x_q)=\mathrm{head}([\bm h^L_q\,\Vert\,\gamma(\bm x_q)])\in(\R^3)^m$.
It depends on the point and, through the trunk, on the whole geometry, and not on the numbering of mesh entities, so the $\bm\psi_j$ are geometric vector fields.
Default sizes are embedding dimension $d=128$, $L=4$ blocks, $4$ heads, $m=24$ fields, $K=6$ modes and a $64$-dimensional Fourier map with length scale $0.1$; the small-scale experiments use a reduced configuration (\cref{sec:experiments}).
% src: configs/field_3d.yaml (embed_dim 128, n_layers 4, n_heads 4, n_basis 24, num_field_modes 6, rff_dim 64, rff_length_scale 0.1)

\subsection{From point values to degrees of freedom}\label{sec:04-map}

The point values $\bm\psi_j(\bm x_q)$ become DOFs of $\Vh$ through a fixed linear map in two stages.
\emph{(i) Element fit.}
Let $\mathbb P_p$ be the polynomials of degree $\le p$ in the reference coordinates (dimension $n_\ell=\binom{p+3}{3}$), $\Phi_t\in\R^{n_q\times n_\ell}$ a basis evaluated at the quadrature points of element $t$, and $\mathsf W_t=\mathrm{diag}\big(|\det J_t(\hat{\bm x}_q)|\,\hat w_q\big)$.
Each Cartesian component of $\bm\psi_j$ is fitted by weighted least squares,
$\bm a_{t,c,j}=\mathsf F_t\,\bm\psi_{t,c,j}$ with $\mathsf F_t=(\Phi_t^{\!\top}\mathsf W_t\Phi_t)^{-1}\Phi_t^{\!\top}\mathsf W_t$,
which is exact on $\mathbb P_p$ because $n_q\ge n_\ell$ ($14\ge10$ at $p=2$, $24\ge20$ at $p=3$).
% src: src/data_gen/highorder.py field_operators (P = solve(A, Phi^T W), W = detJ*wq); n_l = dim P_p = 10, 20
\emph{(ii) Interpolation into $\Vh$.}
The element-wise polynomial (discontinuous) field is mapped into $\Vh$ by a fixed sparse operator $\Cmat$.
In the implementation $\Cmat$ is the NGSolve conversion operator~\cite{schoeberl2014cpp11} from the discontinuous vector-valued space to $\Vh$; it applies the dual basis of $\Vh$ element by element and averages the DOFs shared between elements.
% src: ngsolve ConvertOperator docstring ("interpolation operator defined by element-wise application of dual shapes (and averaging between elements)"); docs/28 §2 calls it projection-based interpolation
It plays the role of a projection-based interpolation operator~\cite{demkowicz2005projection}; the only properties we use are linearity and element-wise locality.
Setting the wall DOFs to zero enforces $\bm n\times\E=0$ structurally.
With $\mathsf I_{\rm int}$ the diagonal $0/1$ matrix of non-wall DOFs and $\mathsf F=\mathrm{blockdiag}(\mathsf F_t)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-V}
  \Vmat_{:,j}=\mathsf I_{\rm int}\,\Cmat\,\mathsf F\,\bm\psi_j,\qquad \Vmat\in\R^{n\times m}.
\end{equation}
$\Vmat$ is linear in $\bm\psi$, so gradients flow through it as two sparse products.
The operators $\Kmat,\Mmat,\Gmat,\Kp,\Cmat,\mathsf F$ are assembled once per geometry with one explicit integration rule on the curved elements, so that the forms of the model and of the label solver coincide.
% src: AGENTS.md invariants (highorder.volume_measure); docs/28 §2 numerical note

\subsection{Kernel projection}\label{sec:04-proj}

The gradients $\nabla\Sh$ (together with one potential per extra connected component of the wall) span the kernel of $\Kmat$ on $\Vh$.
A generic $\Vmat$ contains a large gradient part, which the Rayleigh quotient would report as spurious modes with $\theta\approx0$; in a smoke test of the lowest-order variant about $60\,\%$ of the mass of the raw basis was gradient.
% src: docs/20 §6 (grad_frac 0.59, 30 geometries, N0 model)
With $\mathsf B=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat$ and $\mathsf Z=\Kp^{-1}\mathsf B$ we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-proj}
  \Pmat\Vmat=\Vmat-\Gmat\mathsf Z,\qquad
  \Av:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat,\qquad
  \Mdiv:=(\Pmat\Vmat)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat)=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat-\mathsf B^{\!\top}\mathsf Z .
\end{equation}
The second identity holds because $\Kmat\Gmat=0$, the third because $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ (a Schur complement).
$\Pmat\Vmat$ is the $\Mmat$-orthogonal projection of $\Vmat$ onto the discretely divergence-free subspace $W_h=\{\bm v\in\Vh:\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat=0\}$, and neither Gram matrix requires $\Pmat\Vmat$ to be formed.
$\Kp=\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Gmat$ is symmetric positive definite ($\Gmat$ has full column rank), so in this E formulation no zero-mean handling is needed.

$\mathsf Z$ is computed by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients, batched over samples and over the $m$ right-hand sides, each (sample, column) pair having its own step sizes.
The iteration stops when every column satisfies $\|\mathsf r_j\|_2\le\tau\|\mathsf B_{:,j}\|_2$ with $\tau=10^{-8}$ (checked every ten iterations, at most $3000$ iterations, with a warning if the limit is reached).
% src: src/models/hcurl.py _pcg (check_every=10, thr = tol*||rhs||); configs/field_3d.yaml (kp_tol 1e-8, kp_maxiter 3000)
The solve runs in float64: in float32 the iteration did not converge and $\Mdiv$ was off by about $79\,\%$ in a test.
We attribute this to cancellation in the Schur complement when $\Vmat$ has a large gradient part.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py project_basis comment ("float32 CG does not converge (M_div off by ~79%, docs/21 B3)"); AGENTS.md invariants
The map $\mathsf B\mapsto\mathsf Z$ is the symmetric linear operator $\Kp^{-1}$, so its backward pass is one more solve with the same matrix, $\bar{\mathsf B}=\Kp^{-1}\bar{\mathsf Z}$.
We implement it as a custom autograd function in PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} instead of differentiating through the iterations.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py KpSolve

\subsection{Rank-revealing Rayleigh--Ritz layer}\label{sec:04-ritz}

The Ritz problem on $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ is $\Av\,c=\theta\,\Mdiv\,c$.
It is degenerate exactly when a combination $\Vmat c$ is a pure gradient: then $\Pmat\Vmat c=0$ and both $\Mdiv c$ and $\Av c$ vanish.
A ridge would assign such directions an arbitrary $\theta$ inside the spectrum, and the losses of \cref{sec:04-loss} do not see gradient content, so training would not push them away.
We therefore reveal the rank first.
Let $\mathsf D=\mathrm{diag}(\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat)^{-1/2}$ and $\mathsf D(\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat)\mathsf D+\varepsilon_{\rm M}\mathsf I=\mathsf L\mathsf L^{\!\top}$ with $\varepsilon_{\rm M}=10^{-8}$.
The eigenvalues $\mu_i\in[0,1]$ of $\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}=\mathsf Q\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,\mathsf Q^{\!\top}$ are the divergence-free mass fractions of the whitened directions.
Directions with $\mu_i\le\tau_{\rm drop}=10^{-6}$ are discarded.
With the $r'$ remaining ones, $\mathsf W=\mathsf L^{-\top}\mathsf Q_{\rm kept}\,\mu_{\rm kept}^{-1/2}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-ritz}
  \mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\,\Av\,\mathsf D\mathsf W\,\mathsf y=\theta\,\mathsf y,\qquad c=\mathsf D\mathsf W\mathsf y,
\end{equation}
a standard symmetric eigenproblem of size $r'\le m$.
% src: src/models/hcurl.py projected_eigh (mass_ridge 1e-8, drop_tol 1e-6); docs/20 §2
The discarded directions receive $\theta=+\infty$ (implemented as a large finite number) and sort last.
The $K$ smallest values give the predicted frequencies $f_k=c_0\sqrt{\theta_k}/(2\pi s)$, where $s$ is the length scale (in metres) of the normalised coordinates (eigenvalues scale as $s^{-2}$), and the fields $\E_k=\Pmat\Vmat c_k$, which are $\Mmat$-orthonormal by construction.
Both symmetric eigendecompositions use a custom backward pass in which the factor $1/(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)$ of the standard eigenvector gradient~\cite{ionescu2015matrix,seeger2017autodiff} is replaced by the Lorentzian-broadened $\Delta/(\Delta^2+\varepsilon_{\rm b})$, $\varepsilon_{\rm b}=10^{-4}$.
Without this the gradient is infinite for the (near-)degenerate pairs that are the normal case in cavities (dipole and quadrupole pairs).
% src: src/models/layers.py BroadenedEigh; configs/field_3d.yaml (eig_broadening 1e-4)

\subsection{Upper-bound guarantee}\label{sec:04-bound}

Let $\lamh_1\le\lamh_2\le\dots$ be the positive eigenvalues of $\Kmat x=\lambda\Mmat x$ on $\Vh$ (wall DOFs removed).
Frequencies are the monotone image $f=c_0\sqrt\lambda/(2\pi s)$, so everything below holds for $\lambda$ and for $f$ alike.
We use one standing assumption on the space.
\begin{description}
\item[(A)] The columns of $\Gmat$ span $\ker\Kmat$ on $\Vh$, and $\Mmat$ is positive definite.
\end{description}
(A) is the exactness of the discrete sequence $\Sh\xrightarrow{\ \nabla\ }\Vh\xrightarrow{\ \curl\ }$ for the conforming spaces used here, with one extra potential for every additional connected component of $\partial\Omega$~\cite{hiptmair2002finite,arnold2006finite}.
Under (A), $\Vh=\ker\Kmat\oplus W_h$ is an $\Mmat$-orthogonal splitting and $\Kmat$ is positive definite on $W_h$.
Moreover the positive eigenvalues of the full problem are exactly the eigenvalues of the pencil restricted to $W_h$: if $\Kmat x=\lambda\Mmat x$ with $\lambda>0$ then $\bm g^{\!\top}\Mmat x=\lambda^{-1}\bm g^{\!\top}\Kmat x=0$ for every $\bm g\in\ker\Kmat$, so $x\in W_h$, and the dimensions match.
Courant--Fischer~\cite{parlett1998symmetric} therefore reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-minmax}
  \lamh_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset W_h\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Ritz values are upper bounds]\label{prop:upper-bound}
Assume (A) and that $\mathsf Z$ is the exact solution of $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$.
Let $\Vmat\in\R^{n\times m}$ be arbitrary with zero wall rows, and let $r'$ be the number of directions kept by the Ritz layer of \cref{sec:04-ritz}.
Then there is a subspace $U'\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)\subset W_h$ of dimension $r'$ such that the finite outputs $\theta_1\le\dots\le\theta_{r'}$ of \eqref{eq:04-ritz} are the Rayleigh--Ritz values of $(\Kmat,\Mmat)$ on $U'$, and
\[
  \theta_k\ \ge\ \lamh_k\ >\ 0\qquad(k=1,\dots,r'),
\]
while $\theta_k=+\infty$ for $k>r'$.
In particular no spurious zero mode occurs, and the predicted frequencies satisfy $f_k\ge f_{h,k}$ for every network weight, every input geometry, and before as well as after training.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: $\Pmat\Vmat$ lies in $W_h$.}
$\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Pmat\Vmat=\mathsf B-\Kp\mathsf Z=0$, so every column of $\Pmat\Vmat$ is $\Mmat$-orthogonal to $\mathrm{range}\,\Gmat=\ker\Kmat$ by (A).

\emph{Step 2: the layer computes Rayleigh--Ritz values on a subspace of $W_h$.}
By construction $\mathsf W^{\!\top}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf W=\mu_{\rm kept}^{-1/2}\mathsf Q_{\rm kept}^{\!\top}\big(\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf D\Mdiv\mathsf D\mathsf L^{-\top}\big)\mathsf Q_{\rm kept}\mu_{\rm kept}^{-1/2}=\mathsf I_{r'}$; the ridge $\varepsilon_{\rm M}$ enters only $\mathsf L$ and does not affect this identity.
Put $\mathsf Y=\mathsf D\mathsf W\in\R^{m\times r'}$.
By \eqref{eq:04-proj}, $(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Mmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)=\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Mdiv\mathsf Y=\mathsf I_{r'}$, so the columns of $\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y$ are $\Mmat$-orthonormal, hence linearly independent, and span a subspace $U'\subset\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ of dimension $r'$.
The matrix in \eqref{eq:04-ritz} equals $\mathsf Y^{\!\top}\Av\mathsf Y=(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)^{\!\top}\Kmat(\Pmat\Vmat\mathsf Y)$, the Gram matrix of $\Kmat$ in an $\Mmat$-orthonormal basis of $U'$.
Its eigenvalues are the Rayleigh--Ritz values of $(\Kmat,\Mmat)$ on $U'$~\cite{parlett1998symmetric}, and Courant--Fischer on $U'$ gives
\[
  \theta_k=\min_{\substack{S\subset U'\\ \dim S=k}}\ \max_{0\ne\bm v\in S}\frac{\bm v^{\!\top}\Kmat\bm v}{\bm v^{\!\top}\Mmat\bm v}.
\]
The $m-r'$ discarded directions contribute only the placeholder value.

\emph{Step 3: comparison.}
$U'\subset W_h$ by Step~1, so the minimum defining $\theta_k$ runs over a subfamily of the $k$-dimensional subspaces in \eqref{eq:04-minmax}; hence $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$.
Positivity is $\lamh_1>0$, which follows from (A).
\end{proof}

The statement concerns the \emph{discrete} eigenvalues of the space the model is run in.
Since $\Vh\subset H_0(\curl;\Omega)$ is conforming with property (A), $\lamh_k\to\lambda_k$ as the space is enriched (discrete compactness~\cite{boffi2010finite}), but $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$ does not by itself imply $\theta_k\ge\lambda_k$, and we make no claim about the exact eigenvalue.
Empirically, the discrete eigenvalues of the order-two model space exceeded the corresponding order-three label values in the label comparisons of \cref{sec:multifidelity} ($+0.004$ to $+0.047\,\%$ in frequency).
% src: docs/34 §8.1 (frequency LF/HF - 1 = +0.004 ... +0.047 %)
Two further consequences are used by the losses.
First, if $\Pmat\Vmat$ has full column rank then $\sum_{i\le m}\theta_i^{-1}\le\sum_{i\le m}\lamh_i^{-1}$, with equality iff $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ is spanned by the $m$ lowest eigenvectors; the left side equals $\mathrm{tr}(\Av^{-1}\Mdiv)$.
Second, the Ritz error $\theta_k-\lamh_k\ge0$ is controlled by the energy-norm best-approximation error of the eigenvectors in $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$~\cite{boffi2010finite}, which is the quantity the span loss below minimises.

\begin{remark}[Inexact projection]\label{rem:04-inexact}
The proposition is exact only if $\Kp\mathsf Z=\mathsf B$ holds exactly.
The CG solve returns $\tilde{\mathsf Z}=\mathsf Z+\delta$ with residual $\rho=\Kp\tilde{\mathsf Z}-\mathsf B=\Kp\delta$, $\|\rho_{:,j}\|\le\tau\|\mathsf B_{:,j}\|$.
\emph{The numerator is unaffected.} $\Av=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\Vmat$ is evaluated directly and $\Kmat\Gmat=0$, so the computed field $\tilde{\Pmat}\Vmat=\Pmat\Vmat-\Gmat\delta$ has the curl energy of the exact projection: the residual gradient component carries none.
\emph{The denominator is perturbed.} The layer uses $\tilde{\mathsf M}_{\rm div}=\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\Vmat-\mathsf B^{\!\top}\tilde{\mathsf Z}=\Mdiv+\mathsf E$ with $\mathsf E=-\mathrm{sym}(\mathsf Z^{\!\top}\rho)$.
For a coefficient vector $c$, $c^{\!\top}\mathsf Ec=-\langle\Gmat\mathsf Zc,\Gmat\delta c\rangle_{\Mmat}$, and $\Gmat\mathsf Zc$ is the $\Mmat$-projection of $\Vmat c$ onto $\mathrm{range}\,\Gmat$, so
\[
  |c^{\!\top}\mathsf Ec|\le\|\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}\,\|\rho c\|_{\Kp^{-1}},\qquad
  \eta(c):=\frac{|c^{\!\top}\mathsf Ec|}{c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c}\le\frac{\|\rho c\|_{\Kp^{-1}}}{\mu(c)\,\|\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}},\qquad
  \mu(c)=\frac{\|\Pmat\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}^2}{\|\Vmat c\|_{\Mmat}^2}.
\]
Let $\bar\eta=\sup\eta(c)<1$ over the coefficient subspace kept by the Ritz layer.
Then $c^{\!\top}\tilde{\mathsf M}_{\rm div}c\le(1+\bar\eta)\,c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c$, so every computed Rayleigh quotient is at least $(1+\bar\eta)^{-1}$ times the Rayleigh quotient of the exact field $\Pmat\Vmat c\in W_h$, and the min--max argument of Step~3 gives
\[
  \tilde\theta_k\ \ge\ \lamh_k/(1+\bar\eta).
\]
An inexact solve can therefore lower the Ritz values only by a relative amount $\bar\eta$, which is proportional to the CG residual and inversely proportional to the divergence-free fraction $\mu(c)$; this is why directions with tiny $\mu$ are discarded instead of regularised.
The returned field $\tilde{\Pmat}\Vmat c$ has the same numerator and the larger mass $c^{\!\top}\Mdiv c+\|\rho c\|_{\Kp^{-1}}^2$, so its own Rayleigh quotient is lowered by a relative amount that is second order in the residual.
We do not bound $\bar\eta$ a priori.
Hence $\theta_k\ge\lamh_k$ is a rigorous statement for exact arithmetic and an observed one for the implemented solver.
In every evaluation reported in \cref{sec:experiments} the predicted eigenvalues exceeded the discrete eigenvalue of the evaluated space, with smallest margins of $+0.006\,\%$ (native space), $+0.009\,\%$ (order three and refined mesh) and $+0.47\,\%$ (equal-cost study).
% src: docs/34 §4.1 (smallest difference +0.006 %), §4.2 (min. +0.009 %), §8.2 (at least +0.47 %)
Floating-point rounding in the Gram matrices is not covered by the argument.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Space independence]\label{rem:04-space}
The network sees only points, their features and their quadrature weights; the Ritz layer needs only $\Kmat,\Mmat,\Gmat,\Kp$ and the fixed maps $\Cmat,\mathsf F$ of the space in which it is evaluated.
Nothing in \cref{prop:upper-bound} depends on how $\Vmat$ was produced.
The same trained weights can therefore be evaluated on another conforming space, for example order three on the same mesh or a uniformly refined mesh, by rebuilding these operators; the Ritz values are then upper bounds of the discrete eigenvalues of \emph{that} space.
This was measured in \cref{sec:experiments}, with margin $\ge+0.009\,\%$ in every case.
% src: docs/34 §4.2
Accuracy does not improve in the larger space: the learned fields $\bm\psi_j$ are the same smooth fields, and the token density ($24$ instead of $14$ points per element at order three) and the point-volume feature differ from training.
% src: docs/34 §4.2 ("Doğruluk iyileşmiyor")
\end{remark}

\subsection{Losses}\label{sec:04-loss}

Let $\mathsf T=[t_1,\dots,t_{n_{\rm tgt}}]$ be the stored target fields on $\Vh$ (the label eigenvectors projected onto the model space, \cref{sec:data}).
For $\mathsf N\in\{\Mmat,\Kmat\}$ let $\mathsf G_{\mathsf N}$ be the Gram matrix of $[\Pmat\Vmat\,|\,\mathsf T]$ in the $\mathsf N$-inner product.
It is assembled without forming $\Pmat\Vmat$ and with the same $\mathsf Z$ (no second CG solve): the $\Pmat\Vmat$ block is $\Mdiv$ or $\Av$, and the cross blocks are $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf T-\mathsf Z^{\!\top}(\Gmat^{\!\top}\Mmat\mathsf T)$ and $\Vmat^{\!\top}\Kmat\mathsf T$, valid for any $\mathsf T$.

\paragraph{Span loss.}
The fraction of the target $t_k$ not captured by $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ in the $\mathsf N$-norm is
\[
  r_k^{\mathsf N}=1-\frac{\|\Pi_{\mathsf N}t_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2}{\|t_k\|_{\mathsf N}^2}\in[0,1],
\]
with $\Pi_{\mathsf N}$ the $\mathsf N$-orthogonal projector onto $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$, computed by a Cholesky solve of the Jacobi-scaled Gram matrix with a relative ridge of $10^{-9}$.
The span loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:04-span}
  \mathcal L_{\rm span}=\frac1{n_{\rm tgt}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\rm tgt}}\sqrt{\tfrac12\big(r_k^{\Mmat}+r_k^{\Kmat}\big)+10^{-8}}.
\end{equation}
% src: src/training/lightning_module.py span_residual, _span_terms (span_norm both, span_root true, span_ridge 1e-9, eps 1e-8)
$\sqrt{r_k^{\Mmat}}$ is the relative best-approximation error of $t_k$ in the $L^2$ norm and $\sqrt{r_k^{\Kmat}}$ the one in the energy norm; the square-root form keeps the gradient from vanishing as the span becomes accurate.
$\mathcal L_{\rm span}$ is invariant under any invertible recombination of the columns of $\Vmat$ and under sign changes and rotations inside degenerate target clusters, so it needs neither an eigenvalue matching nor a cluster detection.

\paragraph{Self-supervised term.}
$\mathcal L_{\rm ss}=-\log\mathrm{tr}(\Av^{-1}\Mdiv)=-\log\sum_i\theta_i^{-1}$, evaluated by a Cholesky solve.
It needs no labels.
By the first consequence of \cref{prop:upper-bound} it is bounded below by $-\log\sum_{i\le m}\lamh_i^{-1}$, with equality exactly when the span is that of the $m$ lowest eigenvectors.
Because it is built from the projected mass $\Mdiv$, a nearly-gradient column cannot lower it; the unprojected compliance was measured to blow up by more than a factor $10^3$ in such a case.
% src: docs/20 §3 (selfsup: "Neredeyse-gradyan bir kolonda projeksiyonsuz compliance >10^3x patlar (test edildi)")

\paragraph{Conditioning term.}
$\mathcal L_{\rm ortho}=\frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}\Mdiv_{ij}^2/(\Mdiv_{ii}\Mdiv_{jj})$, the mean squared off-diagonal mass correlation, penalises collinear learned fields and keeps $\spn(\Pmat\Vmat)$ well conditioned.

\paragraph{Frequency term.}
$\mathcal L_{\rm freq}=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K(z_k-z_k^\star)^2$, where $z=(f-\bar f)/\sigma_f$ are the frequencies in GHz standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the training labels.
Its gradient reaches the network through both eigendecompositions of \cref{sec:04-ritz}.

\paragraph{Total and fidelity weights.}
$\mathcal L=\alpha_{\rm span}\mathcal L_{\rm span}+\alpha_{\rm ss}\mathcal L_{\rm ss}+\alpha_{\rm ortho}\mathcal L_{\rm ortho}+\alpha_{\rm freq}\mathcal L_{\rm freq}$ with defaults $(\alpha_{\rm span},\alpha_{\rm ss},\alpha_{\rm ortho},\alpha_{\rm freq})=(1,\,0.01,\,0.01,\,0.1)$.
% src: configs/field_3d.yaml (span_weight 1.0, selfsup_weight 0.01, ortho_weight 0.01, freq_weight 0.1)
A loss on the figures of merit exists in the code but is disabled for this model.
Every training sample carries the fidelity of its label, $\mathrm{fid}\in\{\LF,\HF\}$ (\cref{sec:multifidelity}).
With per-sample values $\ell_b$ of a term, the span term of a batch is $\frac1B\sum_b\omega_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\ell_b$ and the frequency term is $\frac1B\sum_b\omega_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\beta_{\mathrm{fid}(b)}\ell_b$.
Here $\omega=(\omega_\LF,\omega_\HF)$ weights both terms and $\beta=(\beta_\LF,\beta_\HF)$ is an extra factor on the frequency term only.
The latter exists because $\HF$ frequencies can lie below the lowest value that the order-two Ritz layer can reach, whereas $\LF$ frequencies are exactly the discrete eigenvalues of the model space and are reachable by the Ritz values.
The defaults are $\omega=\beta=(1,1)$, i.e.\ no reweighting.
% src: docs/34 §2, configs/field_3d.yaml (fidelity_weights, fidelity_freq_scale), src/training/lightning_module.py _compute_loss_3d, _span_terms

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:04-cost}

The trunk costs $O(Nd^2L)$ time and $O(Nd)$ memory per geometry, linear in the number of tokens $N=n_qN_t$.
With gradient checkpointing (recomputing the encoder and each attention and feed-forward half in the backward pass) the peak process memory of training fell from $9.9$ to $3.8$\,GB with bit-identical gradients.
% src: docs/28 §4 (training memory: 9.9 GB -> 3.8 GB, gradients bit-identical)
The mesh-dependent part dominates.
Each CG iteration costs $O(\mathrm{nnz}(\Kp)\,m)$ in float64; the iteration count grows roughly like $h^{-1}$ (in the lowest-order variant it rose from $60$ to $170$ while the edge count grew from $2\,053$ to $47\,919$), and the backward pass adds one more solve.
% src: docs/20 §5 (PCG iterations 60 ... 170 for N_e 2053 ... 47919)
The Ritz layer is negligible: it handles $m\times m$ matrices.
Mesh size is therefore the critical parameter.
On a curved pillbox-with-pipes mesh of $10.5$k tetrahedra, order two has $112$k DOFs, $41$k potentials and $148$k tokens, and order three $282$k DOFs, $100$k potentials and $253$k tokens; the operators ($25$M and $105$M non-zeros in total) take $7$--$10$\,s and $37$\,s to assemble on one thread.
% src: docs/28 §4 table (netgen curvaturesafety 2: 10.5k tets, p2 112k DOF / 41k potentials / 148k tokens / 25M nnz / 7-10 s; p3 282k / 100k / 253k / 105M / 37 s)
For a mesh of $13$k tetrahedra ($183$k tokens, order two) on four CPU threads we measured $4.2$\,s for operator assembly, $0.34$\,s for batch collation and about $22$\,s per training step.
% src: docs/28 §4 "Measured per step" (13k tets, 183k tokens, CPU, 4 threads)
Operators are rebuilt per sample rather than stored: storing them would take about $0.4$\,GB per sample at order two, whereas the curved mesh itself is $1.4$\,MB.
% src: docs/28 §4 Storage (1.4 MB per sample; ~0.4 GB per sample at p2)
Order three is impractical for training on a CPU at these sizes.
