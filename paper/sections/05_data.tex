\section{Dataset generation}\label{sec:data}

A parametric generator draws a CAD solid; a curved tetrahedral mesh is built on it; the lowest modes are computed in $H(\mathrm{curl})$ of order three (high-fidelity labels, $\HF$); and the fields are projected onto the order-two space of the model mesh on which the network operates.
The full generator refines the order-three solution adaptively on a label mesh that may be finer than the model mesh.
\emph{All training experiments of this paper, however, use a one-level variant}: the budget of 150k label DOFs is exhausted by the first order-three solve, so the $\HF$ label is one order-three solve on the start mesh, the label mesh equals the model mesh, and neither the adaptive loop nor the label/model-mesh transfer of \cref{sec:05-adaptive,sec:05-transfer} is exercised by training.
% src: docs/34 §3 (adapt_iters = 1, adapt_stop = budget, max_ndof 150000), EDITOR_NOTES_2 decision 3
Geometry is built with OpenCascade~\cite{opencascade} through gmsh~\cite{geuzaine2009gmsh}; meshing, curving and all finite-element solves use Netgen~\cite{schoberl1997netgen} and NGSolve~\cite{schoberl2014cpp11} (version 6.2; a re-check of the shared start mesh on 6.2.2608 reproduced the identical LF and HF meshes with slightly different tetrahedron counts, so mesh sizes depend on the mesher version).
Beam pipes are capped by flat PEC walls, and the $\E$ formulation of \cref{sec:formulation} admits cavities with handles and floating conductors.

\subsection{Geometry families}\label{sec:05-geometry}

Eight families with CAD solids are available for field labels: multi-cell elliptical cavities, re-entrant (nose-cone) cells, pillbox cells with beam pipes, ridged rectangular cavities, constructive-solid-geometry composites, half-wave coaxial resonators, spoke cavities and drift-tube-linac tanks; five further families are held out of distribution.
Parameter ranges, the construction of elliptical half-cells and the published half-cells that the elliptical family accepts are in \cref{sec:A05-families,sec:A05-halfcell}.
\emph{Only one family, pillbox cells with beam pipes, is used in any training experiment; the other families are generated and labelled but untested in training.}
Parameters are drawn uniformly, the first 64 draws of sample $i$ from the $i$-th point of a scrambled Sobol' sequence~\cite{sobol1967distribution,virtanen2020scipy}; an optional smooth random deformation of the mesh is switched off in our experiments.
% src: dataset_generator_3d.py FAMILIES, parse_args; cavity_shapes.OOD_FAMILIES; BRIEF §1; _sample_rng (qmc.Sobol d=64, scramble=True); docs/19 §2 (deformation); docs/34 §3 (--deform_prob 0, family pillbox_pipes); referee 2.1 (05 long, one family used)

\paragraph{Fillet floor.}
A corner radius far below the cavity size forces a curved mesh into many tiny elements while changing frequencies little (for a pillbox cell with pipes the count falls from $45$k to $18$k tets as the floor rises from $0.02$ to $0.08$; \cref{sec:A05-counts}).
Every drawn corner radius is therefore raised to at least a fraction of the cavity size, and re-entrant edges left by boolean operations are rounded likewise; where OpenCascade fails the solid keeps its sharp edges and its surface peaks are stored as NaN.
The generator default for field labels is $0.05$; \emph{the experiments use $0.12$}, which rounds the geometry substantially and limits how far they generalise to sharp features.
% src: dataset_generator_3d.py min_fillet default 0.05 (labels=field); docs/34 §3 (--min_fillet 0.12); docs/29 §2.1 table; docs/30 §3 (rounding, NaN peaks); EDITOR_NOTES_2 decision 10

\subsection{Curved meshes}\label{sec:05-mesh}

Netgen generates tetrahedra (curvature safety $2$, grading $0.5$) and bends them onto the CAD surfaces to geometry order three.
Element quality, the ratio of the minimum to the maximum Jacobian determinant over an element ($\le0$: folded), hardly affects frequencies but strongly affects wall quantities: on one pillbox cell with pipes a 9k-tet mesh with minimum ratio $0.010$ gave frequencies within $10^{-5}$ but $\Qz$ off by $50\,\%$ and peaks off by $100$--$1600\times$.
Meshes below a ratio of $0.05$ are rejected, distorted elements are repaired locally, and draws with more than $30\,000$ tets are re-drawn (\cref{sec:A05-mesh}).

\subsection{Adaptive order-three labels}\label{sec:05-adaptive}

On each mesh the $\nmodes$ lowest modes (plus one) are computed in $H_0(\mathrm{curl})$ of order three by shift-invert Arnoldi iteration~\cite{lehoucq1998arpack} with the kernel projection after every solve; modes have unit $L^2$ norm, so $\int|\curl\E_k|^2=\lambda_k$.
With $\mathcal R$ the averaging interpolation into the continuous vector Lagrange space of the same order, the jumps of the discrete fields serve as error indicator, in the spirit of recovery estimators~\cite{zienkiewicz1987simple}:
\begin{align*}
\eta_{\mathrm{vol},e}^2 &= \sum_{k=1}^{\nmodes}\frac{\|\curl\E_k-\mathcal R\curl\E_k\|_e^2+\lambda_k\|\E_k-\mathcal R\E_k\|_e^2}{\lambda_k},\\
\eta_{\mathrm{wall},e}^2 &= \sum_{k=1}^{\nmodes}\Bigl(\frac{\int_{\partial e\cap\bdry}|\curl\E_k-\mathcal R\curl\E_k|^2}{\int_{\bdry}|\curl\E_k|^2}+\frac{\int_{\partial e\cap\bdry}|\E_k-\mathcal R\E_k|^2}{\int_{\bdry}|\E_k|^2}\Bigr).
\end{align*}
% src: docs/30 §2; adaptive.py indicator
The volume term estimates the relative eigenvalue error (second order in the field error); wall loss and peaks are first order in the field error at the wall, hence the separately normalised wall term, added with equal weight.
% src: docs/30 §2

\paragraph{Remeshing and stopping.}
Bisection explodes on curvature-graded meshes (marking 200 of 8109 tets gave $29$k tets), so each iteration builds a new mesh from an error-equidistributing size field (new size $h_e(\gamma\,\overline{\eta^2}/\eta_e^2)^{1/(2p)}$ on element $e$, with $\eta_e^2$ the sum of the two terms, $\gamma=0.25$, $p$ the order), passed to Netgen as accumulating size restrictions (\cref{sec:A05-mesh}).
The start mesh is deliberately coarse; its selection rule is shared with the low-fidelity labels (\cref{sec:multifidelity}).
Refinement stops when cluster-mean frequency and figures of merit change by less than $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-3}$ (relative) between two levels, when the next mesh would exceed the DOF budget (default 900k label DOFs, about \SI{12}{GB}), or after eight levels.
This compares successive levels and is not an error bound; the accuracy targets (frequency $10^{-5}$, $\Qz$, $G$, $\RoQ$ $0.2\,\%$, peaks $1\,\%$) are checked in \cref{sec:05-validation}.
In the experiments the loop stops at the DOF budget after the first order-three solve (\cref{sec:data}).

\subsection{Label mesh, model mesh and storage}\label{sec:05-transfer}

In the full generator the \emph{label mesh} is the finest adaptive level and the \emph{model mesh} the finest level with at most $10\,000$ tets (about $10^5$ order-two DOFs per geometry); the label fields are $L^2$-projected onto the order-two space of the model mesh, $\mathsf M_2\mathsf u_2=\int\mathbf v_2\cdot\E_3$, and per mode we store $\text{proj\_err}=\|\E_3-\E_2\|/\|\E_3\|$ and the Rayleigh quotient of $\E_2$ in the model space.
In the experiments both meshes coincide (the projection is then the exact best approximation, the spaces being nested).
% src: docs/30 §5, §6 (model_max_elements 10000, about 100k DOF); docs/34 §8.1 (LF mesh = HF model mesh on 8/8 and 6/6 meshes)
On two pillbox-with-pipes model meshes of the full generator (not used for training) $\text{proj\_err}$ was $0.18$--$0.54\,\%$ and the Rayleigh quotients of the projected modes exceeded the order-three frequencies by $0.04$--$0.9\,\%$, which is the quality of the projected field \emph{target}.
By contrast, the discrete order-two eigenvalue on the model mesh lies at most $0.047\,\%$ above the $\HF$ frequency on the six test geometries ($0.033\,\%$ on four earlier geometries; \cref{sec:multifidelity}).
% src: docs/30 §5 (proj_err 1.8e-3–5.4e-3; RQ excess 4e-4–8.6e-3, meshes of 11300 and 13052 tets); docs/34 §4.1 (≤ 0.033 %), §8.1 (0.004–0.047 %); EDITOR_NOTES_2 decision 9

Each geometry is stored as the Netgen mesh file of the model mesh and the BREP of the meshed shape, with the frequencies, projected fields (single precision), figures of merit, axial field and the wall-distance token features of \cref{sec:method}: about \SI{9}{MB} for ten modes (the production setting; the network of \cref{sec:experiments} predicts $K=4$).
% src: docs/29 §1, §4
Because mesh generation is not bit-reproducible, the stored mesh is the record of a sample; the generator verifies that it rebuilds the same DOF numbering and the loader that the stored fields keep their Rayleigh quotients (relative $10^{-5}$) under the rebuilt operators (\cref{sec:A05-reload}).
% src: docs/29 §3.3, §6; docs/30 §5 (single_level, _check_reload)

\subsection{Figures of merit}\label{sec:05-qoi}

Figures of merit follow \cref{sec:formulation} and are evaluated directly from the order-three fields (copper, $\sigma=\SI{5.8e7}{S/m}$, $U=\SI{1}{J}$, wall fields from the adjacent volume element); $\Epk$ and $\Bpk$ are maxima over wall integration points.
The beam axis and the accelerating length $\Lacc$ (beam pipes excluded) are family-specific (\cref{sec:A03-qoi}); using the axis chord for $\Lacc$, as an earlier version did, inflated $\Epk/\Eacc$ and $\Bpk/\Eacc$ by a median $1.2$--$2.4\times$.
The axial field is stored, so $T$ and $\RoQ$ follow at any velocity $\beta$.

\subsection{Validation}\label{sec:05-validation}

The adaptive label path (remeshing, order-three solve, figures of merit) is compared with closed forms on a copper pillbox ($R=\SI{40}{mm}$, $L=\SI{50}{mm}$; six lowest modes, TM$_{010}$ figures of merit) in \cref{tab:05-validation}; \cref{fig:05-modes} shows the two lowest modes of an $\HF$ label of the experimental family.
This pillbox is smooth, without beam pipes or fillets, so the check validates the solver and the figures of merit but \emph{not} the labels of the experimental family (pillbox cells with pipes and fillets), for which no closed form exists.
The wall term reduces the $\Qz$ and $\Bpk$ errors about threefold at slightly fewer DOFs, at the price of a larger (still far inside the target) $\RoQ$ error; the $\Epk/\Eacc$ error stalls at $2\cdot10^{-4}$ because the maximum is sampled at wall integration points.
On a pillbox cell \emph{with} pipes, $\Bpk$ changed by $4\,\%$ between the two finest levels at a 400k-DOF limit, so the wall quantities of $\HF$ labels of that family are uncertain at the percent level.
We have not yet cross-validated against an independent solver.

\subsection{Cost per sample and generator speed-ups}\label{sec:05-cost}

\emph{Cost basis.} In the experiments the $\HF$ label is a single order-three solve on the start mesh (150k-DOF budget); on four earlier samples, including meshing, it took \SI{157}{s} on average (\SI{120}{s}--\SI{209}{s}; two workers of one thread on a shared four-core VM).
Its cost is $2.8$--$4.3\times$ that of a single order-two solve on the same mesh, depending on machine load (conditions in \cref{sec:multifidelity}); this is an order-three versus order-two comparison on one mesh, not a comparison with the adaptive generator, which needs about three to four single solves.
% src: docs/34 §3 (172.5, 119.9, 208.7, 125.7 s), §8.1 (2.8–4.3); docs/30 §6 (3–4x one solve); EDITOR_NOTES_2 decision 4
The generator was optimised without changing the labels (frequencies unchanged to $2\cdot10^{-15}$): a full adaptive sample at a 450k-DOF budget fell from \SI{664}{s} to \SI{433}{s} on two threads (\cref{sec:A05-speedup}).
