\section{Figures of merit: conventions and details}\label{sec:A03-qoi}
% src: docs/24 §0.2, §1.2, §1.3; src/qoi/operators.py beam_axis, acceleration_length; src/data_gen/highorder.py mode_qoi; docs/29 §2.2
\paragraph{Beam axis and accelerating length.}
This is the single place where the convention is stated; the data section refers to it. All geometry families are built with the beam along $z$, and the default axis is the line $x=y=0$.
Exceptions: for the half-wave coaxial resonator the beam crosses the coaxial line along $x$ at mid-length; for the ridged box it runs along $x$ through the ridge gap; for the plain box along $z$
through the box centre. $\Lacc$ is the inner length of the cavity along the axis, beam pipes excluded: the sum of the cell lengths for elliptical cavities; the cell length for pillbox and re-entrant
cells; the tank length for spoke and drift-tube structures; the outer diameter for the coaxial resonator; the box length for the ridged and plain boxes. For families without a beam it is the chord
of the axis inside $\Omega$, and $\RoQ$ is then a defined functional and not an accelerator figure. Axis and $\Lacc$ follow the nominal geometry parameters, so on smoothly deformed samples they
are those of the undeformed shape. Labels and model-side figures use the same definition.

\paragraph{Eigenpair consistency.}
For a discrete eigenpair, $\tfrac12\mu_0\!\int|\Hf|^2$ computed from $\E$ and the eigenfrequency equals $\tfrac12\varepsilon_0\!\int|\E|^2$, because
$\omega^2=c_0^2\lambda/s^2$ with $\lambda$ the Rayleigh quotient. For a field and frequency that are not an eigenpair the two differ by the
Rayleigh-quotient mismatch; we take $U$ from $\E$ and use the frequency explicitly in $\Hf=(j/\omega\mu_0)\curl\E$.

\paragraph{Evaluation on high-order fields.}
The functionals are evaluated directly on the polynomial fields. Wall values are taken from the adjacent volume element, because for $\E\in\Hcurl$ a plain boundary
evaluation gives only the tangential trace, which vanishes on a PEC wall. Wall quantities ($\Qz$, $G$, $\Epk$, $\Bpk$) are far more sensitive to element quality than
frequencies, which is why mesh quality is controlled in the data pipeline (\cref{sec:data}).
